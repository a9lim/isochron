# Open work

The people's three batches, then the planet's open work in rough order. Earth's own errors are listed in `earth.md`.

## People: three batches

Ratified by a9 on 2026-11-18, from a brainstorm by Claude and Codex (gaslamp thread `isochron-brainstorm-2026-11-18`). Batch 1 is the land and its flows: every human state per cell, and what moves between cells, in three landings (1a, local texture; 1b, flows; both built; 1c, peoples, ratified 2026-11-19). Batch 2 is institutions: who controls those flows. Batch 3 is the game, a third scenario. Each item that moves the people lands with `tools/people/earth-score.mjs` re-run and its oracle in `tests/people.test.mjs`; each landing ends scored and retuned. When the model's arc moves, the social card's run is re-exported (`AGENTS.md`).

The seam between the batches holds by four rules:

1. The people's state lives on their grid, per cell; the only world-level arrays are the industrial economy's core (output, capital, energy, compute) and the endings.
2. Every flow between cells goes through one transport cost, the route graph (1b): food, culture, crops, and later deliveries and goods. Polities add owners to flows that already exist.
3. The world economy reads per-cell inputs (workers from each cell's pyramid) and writes per-cell outputs (goods), so batch 2 splits it by summing over a polity's cells instead of the world's.
4. The endings' draw reads a state, so batch 2 swaps the race in without touching the endings' mechanics.

### Batch 1a: local texture

Built 2026-11-18 (`people.md`, "Food, years, ages and works" and the pause; the night side and its lights in `scene.md`; Fig. 9 the ages). Earth's score held within its draws' spread (~1.16 ± 0.06 over eight against ~1.12). Open from it:

- **Births by age**: births don't read the pyramid, so a baby bust echoes in deaths and workers but not in births a generation on.
- **The pause**: what a paused world does in space, and whether a pause can lapse. Batch 2 makes it something polities agree to.
- **Herders** take the unfarmed land where they'd feed more than foragers (a hard choice, softened only in the drawing).
- **Cost**: the pyramids add ~5 ms to a step under `T_STABLE` with the land full; `places` ~13 ms every `DT_PLACES` years.

### Batch 1b: flows

Built 2026-11-18 (`people.md`: "Food, years, ages and works" for the hungry season, herds, works and salt; "Routes, trade and cities"). Earth's score went from ~1.16 ± 0.06 to ~1.12 ± 0.02 over eight draws (the herds' ~1.12, then routes, trade and cities ~1.14, then the works ~1.12), the Americas from ~24% to ~20%, early takeoff ~250–340 years after industry at ~8–11 billion. Each item, what it left open:

- **Trade on the winds**: built (`people.md`, "Routes, trade and cities"; `src/engine/routes.js`, `tests/routes.test.mjs`): the route graph on the people's parent grid, sail on the planet's six winds and the gyres (the volta do mar and the monsoon's reversal come out of them) and steam; markets on the grandparent grid with Dijkstra between them; a gravity kernel pooling the harvests in place of `L_CORR`, carrying culture and crops; the long-distance flows drawn. The graph runs in twelve directions (on six, its paths drew triangles), and the land's roads and canals cheapen the markets' routes by land. Open: the routes' kernel isn't reread as the winds shift between rebuilds (only as the land changes); the routes' paths don't follow the works (only their costs do); goods and energy on the routes wait for batch 2's regional economies.
- **Cities that must be fed**: built (the same section): each farming cell's urban share of its food goes to its cities over its catchment by the local routes, and a fifth to the largest cities over the markets' routes, less what its carriers eat; the share follows the urban share over a generation, and the people follow the food by the spread; cities' crowd diseases until sanitation. The cities' reach has a floor (~100 km by road, the country's ~500), so farming's cities reach history's sizes (~40,000 at ~100 million people, ~800,000 at ~350 million; at the trade's reach alone nothing passed 100,000 before industry). Open: cities lengthen the industrial era by a few decades, from the urban deaths and the lag as the countryside empties into them; the largest cities reach ~70–150 million by takeoff (today's largest metropolitan areas ~30–40 million), the agglomeration's exponent fitted when cities were drawn only; a pre-industrial city is drawn as the share of its ~12,000 km² cell its people need (a million people ~2% of it), so it shows only zoomed in; a city's foodshed isn't drawn as its own, only its far food on the routes.
- **The hungry season**: built (`people.md`, "The hungry season"; `tests/hungry.test.mjs`): each cell's harvest calendar, the store's losses over the longest gap, the crops' rain spread pooled over their months, the granary's room past the season kept against bad years. Open: the lean month (`H.lean`) isn't drawn; granaries as works (below) would set the store's room and losses by place, not by culture alone; foragers' and herders' own lean seasons.
- **Herds as a stock**: built (`people.md`, "Herds"; `tests/herds.test.mjs`): animals domesticated where large game survived a farming origin, carried with crops and by contact; a per-cell herd share, logistic less die-offs (drought, the dzud); herders need it; manure and traction on mixed farms. Open: winter fodder as its own limit (hay with culture), the animals' own climates (the camel's desert, the yak's plateau, the water buffalo's paddies), the horse's reach for batch 2's frontiers, and the herd drawn.
- **Irrigation and salt**: built (`people.md`, "Works", canals and salt; `tests/works.test.mjs`): canals water dry land past the floodplain over a strip beside the river as far as its water meets the deficit, salt rises on them in a dry land and the canals are let go as it does. Open: the canals water ~0.7–1 million km² by industry on Earth, several times the pre-industrial world's (the model's Sahara, Australia and steppe hold rivers and dry warm seasons); southern Mesopotamia still holds ~0.5% of the people (2.4% now), the climate's; industry's own irrigation (dams and pumps, ~3 million km² now) is only in its lift of yields, so the canals run down after industry; drainage as a work (which keeps salt down) and the shift to salt-tolerant crops.
- **A region's luck** (added 2026-11-19, `people.md`): ideas × a smooth random field renewed as culture advances, its mean held at one, so the lead passes among dense regions instead of holding at the densest (every line of equal culture had risen at one spot). Open: it hastens history (industry ~7–15 kyr after the first fields, takeoff ~220–290 years after industry; `Y_B` retuned to 6.5 for ~8 billion at takeoff); culture is still one number (writing here, iron there would want several traits: batch 1c's domains).
- **Works as places' improvements**: built (`people.md`, "Works"): learning in the cities, and the land's terraces, canals, granaries and roads built by the cities' labour sent back to the land that feeds them, each to the room its place has for it, decaying unkept. Drawn on the Culture map as a stipple by the works a km², beside culture's lag and its fronts (lines of equal culture). Open: terraces and canals as patterns on the Settlement map's fields; the land's works stop with industry rather than turning into its capital; batch 2's polities belong on the Culture map too, their borders where deliveries stop.

### Batch 1c: peoples

Ratified by a9 on 2026-11-19, from a discussion with Claude: who people are and what they know, between 1b's flows and 2's institutions. Five landings, each scored on Earth.

1. **The first people.** The run starts with Homo sapiens 150 kyr before the dial runs out, not with the first hominins: the lineages, their trait g, gene flow, splits and merges go (a heritable trait that could differ between modern peoples explains nothing the model needs, and invites the wrong reading). One population per cell. A clock for the whole species lengthens contact, rising e-fold per `T_S` and capped smoothly, so the tip is still the loop's threshold, crossed where density and climate allow; the trait's endpoint becomes a constant in foraging's yield. Large game is wary on the home world-island (the origin's land, its short crossings, as cold as the archaic peoples went) and naive elsewhere. Earth: the tip ~100 kyr in, the rest of the timeline as before. Built 2026-11-19 (`people.md`, "The first people"; `tests/people.test.mjs`): the people start with culture `Z0` (0.5, the Middle Stone Age's: from none, culture's own climb at λ took ~140 kyr to tip whatever the clock), the clock from 1,000 km toward 3,000 (the old trait's contact in the farming era; capped at 4,000 the Americas held ~18%), e-fold per 35 kyr. Earth ~1.03 ± 0.05 over eight draws; the tip at ~100 kyr, farming ~40 kyr later, takeoff ~147 kyr after the first people; on the page the people tip ~12 s in. Open: industry begins in Australia in five draws of eight (a third before); the earthlike seed tips at ~120 kyr and farms ~70 kyr after (its own long forager era, ~80 kyr from the first hominins too).
2. **Two domains, equal.** Culture splits into production (techniques: every yield and its thresholds, cold, boats, trade's and cities' reach, industry's dispersal) and learning (writing, numeracy, science: contact length, copying's pace, research's yield past industry); reach folds into them, coordination waits for batch 2. Readers of both (ocean crossing, the economy) take a softer minimum, exact where the two agree. With the domains' parameters equal they stay equal at every step and the score is landing 1's exactly. Built 2026-11-19 (`people.md`, "Two domains of culture"): Earth's eight draws match landing 1's to the last digit (computing the joint on the fly inside the spread's substeps: read as the step found it, it had drifted from production there and the draws parted).
3. **The domains apart**, in three scored steps: sources (learning's minds weighted by the cities' sizes, production's not; works keep learning; a luck field each), spread (learning by the routes, texts; production by contact and migrants, tacit), couplings (production's gain falls once it runs more than Δ ahead of learning, after Mokyr's epistemic base; industry and AI need both, compared on a copy against the bound alone). Renewables move before the takeoff (`Z_REN` ~10: near parity, still a little dearer than fossil carbon at its start) and so well before space. The Culture map: production's lag as a wash in the accent with solid contours, learning's in verdigris with dashed ones.
4. **Peoples.** Each cell's identity as a few weighted points in a continuous space, kept by way of life (foragers, herders, fishers, farmers, cities: Barth's groups side by side by living differently), so continua and mosaics both hold: points drift, contact pulls them together at a rate falling with their distance, migrants carry them (farming's dispersals carry language families), people shift between them by conformity within a way of life, near points merge. Contact runs over learning's contact length, so the world blends once it spans the world. Feedbacks: learning's copying by how alike two places are, a common language in trade's kernel. Oracles: two parted peoples' distance (E d² = 2Dσ²t), a continuum's distance rising as the log of the distance between places (Séguy's curve), conformity's closed form, herders and farmers kept apart in one cell. Drawn as hairlines where the field tears; the caption counts the peoples (a Hill number by similarity, Leinster and Cobbold).
5. **The record**: docs, the about panel, the card re-exported and repainted.

### Batch 2: institutions

Polities and war return (`people.md`, Out of scope). The batch starts with a design pass, recorded here before any code: a polity's state (which cells deliver to which centre, and its cohesion), how the world economy splits, and conflict as fluxes.

- **Polities made of deliveries** (speculation). A polity is the set of places still delivering grain, labour or tax along the routes to a centre, which returns stores, upkeep or protection. Its territory is a field (each cell's share delivered to each centre), and its border is where deliveries stop, drawn as hairlines. Centres arise where surplus is concentrated and hemmed in (circumscription: Carneiro 1970, from memory). Polities grow along cheap links and split along dear ones when a harvest fails. Cohesion rises on frontiers, with other polities or with herders (the horse's reach), and decays in cores (after Turchin's metaethnic frontiers and Turchin et al. 2013's grid model of the Old World's states, from memory). Polities hold the stores and the works.
- **Conflict as fluxes** (speculation): war and conquest where cohesion and numbers press across a border (cells change hands; people and works are lost), rebellion where a centre takes more than it returns, insurgency where a core's cohesion has fallen. Each consumes people, output and works. There are no units: fronts are gradients.
- **Regional economies after industry.** The world economy splits into polities' economies, each its output, capital, energy and compute summed over its cells, trading goods and energy along the routes. Compute then has a place, and so does the takeoff.
- **Alignment from the race** (speculation, said so on the page). The alignment draw's odds come from the takeoff's state: how many polities race, how fast, and the share of compute spent on alignment. A pause becomes something polities agree to or don't.
- **Claims on the machines** (speculation): the distribution of claims on food, energy and the machines' output, carried through each ending to decide who can return and who is housed.

### Batch 3: the game

- **A third scenario** beside World and People, the only one with controls. The other two keep the dial alone.
- **The frame** (themed after Scott Alexander's "The Goddess of Everything Else", 2015, from memory; none of its text is used). The model is the drive to multiply and consume, and the player never opposes it, only bends it. The score is the effective number of kinds the drive hasn't flattened (a Hill number over peoples, crops, polities, cities, works, worlds and stars). A star the probes reach could seed the next run.
- **Standing rules.** The player composes rules from words that are objects in the world (after Baba Is You's rules as blocks). A rule pairs a trigger on a quantity with an action on a flow; it then runs unattended, and its consequences pile up where they fall. Revisions are scarce. Rules are ideas: each must spread by culture's copying and take root, held by a polity or its works, before it acts. Its forms open with the eras: prophecies, commandments, edicts, constitutions, manifestoes.
- **The race** as its last act: the takeoff's century played among batch 2's polities, each choosing pace against care, with the alignment hazard reading the race. A treaty costs its signers.
- **Needs**: saves (snapshots of the engine's and the people's workers), and, for revisions or rewinds, repeatability (the GPU plate step isn't repeatable, below).

### Open in the model as it stands

- **People on Earth** (`people.md` "On Earth"): Asia's paddies (wet rice beyond the floodplains, terraced and canal-fed, the densest pre-industrial farming); Eurasia's head start in large domesticates, now from the overkill (1b's herds), leaks through the model's mild Chukotka and gives Africa as much as Eurasia; industry-era migration (the Americas and Australia filled by ships, not by diffusion); and, first, the climate's monsoon and tropical rain, which set most of what's left.
- **People, open**: the robot fleet's swings long after the dial (Ap between ~10¹⁷ and ~10²¹ in the same ending, a cycle of decades); capital's weight (K/Y ~2.3 against Earth's 3–4); a runaway's end (carbonates baked into a CO₂ atmosphere); the swarm's pace (mining's own ramp would slow it past the belt); surveys across seeds (how often a world tips, farming's origins, the follow dial's length).

## The planet

- **Eastern North America.** Its coast's sea (a cold Labrador Current inshore of the warm boundary current, so the Gulf of St Lawrence freezes) and Canada's winter (the template's pressure is the latitude's belts and the thermal departure; the continents' winter troughs and the high behind them aren't in it).
- **Clouds.** Deep convection's longwave (a convective cloud from the convective rain alone), the layer cloud reaching into deserts (by the column's humidity and its descent), eastern China's and Southeast Asia's shortwave.
- **The sea's fall on today's Earth** (~20 m in 2 Myr): the basins deepening as the opening's thick oceanic crust is replaced and its floor ages, and the ice moving off West Antarctica onto northern land; the Gulf drains with it (`earth.md`).
- **Eastern Africa's dryness** (the East African paradox): the Indian Ocean's water comes onshore on the template's easterlies all year; Earth's low-level flow runs along the coast (the Somali jet, the highlands' wall) with a dry column over the plateau. Until it's dry the first hominins can't start there: it's closed forest (`earth.md`).
- **The land's evaporation by its cover**: the air takes the ground's evaporation, which reads the bucket's β alone, so a forest evaporates as grass does (the moisture's own form gave trees √β, their deeper roots); the cover into the ground's year (its β, its interception) would let forests carry their rain through a dry season. The deep tropics are wet meanwhile.
- **Central Asia's water** (the Caspian fills to its spill with the Aral's water) and **India's monsoon** (the land's seasonal phase, the template's trough).
- **Ice that keeps orbital time.** The sheets barely answer the orbit: the lowlands at 55–70° where Earth's paced sheets grew are too warm in summer and too dry for ice to start. Levers not pulled: the moisture reaching high latitudes (the template carries little poleward), refreezing in the degree-day scheme (Reeh's, standard), the snow–albedo and tundra feedbacks. Run the ice-age survey on Earth (`tools/iceage-survey.mjs earth`) to tell geography from model. A free-CO₂ survey at ≤ 1 kyr/s would show the dust–iron swing.
- **Foreland basins.** A collision's stacking stands on local isostasy (`surface.md`, the crust's flexure): the grid stacks a column onto one cell a step, and held by the plate cell by cell it left orogens speckled ±2 km. A foreland basin is the downgoing plate bending under the orogen; the grid consumes that plate's crust at the front. One way: lay the stacking's load, smoothed along the front, on the incoming plate's cells ahead of it. The flexure's 2 km cap (`C_MAX`) dates from the stacking too, and can be retested on the loads it bears now (arcs, the plumes' basalt, the ice's till).
- **The mantle's flow** is a potential flow from the piles to the slabs, with no toroidal part (which lateral viscosity and the plates' own motion make), so North America runs 55° off Müller's direction and Africa 32°; the Himalayan front closes at ~1 cm/yr against Earth's ~4. True polar wander would follow from the same density (the graveyard and the piles set the spin axis): a round of its own, since the climate, weather, orbit, people and the globe's pole all take the spin axis as the grid's z.
- **The GPU's plate step isn't repeatable** run to run (a float reduction's order or a race in a pass feeding the elevation), so a seed's page run can't be replayed.
- **Deltas.** First the supply (three quarters of the eroded rock stops in closed depressions on land, and the load is spread over thousands of mouths), then delta plains (sediment building to sea level at a mouth before the shelf), then drawn deltas (distributaries by Galloway's triangle, standard).
- **Speed.** The plate step's latency (a 0.5 Myr step is ~4 GPU round trips, each waiting ~4 ms and followed by the CPU's sea solve, ~4 ms; pipelined with the landscape, ~35 ms a step), then the climate's per-cell physics on the GPU (the dust's lifting and packing, the clouds, the template's pressure: ~150 ms of a warm cycle's ~400 at level 7) and the ground's year (~70). Targets: the dial's top at 10 Myr/s at level 7 (~3.5 now), a climate cycle under ~100 ms.
- **Earth at past dates** (`?seed=earth-66`): snapshots baked like today's, each with its CO₂, sunlight, day and Moon. The PaleoDEMs (Scotese and Wright 2018, CC BY 4.0) are in PALEOMAP's frame, whose plate model has continental blocks only; the leaning is PALEOMAP throughout, ocean plates grown around the continents as genesis does.
- **Not built**: the Storm track, Spin, Tilt and Supercontinent experiments; floodplain lakes and backflow; an Equal Earth view; moving sea ice (built once and dropped: at 220 km its drift didn't move the ice edge).
- **Risks**: the weather–climate seam; the supercontinent cycle may not cycle; show-don't-tell under nine layers; tuning drift.
