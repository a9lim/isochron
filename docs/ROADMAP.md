- **3a. Classes and secular cycles.**
  - *Classes by their claims.* Each way of life's people are commoners and an elite, the elite named by the asset whose return it lives on (a9's axis, orthogonal to the way): the herders' nobility on the herds' increase, the fishers' chiefs on rich, storable fisheries, the farmers' aristocracy on land rent. In urban places (cities, factories, datacenters) the elite is capital's: merchants on trade's margin, then the bourgeoisie on industry's profit, then the owners of compute and robots (a9). Foragers hold no storable surplus, so no elite. Per cell, an elite share a way; no second demography: the share moves by the two classes' own rates (`demography` read at each class's income, so the well-fed elite outbreed commoners before industry, Clark's survival of the richest, and with it their births fall first) and by mobility. Open: whether a colony's capital, invested from its state's pool, pays its profit to the colony's merchants or home (beside N_TAX's drain).
  - *Office* is a state's own elite (a9): a stock in the polity table, its positions what the state's take and tribute fund, spread across its places by where the take is gathered, the court at the capital; its claimants past its positions are its overproduction.
  - *The take.* The commoners' wage is their outside option, the best income the migration potential (3d; until then crowding's) reaches net of the route's cost, lowered by coercion: the elites' arms against the commoners' exit, π₀'s terms. The elite takes the rest of its way's output: Domar's free land, free peasants and idle landlords (1970), and Ricardo's rent rising as people fill the land, the cycle's first half. Famine falls on commoners: whoever holds the granary eats (Sen's entitlements).
  - *Stress.* Elites past their positions fall back over a generation or fight. ψ = the commoners' immiseration (their wage against its trailing mean) × the elites' overproduction × the state's fiscal strain (Goldstone's political stress; Turchin and Nefedov's secular cycles), a product of logistics. It speeds the core's loss of cohesion, D_ASA (1 + K_PSI ψ), and internal war kills and breaks works where no border moves and culls the elites, which resets the cycle. Where the dial resolves the year a bad harvest is a revolt where it falls, and a continent's run of them a general crisis (Parker's seventeenth century). The economy reads the classes only in 3f; here, the stress and famine's split.

- **3c. Capacity and tribute: war makes states, the steppe, vassals.**
  - *Fiscal capacity* κ, a state's: how much of the power it holds over a place it rules directly; its offices are office's positions. War pressure on its borders (the rivalry's parity) grows it toward a ceiling that rises with learning, and peace lets it decay (Tilly; Besley and Persson); the take it allows raises immiseration.
  - *Tribute* (a9: one flow): power beyond what a state administers becomes a flow of surplus to its capital, over the routes, from whoever does administer the place, by its power over the payer's core against the payer's own. A low-κ state is ringed by tributaries whose land elites keep their rent and pay up (vassals); war raises κ and rent becomes tax (centralization).
  - *The steppe.* Founding needs a fed city now, so a herders' steppe never has a state of its own. Herders found states where their arms gather beside settled wealth in reach, their capital a camp at the herders' centre of arms; herders deliver wholly to a herders' state, at DL_HERD to a settled one. With arms and no administration of farmers, a steppe state takes tribute or raids (goods and lives move, allegiance doesn't), growing with the agrarian state it milks (Barfield's outer frontier; Turchin's mirror empires). When stress breaks the settled frontier it conquers, its capital moving into the largest city taken: a dynasty with the steppe's cohesion to spend (Ibn Khaldun's cycle with its actors). Gunpowder closes the steppe, M_HERD fading past a production threshold (Perdue). Deserts' herders by the same rules; sea raiders perhaps (speculation).
  - *Conquest elites* (a9). A state's identity becomes its office's own point on the torus (seam rule 1 lists identity in the polity table): set at founding from its capital's people's, kept when its capital moves, and drawn toward its capital's people as they are drawn toward it (R_STATE). Each reader of a state's identity reads it in place of its capital's people's (`capSlot` and `ids.sim(c, q.cap)`: kin's welcome, the schooled's resistance to strangers, the state's and the media's pull), so a steppe dynasty in a conquered city rules as strangers until it assimilates; now it becomes the city's people at once. A conquest replacing a province's aristocracy (the Normans) needs elite points a way and a cell, for later.
