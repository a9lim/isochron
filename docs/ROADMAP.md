## Next: the industrial states (batch 2b)

Built on the `batch-2b` branch, held unmerged with 2a (docs/MODEL.md has the forms): overseas empires (power projected by steam, the troops raised overseas armed at the state's wealth against the world's, settlers carrying their state, nationalism's cut on strangers' rule, a state's cohesion its core's) and regional economies (one market for energy and materials, each place's output by its own productivity, capitals' share and fossil rent, saving invested within each state). - **The industrial world consolidates** to ~8 effective states at takeoff (2a's ~11; history ~14): industry's arms favour the large and rich, and decolonization's new states are few.
- Goods' trade has no term of its own (borders cut food's trade and ideas' copying, which carry it); ores set where industry begins but pay no rent.

## Parked: the race (batch 2c)

Its detail is the race's own, so it may be built with the game (batch 3), whose last act is the race (a9). Its seam is ready: the endings' draw reads a state (seam rule 4).

1. **The race.** Each polity has its compute, research minds and algorithmic frontier: grown by research, bounded by its learning, leaking across borders at a rate that closure lowers, with export controls on chips. The endings' draw reads the leader's margin in years, the contenders within a year, and the share they spend on safety and coordination (likeness on the torus, trade's ties). Alignment's odds rise with the margin and with safety (Armstrong, Bostrom and Shulman's race to the precipice); a pause's odds rise with coordination. The draw's mechanics stay (seam rule 4).
2. **Claims on the machines** (speculation, said so on the page): the aligned endings ask "aligned to whom", and claims by polity decide who returns and who is housed.

Left out: industrial war's nuclear exchanges (soot into the climate's forcing), a catastrophe outside the AI endings, to come after the race.

## Then: the game (batch 3)

- A third scenario beside World and People, the only one with controls; the other two keep the dial alone.
- The frame follows Scott Alexander's "The Goddess of Everything Else" (2015; none of its text is used). The model is the drive to multiply and consume, and the player never opposes it, only bends it. The score is the effective number of kinds the drive hasn't flattened: a Hill number over peoples, crops, polities, cities, works, worlds and stars. A star the probes reach could seed the next run.
- Standing rules: the player composes rules from words that are objects in the world (after Baba Is You). A rule pairs a trigger on a quantity with an action on a flow, then runs unattended. Revisions are scarce. Rules are ideas: each spreads by copying and takes root, held by a polity or its works, before it acts. Their forms open with the eras: prophecies, commandments, edicts, constitutions, manifestoes.
- The race as the last act: the takeoff's century played among the polities, each choosing pace against care. A treaty costs its signers.
- Needs saves (snapshots of the engine's and the people's workers) and, for rewinds, a repeatable GPU plate step (below).

Ideas not yet placed: place names laid down in layers; crowd diseases and the meeting of long-parted worlds (Miasma's SEIR); ruins the planet buries and subducts; a census; one cohort's life at the slow dial; an ancestry traced back by the coalescent; a Histomap of polities; the Silurian epilogue (the planet run on after the ending); a second rise on a planet whose coal is spent; worlds unlike this one (spin, tilt, no Moon, tidal locking).

## Open in the people's model

- **States**: Turchin's secular cycles (hunger lowering cohesion) aren't in; the largest city at takeoff is ~170 million, since capitals take a state's whole share; the Americas stay stateless longer than history's.
- **Earth**: Asia's paddies (wet rice beyond the floodplains) are missing; the model's Chukotka keeps half a steppe's pasture, so Eurasia's animals reach Alaska early and Africa gains as much from its animals as Eurasia; the Americas and Australia fill by diffusion, not by industry-era ships (the ships' flux runs down crowding, and without epidemics the Americas are never emptied to draw it); India holds ~4.5% of the people and China ~6% (India's monsoon is now near Earth's rain, so the shortfall is the people's: Asia's paddies, above); the model's Sahel, three times Earth's rain, holds ~10% (Earth's ~4%) and can spark industry (two draws of eight).
- **Food and ages**: births don't read the pyramid, so a baby bust doesn't echo in births a generation on; winter fodder, the animals' own climates and the horse's reach; the lean month isn't drawn; granaries as works should set the store by place.
- **Routes and cities**: the routes' kernel isn't reread as the winds shift between rebuilds; the routes' paths don't follow the works; the largest cities reach ~70–150 million by takeoff against today's ~30–40.
- **Works**: the land's works stop with industry instead of becoming its capital; canals water several times the pre-industrial world's land; drainage and salt-tolerant crops.

## Open in the planet

- **The climate's next round: moist static energy.** The largest error on land is the dry subtropics' summers, 4–7 K cold (the Sahara's July 25 °C against 32.5, the Middle East's 24 against 30, the US Southwest's 15 against 21), with deserts' clear sky ~35 W/m² darker than CERES's to keep them from being colder still (`ALB_LAND`'s note). The energy balance diffuses temperature, so it imports heat only where land is colder than its neighbours; Earth's atmosphere moves moist static energy, c_p T + L q, down its gradient, so dry air draws heat in, and the Sahara is a radiative sink of ~23 W/m² and the hottest land (standard in zonal models: Frierson and others' moist EBMs, from memory; for deserts in 2D, synthesis). Tried on a scratch copy (2026-11-23): diffusing h with the moist part implicit on each edge, D(1 + μₑ)∇T, μ = (L/c_p) RH ∂q_s/∂T, the rest lagged, brought the deserts' summers within ~1–2 K (the Middle East's July 30.3, the Sahara's 32.2), but as a swap it scored worse (the tuner's objective 45.5 against 40.7 after ten rounds over ten constants): every continent imported heat and warmed (+2.6 K), because a land humidity of 0.2 + 0.6β reads even humid land drier than the sea; the template rained on the heat (the Sahara ~1,000 mm) until the ITCZ was placed at the sector's largest h, and then India's monsoon failed (its pre-monsoon soil dry, its h low); and it didn't settle within 16 cycles, the soil's wetness, the humidity and the heat feeding back between cycles. Needed: a land humidity that knows advected moisture (neither β nor the moisture's column humidity, which the rain scheme holds at ~0.4 over land and ~0.6 at sea, serves), the ITCZ and the rain's thresholds on h (heat lows stay on T: the Saharan heat low is real), the settling, the GPU's per-level edge weights rewritten each step (the lagged part can ride `XF`), a guard for hot climates (q_s past ~60 °C), and a full retune; the people's eras checked after. A go/no-go: a CPU prototype that settles and gets India and the Sahara both.
- **Eastern North America**: its year ~4.5 K warm (its January ~6.6) and its rain ~0.8 of Earth's; Canada's January ~8 K warm. The gyres' boundary current runs up the coast with no cold Labrador Current inshore, the template's zonal pressure has no continental winter trough, and the air's heat crosses the Rockies (`airheat.js` knows no terrain: speculation that it matters).
- **The Nordic Seas and the subpolar oceans**: the Nordic Seas ~5 K cold (1.3 °C against 6.3) and the Barents' sea ice ~70% of the year against ~30; the subpolar Atlantic and Pacific at 40–55° N 2–4 K warm. The overturning's sinking is spread over every deep basin past 55°, and `SURF` hides the subpolar Atlantic's warm water. The storms' gusts on the stress (GUST) alone warmed the Nordic Seas to ~4 °C and their ice to ~9%, but the subpolar Atlantic with them, and the tune traded it back. Next: the sinking by each basin's freshwater (its E − P and runoff, a salinity's stand-in), and the subpolar gyres' cold western currents.
- **Clouds**: deep convection's high cloud is about half Earth's (it saturates at `HI_MAX` by ~2 mm/day, so the Congo's and the Amazon's OLR run ~40 W/m² high and their clouds' shortwave half Earth's): high cloud from the convective rain, Slingo's way, after the next round; deserts are too cloudy; eastern China's shortwave is short; the stratocumulus decks are thin; the polar clear sky's OLR is high.
- **Rain**: the deep tropics are wet (Southeast Asia nearly twice Earth's), the Sahel three times Earth's (the equatorial Atlantic's upwelling sends West Africa's rain band north, onto a Sahara the energy balance holds too cold: the next round's), the eastern Pacific's cold tongue still rains ~3.6 m a year (Earth's ~1, from memory), land's rain ~30% over UDel's, and eastern Africa twice Earth's (the template's easterlies bring the Indian Ocean's water onshore where Earth's low-level flow runs along the coast). Forests evaporate as grass does, since the ground's evaporation reads the bucket's β alone; the cover's β and interception in the ground's year would fix that. The Caspian fills to its spill.
- **Ice that keeps orbital time**: the sheets barely answer the orbit, because the lowlands at 55–70° are too warm and dry in summer. Levers: moisture reaching high latitudes, refreezing in the degree-day scheme, the snow–albedo and tundra feedbacks. Run `tools/iceage-survey.mjs earth` to tell geography from model.
- **Plates on Earth**: Indo-Australia slows from 7 to ~4 cm/yr within 10 Myr, so the Himalayan front closes at ~1 cm/yr against Earth's ~4; Africa runs 32° and North America 55° off Müller's directions, because the mantle's flow is a potential flow with no toroidal part. True polar wander would follow from the same density, but every layer takes the spin axis as the grid's z.
- **The sea falls on today's Earth**, ~20 m in 2 Myr, as the opening's thick oceanic crust is replaced and West Antarctica's ice moves onto northern land; the Gulf drains with it.
- **Foreland basins**: a collision's stacking stands on local isostasy, so there is no foreland basin. One way: lay the stacking's load, smoothed along the front, on the incoming plate's cells ahead of it. The flexure's 2 km cap can then be retested.
- **Deltas**: three quarters of the eroded rock stops in closed depressions on land, and the load spreads over thousands of mouths; then delta plains, then drawn deltas.
- **A NaN seen once** (a9, on the page, early in the industrial era): the sea's carbon failed its implicit step at every halving, as a non-finite input or pulse makes it. Likely the people's (synthesis): until the trade's arrays followed the markets' count, a routes' rebuild that added markets (an ice sheet gone, the sea fallen) made their costs and pooled years NaN, and with them capacities and famines (a9's `iabied` and `claude` worlds thinning near industry, the K that went NaN), and what the people write back. - **Repeatability**: the GPU plate step isn't repeatable run to run (a reduction's order or a race), so a seed's page run can't be replayed.
- **The opening's wait**: on the page (M5 Max, level 7) ~10 s on today's Earth and ~16 s on a seeded planet before the clock runs and the first people come: the plates' spin-up and the layers' build ~3 s (seeded: 80 Myr at level 6, 2.3 s), the climate's 16 cycles ~0.5 s each (both stop at SETTLE_MAX: at level 7 two calm cycles never come, the mean drifting 0.01–0.05 K a cycle), a seeded planet's ice grown off the clock ~4 s; headless on the CPU a level-7 cycle is 5–17 s.
