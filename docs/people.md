# People

The model is `src/engine/people.js` (`PEOPLE`; its header holds the forms), run on its own thread in the People scenario (`src/engine/people-worker.js`) and linked to the planet by `src/engine/peoplelink.js`. Headless: `tools/people-run.mjs` on a cached opening (`tools/people/`; `ENGINE=7` builds it the way the page's desktop engine sees it), `LIVE=1` beside a live planet (`tools/people/couple.mjs`); `tools/people/earth-score.mjs` scores today's Earth (below). Fig. 8's hyperbola: `src/engine/peoplefit.js`; Fig. 2's economy: `src/render/economy.js`. Tests: `tests/people.test.mjs`, `tests/economy.test.mjs`.

## Design

- **One loop, run four times** (Kremer's, standard, from memory): minds make ideas, ideas raise capacity, capacity makes minds. Each era changes what the minds are and what they draw on, and each transition is a bifurcation of the loop, not a scripted age.

  | Era | Minds | Capacity from | Loop |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Foragers | people | foraged NPP | closes only past a threshold in the connected population |
  | Farmers | people | cropland | Malthusian, hyperbolic |
  | Industry | people | fossil energy | broken by the demographic transition |
  | Compute | compute | electricity | closed again, hyperbolic, capped by energy and heat |

- **Few new sinks**: the people write through the planet's own inputs: carbon pulses, tillage in the erosion field, land use on the cover, waste heat and sulfate as forcings, the boiled sea.
- **Factors** split where a split brings its own dynamics: soil (regolith), forest (cover), fossil carbon and ores (finite, their return falling with draw-down), ideas (non-rival, forgotten), fixed capital, energy capital, compute. **AI is capital that does labour** (synthesis): a task CES, so tasks AI can't do bottleneck. **Soft forms over hard caps**: sums over uneven units harmonic, thresholds logistic hazards, floors exponential approaches.
- **Out of scope**: agents, named cultures, tech trees; polities and war until the roadmap's second batch (`roadmap.md`). What a superintelligence wants isn't modelled; the model says what each ending costs, not why it happens.

## Lineages, spread and farming

- **The first hominins** start at the heart of the broadest tree–grass mosaic (H.mosaic, b × 4·trees·grass, summed within `R_ORIGIN` 1,000 km) on the landmass that can hold the most of them (`seed`; speculation): an ape radiation lives across an ecotone, and the best single cell is a near tie among tropical coasts.
- **Lineages are kinds of hominin** (soft species: a heritable trait; splits after 200 kyr apart). Two in contact (sharing `MERGE` 10%) for `T_MERGE` merge, faster with culture's pace; a merge keeps each cell's higher culture and the higher trait, ancestry weighted by numbers; gene flow carries each side's advantage across while in contact. Isolation grows with the time their lines have been apart (`divergence`, τ), as incompatibilities snowball (∝ τ², Orr, from memory): gene flow and the merge's clock both go as e^{−(τ/`T_ISO`)²} (300 kyr; speculation), so lineages 100–200 kyr apart merge nearly as freely and ones 500 kyr or more apart hardly at all. Lineages long apart meet as near strangers, and the abler replaces the other where they share land (competitive exclusion), keeping a little of its ancestry, as Neanderthals' few percent in us.
- **Minds' bodies** (synthesis): minds pool across land peopled at `MIND_REL` (0.3) of the inhabited world's mean density. A forager world is one body; once farming crowds the cores, the sparse margins part it, so a continent reached only through the Arctic or across a strait keeps Kremer's lag (the Americas, Australia). Culture is also copied from any neighbour who knows more, whatever their lineage, at a rate weighted by each side's density (`RHO_COPY`). Copying only one's own lineage had kept a lineage that held East Asia since before the tip ~1 unit of culture behind into the farming era.
- **Crossing the sea** (synthesis, `tools/people/settle.mjs`): sea edges are every crossing of up to `HOPS` (6) sea cells, plus each landmass group's `K_LONG` (4) nearest groups to `LONG_HOPS` (40), then whatever is still apart. Then come ocean routes (`graph`): each landmass (by land alone) to every other within `OCEAN_HOPS` (80) cells, with up to `M_ROUTE` (6) landings at least `D_ROUTE` (1,500 km) from each other and from the crossings already made. Without them, Earth's Americas were joined to Eurasia only at Bering, and nothing crossed the Atlantic. An empty shore is settled by a voyage (`voyages`): `FOUNDERS` (50) at a rate `S_SEA` × crowding × e^{z/Z_V} × e^{−km/reach}. The reach is `L_SEA0` + `L_SEA` (1 − e^{−z/Z_SEA}) + `L_OCEAN` sig((z − `Z_OCEAN`)/`W_OCEAN`): rafts and boats along coasts and between islands, a few hundred km by the Upper Palaeolithic (Sahul, Bering), then deep-sea navigation in a few centuries about `Z_OCEAN` (6.2). Farming and industry spread across a crossing by e^{−km/reach} too. Cold opens faster with culture (`K_COLD`).
- **Crops' land** (`rates`; synthesis):
  - **Floodplains:** each river has a floodplain `W_FP` √Q km wide along its course through the cell (hydraulic geometry's width ∝ √Q, standard). There the crops are watered and the floods renew the soil (`FP_RENEW` of what the uplands lack). Only the growing season's warmth limits them, and they are cropped as often as it allows (GDD/`GDD_CROP`, at most `CROPS_MAX`: wet rice's two and three a year).
  - **Uplands:** rain-fed (Miami's rain limb). A crop's warmth is 1 − e^{−(GDD − GDD0)/GDD_E}: none in a boreal summer, two thirds at the prairies'.
  - **Fertility** is 1/(1 + leaching/(`L_FERT` (1 + erosion/`E_FERT`))), with leaching the drainage × e^{(T − 15)/T_LEACH}. Old, leached tropical soils lose their nutrients; young and eroding ones keep them (Walker and Syers, Porder and Vitousek, from memory). The uplands are cropped only in that share and lie fallow the rest (swidden), until industry's fertilizer makes it up.
  - **The arable share** is the floodplain plus the uplands a plough can work, at most `ARABLE_MAX`:
    - unfrozen;
    - flat enough (`S_ARABLE`);
    - with a growing period of `LGP_DRY`–`LGP_WET` months (FAO's length of growing period: warm months whose rain meets half the potential evaporation, standard), so a monsoon's wet months count where the year's mean aridity wouldn't;
    - not the cold grasslands' sod (`SOD`), which only industry's steel ploughs turn.
  - **Disease:** the warm, wet tropics' diseases thin farmers (`PATH`, malaria with clearing and standing water) until medicine.
  - **Fields** keep to each cell's deep, flat bottoms (`fieldSoil`; on stony, thin ground they still bear `Y_STONY`). Tillage strips the uplands' soil, `SDR` of it leaving the cell. Fields go back to the wild over `T_WILD` where no one works them, and nothing grows past the cover's heat limit.
- **Farming's origins and crops** (synthesis):
  - **Origins:** the hazard is `H_FARM` × domesticates × knowledge (z about `Z_FARM`) × settledness × steady harvests, × (1 − s)², where s is the suitability of the crops farmed here or next door. One climate's crops don't pre-empt another's own (rice and millet beside wheat), so a world has ten to thirty origins. Domesticates are the zones' (`DOMESTIC`: the seasonally dry climates' large-seeded annuals, next to none in the perhumid tropics) plus an oasis's: a river's floodplain in a dry land, `D_OASIS` × its floodplain share × aridity (after Childe, from memory). With deserts at none, the Nile's and the Tigris's floodplains could never begin farming, and Earth's first fields were by Lake Victoria and on the Congo's rim.
  - **Packages:** each carries its origin's climate, and its suitability elsewhere is a Gaussian in the coldest month, the warmest, log water (a floodplain's river counts) and the summer's share of rain (`CLIM_S`, after BIOCLIM's envelopes). Industry's breeding widens it (`BREED`).
  - **Spread:** a better-suited crop farmed next door is taken up at a rate by contact, the neighbour's fields and the gain (`C_PKG`). Once a crop is taken up, farmers breed it toward the place: s^a, with a falling e-fold per `T_ADAPT` (2 kyr) to `A_ADAPT`.
  - **Farmed share:** it rises toward the arable land × s/(s + `S_HALF`) × (1 + `S_HALF`), clearing empty land next to farmers too: the Nile's valley had no foragers to take crops up.
- **Demic spread** (synthesis):
  - **Migrants judge land by what they'd farm.** A farming people's migrants (their best culture past `Z_FARM`) see unfarmed land as fields: `MIGR_F` of its arable land, crops suited `MIGR_S`. That sets only the spread's weights, after Ammerman and Cavalli-Sforza's wave of advance (from memory). With the cell's own fields alone, a continent of foragers had looked full to them, and farmers never came.
  - **Industry's mobility:** railways and steamships add `D_IND` (10⁴ km²/yr, a logistic about `Z_IND`) to the dispersal, and the spread takes up to `NSUB_IND` (48) substeps a step once industry runs. Capped at `NSUB_MAX` (4), the economy's 10-year steps held migration to a forager's few km a year, and the Americas stayed empty past the takeoff on the page's planet.
  - **Industry's crops:** past industry, crops travel at its pace. The uptake of a better crop, and the fields' spread from next door, × 1 + (min(culture's pace, `IND_PACE`) − 1) sig((z − `Z_IND`)/0.5); the clearing at most `CLEAR_PACE` (5) times faster. At the Neolithic's ~1 km/yr, the settlers' crops had reached a few hundred km inland by the takeoff.

## Food, years, ages and works

- **The sea's people** (synthesis). A coastal cell's sea feeds foragers by its coast's export production (`sea`, the most of its sea neighbours' in `life.export`): `B_SEA` × x/(1 + x), x the production over `PS_REF` (13 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹, the coasts' median). It replaced a flat coastal bonus of half the land's foraging, and `B_SEA` (0.019) keeps the coasts' mean where that bonus was, so the sea's food moves toward upwelling coasts and river mouths: Earth's richest are the Canary Current's desert coast, the Yangtze's and the Congo's mouths. Fields don't displace it, it doesn't follow the land's rain or warmth (it steadies the foragers' harvest), and its fish are a stock (below). The first hominins' mosaic still reads the land's foraging with the old coastal factor, so their origin is unchanged.
- **Herders** (synthesis). Pastoralism on the land fields don't take, once a farming package exists somewhere and culture passes `Z_HERD` (3.25, a quarter past farming's): `D_HERD` (0.8 a km² at `NPP_REF`'s grass) × a soft minimum of the best summer pasture and the best winter one within a season's move (the cell and its neighbours), on the cell's own grass. Summer's is the grass's production; winter's what of it stands to be grazed, none where the coldest month is under −`T_SNOW` (8 °C) and the winter's four coldest months bring more than `P_SNOW` (60 mm) of snow. Productivity rises with culture to `CAP_H` (2). Herders take the unfarmed land where they'd feed more than foragers.
- **Large game** (synthesis; the overkill's timing speculation). A stock a cell, its share of what it would be without people: logistic at `R_MEGA` (3%/yr) less a take of `H_MEGA` × wariness × the foragers' density against `B0` (at most 3) × their foraging's culture (P_F/CAP_F), solved exactly; under `M_EXT` it's gone for good. Game that first met people before any lineage tipped is wary (`NAIVE_CO`, 0.15 of a naive fauna's take), so it holds where hominins began and is hunted out where people arrived after the tip. Its meat is `MEAT` (30%) of foraging at the full stock, calibrated at `MG_REF` (0.6). Live, its share scales the cover's browsing and adds to its fire (`FUEL_MEGA`: the grass the herds would have eaten burns; `vegetation.js`, `disturbance`).
- **Fish** (synthesis). A coastal cell's fish are a stock alike at `R_FISH` (0.3/yr): taken at `H_FISH` where people live on the coast (by their crowding), and by industry's fleets at `H_FISH_IND` (0.45, past the regrowth) × its share, easing with culture as tillage does. Before industry they hold near 0.9; industry's fleets collapse them, and they come back as the take eases.
- **The planet's years in the harvest** (synthesis). The harvest's year-to-year anomaly is a = α R + β T̄′ over a year's log-rain anomaly and its mean temperature anomaly (`harvestCoef`): α = `E_RAIN` (× `IRR` by a river or lake), β the yield's warmth sensitivity d ln Y/dT, the crops' through their degree-days where warmth rather than rain limits them (at most `B_TMAX`, 0.5/K), the foragers' through Miami's temperature limb. Farmers bear it whole, foragers half less the sea's share. The closure (`harvestSigma`) takes the spreads the year keeps (`sigP`, and `sigTy`, a month's T′ spread × √ of its memory), correlated as the year makes them (a warm year a dry one over land). Where the dial resolves the year, the engine's `HarvestYears` (`peoplelink.js`, a hook on each of the year's steps) gathers each whole year's log-rain unit and T̄′ on the year's grid and sends their moments with `air`; the people pool them (`addYears`), and each step takes as many years as it lasts, the realized years' mean and spread standing in for that share of the closure (`demography`'s `real`). A drought decade or a volcanic winter is then a famine where it fell; outrun, the closure stands. The farming origin's steady-harvest term reads the farmers' full spread.
- **Age cohorts** (`src/engine/cohorts.js`; Siler's form and Lotka's stable population, standard; the riding synthesis). Each cell carries one-year cohorts to 100 that ride the total the demography solves: the total keeps its own law, the pyramid its shape. Deaths past aging, d₀ (hunger, disease and density, famine), fall on all ages as μ₀ (1 + `J` e^{−a/`T_JUV`}), μ₀ set so the cell's crude rate is d₀ (children most: ~40% dead by five in the pre-industrial reference); senescence is Gompertz's, `S0` (1.2%/yr) at 60 doubling every ~8 years. A cell's aging, which replaces the `AGE` closure in its crude deaths, is its senescent rate less the pre-industrial reference's (births `B_PRE`, no growth), scaled so a rich population at replacement adds `AGE` (`agingScale`), never below none. Its workers' share is `LFP` × its working-age share (15–64) over `W_REF` (0.65), which the economy's labour sums. All cells share one clock (`ageClock`), and cohorts move on whole as it passes each year (a boxcar), so steps of a month don't smear them; the step's integrals of the children's excess and senescence are tables (`stepTables`). A step of `T_STABLE` (60 yr) or more leaves only its rates' stable population; those steps come before industry, so the pyramids are set from a table at the first shorter step. Migrants move by a Rogers–Castro-like schedule (`MIG`: children with parents, a peak at 24), so frontiers are young and the places they leave age. Births don't read the pyramid (a baby bust echoes in deaths and workers, not in births).
- **Cities' minds** (synthesis on urban scaling, innovation ∝ N^β: Bettencourt and others 2007, from memory). A city dweller's research × (its city's size/`S_CITY_REF`)^{`BETA_CITY` − 1} (2 × 10⁴, 1.15), the city its cell's urban share lives in as `places` shares it out (`cityOf`); normalized over everyone, so the world's minds, and Kremer's calibration, are kept, and the loop runs faster where cities are large. `places` runs in the step every `DT_PLACES` (25) years once anyone farms.
- **Works** (speculation). A stock a cell in person-years: `S_WORK` (a quarter) of its urban share's labour a year, decaying over `T_WORK` (500 yr). Forgetting falls as λ/(1 + w/`W_WORK`), w the works a person (10 person-years halves it), so a people with works keeps its culture through a crash. Their labour feeds no one: the cell's capacity × (1 − `S_WORK` u) before industry.

## The industrial economy

- Industry starts where a dense farming people (`RHO_IND`, 10 a km²: labour and markets) has coal and ore within reach (`oreMap`). By the farmed share alone, it had begun on the Arctic's thin fields. Coal lies in the lowland basins (low and flat), not under today's forests: coal is the peat of swamps long gone. It spreads by contact at up to `IND_PACE` (60) times its own pace, reaching the world in two centuries, not one. Research intensity is capped at a subsistence-year's (`S_R_MAX`) and yields less past industry's culture (`BETA_R`); productivity ×e^{0.85} a unit of culture (`THETA_I`); energy intensity falls e-fold per `Z_EFF`; crops are bred beyond their climate (`BREED`).
- **Compute** draws `W_C` (12 W) a subsistence-year of hardware; ordinary computing takes `S_IT` (1.2%) of investment, so compute draws ~40–60 GW around early takeoff. AI's capability follows effective compute (~10×/yr at early takeoff, as measured); `THETA_C`, `THETA_AI`.
- **Robots lag AI**: the working fleet grows at most e-fold per `T_ROB` (1.44 yr) from `B_SEED` (10⁶ bodies) as far as paid for; investment in robots is its own smooth decision (a unit's product against its power, wear and `R_MIN`).
- **Building power takes physical work** (synthesis): the power built a year is at most `S_BUILD` of the physical work (people's and the robots') × `B_POW` (10 kW a worker-year), rising with culture to `B_UP`; space solar by the robots at `B_SPACE` (speculation).
- **Solar farms** compete with fields: their ceiling is the land they may stand on (`solarRoom`, `SOL_ROOM` 0.9 of reached living land and the land the people's cells don't hold) at `D_SOL` (10 W/m²) × the cell's sun; placed sunniest and open land first (`placeSolar`, fields valued over open land by `SOL_FARM`); steam dims them.
- **Materials** come from the crust's ores, their grade falling as they're drawn (Lasky's rule, then Skinner's barrier), and, once industry is off-world, from asteroids wherever those cost less (`E_SPACE`; a smooth share `aA`): the crust draws down only by the rest. Off-world the mass is counted by what's built (Off-world, below). Food is bought with energy for those the land can't feed.
- **Coal's sulfate** (synthesis): its global mean is `aerC` (scrubbed with culture), lifted where industry's workers are and carried on the dust's balance (one bin, level 5; `PeopleLink.sulfateSteps`), so the forcing is local and downwind; a preserving AI's sprayed sulfate is global.
- At early takeoff on the cached openings: ~9–9.7 B people, ~30 TW, ~650–700 Pg C burned (CO₂ ~560 ppm), ~255–310 years after industry; the dial runs out ~4–5 years after early takeoff.

## On Earth

`tools/people/earth-score.mjs` runs the people on today's Earth from the first hominins to early takeoff, over several draws of their chances in parallel. It scores where the people are against where people live now: the regions' shares of ~2023 (rough, from memory), Σ share × |ln(model/observed)|.

- **Score:** ~1.11 on the cached level-6 opening and ~1.15 on the page's level-7 engine (`ENGINE=7`); the model before this was ~2.5, with 62% of the people in Africa and industry always beginning there.
- **Timeline:**
  - 20–35 origins of farming, the first ~10–25 kyr before industry, mostly in seasonally dry lands: the Sahel, Mesopotamia, India, South China, the steppe's edge, sometimes the Nile's floodplain or East Africa's savanna. (With the perhumid tropics' domesticates at a tenth of the dry lands' and the steady-harvest term at `SIG_REF` 0.15, the wet tropics' steady rain had won, and the first fields were in the Congo.)
  - Industry in a dense farming people's basins: Manchuria, the Caspian's shores, the Ganges, southern Siberia, sometimes Arabia or Australia.
  - Early takeoff ~280–310 years after industry, at ~9.3 billion people.
- **Continents:** Asia ~28–36% (now 58%), Africa ~27–29% (20%), the Americas ~14–23% (13%), Europe ~6% (9%), Oceania ~7% (1%).

What's left mostly follows the climate's errors (`earth.md`):

- India is ~60% of its rain and East Asia's monsoon is weak, so India holds ~3% of the people (24% now) and China ~7% (14%).
- The Sahel, East Africa, Mongolia and Australia are far too wet, so they farm as if fertile.

Two gaps belong to the people's model itself:

- **Asia's paddies:** wet-rice farming beyond the floodplains is missing.
- **Eurasia's head start in domesticates:** large animals for the plough, manure and traction are missing, so the Americas, reached through Bering on the cached map, farm like Eurasia. On the page's live planet the ice ages can close Beringia while culture is low, so South America stays a thin, lagging foragers' land until industry's migrants come.

## The endings

A chance draw on two axes (`P_PAUSE` a third paused, the rest aligned or not at `P_ALIGN`; `P_PRESERVE` even; `ENDING` forces): **1a** misaligned and Earth sacrificed (the oceans boil), **1b** misaligned and Earth preserved (people disempowered), **2a** aligned and Earth sacrificed for the stars, **2b** aligned and preserved, **3a** paused with the climate left to the economy, **3b** paused with the climate held. Levers follow AI's share of the work over `T_SA` (2 yr), control over `T_DIS` (30 yr). Preserving: CO₂ drawn to 280 ppm over `T_CDR`, sulfate holding the forcing at none, fossil carbon left, farms rewilded. Sacrificing: Earth's heat budget opened; past the runaway's limit the sea boils (`climate.md`). The land a boiled sea leaves takes solar farms and machines but no people (`People.X`).

**A pause** (speculation): frontier capability is held where the draw finds it (`X_CAP`, log₁₀ effective compute, in `capability`); compute and culture go on, so AI's labour grows within the tasks it already does, the task CES bounds output by the rest, and the hyperbola breaks: the dial doesn't run out, and the run goes on into the long aftermath at the follow dial. Neither the aligned nor the misaligned branches act (no disempowerment, no swarm, no off-world people), and the heat budget is people's. In 3a the fossil carbon burns as its cost allows (earthlike's cached opening: ~2,500 Pg C by the peak). In 3b fossil carbon is phased out e-fold per `T_PHASE` (30 yr) from the draw, and people's own capture draws the excess CO₂ down over `T_CDR_P` (300 yr), ramping in alike. Open: what a paused world does in space, and whether a pause can lapse.

## Off-world

Speculation throughout, the parts synthesis; `tools/people-run.mjs` prints each ending's end state.

- **Launch is no bind** (a9, on Starship's progress): robots go where they're wanted, so space solar's building draws on the whole fleet (`B_SPACE`), and off-world industry matures with culture (`Z_SPACE`), which saturates within a year of the dial.
- **Where the robots work** (`earthBots`): on Earth, the physical work its own economy needs, the jobs its workers' physical tasks filled (Φ Lh, ~1.4 B worker-equivalents for 5.6 B people), or Earth's share of the energy where that's more (a sacrificed Earth's industry), together as a soft maximum; the rest are off-world. By the energy's share alone, a preserved Earth on 51 TW of fusion beside a swarm's 10²⁵ W had kept ~10⁶ robots. The split is where they're counted and drawn.
- **The swarm is as large as its material.** Collectors at 1 AU of `SIGMA_SW` (1 kg/m², with their radiators) take ~2.8 × 10²³ kg for the whole Sun. The inner planet (`M_MERC`: Mercury, 3.3 × 10²³ kg, on today's Earth) is taken apart for the swarm (a9: a Kardashev-2 civilization is short of energy before room; after Armstrong and Sandberg); the belt (`M_BELT`, the main belt's 2.4 × 10²¹ kg) is for habitats, but for what the swarm needs past the inner planet. Both, and the worlds, are the planet's own solar system's (below). The Moon (the tides), the settled worlds and Venus are kept. The ceiling (`swarmRoom`) is the Sun's output softly against what that mass builds (~90%). What's mined of each is the most that has ever stood (`massBooks`); its flow is Fig. 2's arrow from space, named for the source drawn on most.
- **People off-planet** (aligned endings) have room (`offRoom`) for what off-world power keeps (`W_HAB`, 10 kW a person), softly against the worlds' surfaces opened (the system's worlds, at `D_SURF` 4,000 a km²: on today's Earth `WORLDS` in `src/engine/worlds.js`, L4 and L5, the Moon, Mars, Ceres, Callisto, Ganymede, Europa, Titan, each opening at a step in log₁₀ of the most robots yet at work off-world) and habitats about the Sun built from the belt (`M_HAB`, 10⁶ kg a person: ~2.4 × 10¹⁵ people). With people first, an aligned ending's habitats had taken all of Mercury back from the swarm, and AI's minds fell 10⁴-fold.
- **Earth past scarcity** (aligned; a9; speculation): its people grow too, where they live, toward an ecumenopolis (`earthRoom`: `D_ECU` 10,000 a km² of living land × its habitability, ~1.5 × 10¹² on Earth's), and returnees come back as far as that room allows. Its factories are drawn by the power its people eat by (`EfoodE`) and its share of output's, its datacenters by its share of compute's and `W_DC_P` (5 kW) a person; where the uses want more land than there is, datacenters keep theirs and cities and factories share the rest. (Held at the numbers it had when the ending was drawn, Earth stood at ~10 B while Mars held 6 × 10¹¹.)
- **Heritable fertility** (`fertility`; Fisher's fundamental theorem, standard; its use here speculation): where room on Earth or off it is slack, the mean growth rate past replacement rises at `V_FERT` (the additive variance, from fertility's heritability and its spread between families), eroding toward a frontier's `R_FRONT` (2.8%/yr: Malthus's colonies doubling in 25 years). It reaches half of that ~115 years after space opens (R ln 2/V). People on Earth and off it fill their rooms as logistics, stepped exactly.
- **Migration** (`migration`): out of Earth at `R_VOL`, and `R_MIG` as its habitability fails, as far as there's room off-world; back at `R_RET` as far as Earth has room (`earthRoom`). (Relaxing to half of everyone each way had sent half of a trillion off-world people back to Earth.)
- **Probes** (`stars`) leave once the swarm takes `F_PROBE` (1%) of the Sun, at `V_PROBE` (0.5) of light's speed; a star is seen settled once the light of a settlement there could be back, d/v + d/c ≤ t, and `N_STAR` (0.004 a cubic light-year) gives the count within reach. **People at other stars** (aligned; drawn only: light-years apart, each system lives on its own) are summed over shells out to the reach, each star's growing from `FOUNDERS_STAR` (10⁴) at the frontier's rate toward what the home system holds, from when the probes reach it.
- **The shade**: where Earth is sacrificed, the swarm shades it by its share of the Sun (−`S_ABS` × share, a global forcing sent with the waste heat); where it's preserved, the swarm keeps out of Earth's light.
- **End states** (earthlike's cached opening, 1,500 years past the dial): the swarm passes 1% of the Sun ~80 years after the dial (Mercury begun, the probes away) and holds at 80–90% of it, leaving about a quarter of Mercury. Aligned: in 2b Earth grows to its ecumenopolis (~1.5 × 10¹² by ~450 years after the dial); a trillion people off-planet by ~300 (2a) to ~370 (2b) years, the belt's habitats full at ~2.4 × 10¹⁵ by ~900; ~6 × 10²⁰ people at other stars by 1,500. Misaligned: no one off-planet. Each ending's probes have reached ~6 million stars.

## On the page

- **Threads.** The people run at level 6 on their own worker, linked to the engine's by a `MessageChannel`: after each climate cycle the engine sends their fields (`fieldsOf`, restricted), a few times a second the CO₂, the regolith and, while the dial resolves the year, its whole years since the last (`HarvestYears`); ~10 times a second the people send back their farmed shares, tillage, large game's share, carbon burned less captured, forcing and the sea's boiled share, which the engine applies around each fast step (`PeopleLink`: `CLIMATE.F_PEOPLE`, `Tectonics.boiled`, the cover's tree balance through `Life.onCover`).
- **The clock.** The page holds the planet at its opening while it loads and its climate settles, then places the first hominins; the people owe the years the page's ticks give them and step at their own pace (`People.nextDt`), and the page holds its clock while they're behind (more than a quarter second's years). Their worker steps as soon as a whole step is owed, or once the owed years have waited 50 ms; each lineage's spread runs over the cells its substeps can reach.
- **The follow dial**: the rate is set so a doubling of what changes fastest (the minds, farmed land, energy, AI's labour, people on and off Earth: `paceOf`) takes `follow.secs` (10 s), within what the people keep up with and at least 5 kyr/s before the first tip. Under hyperbolic growth the doubling time ∝ t* − t, so the dial falls log-linearly by itself. Taking the dial ends it; F starts it again.
- **Where people and machines are** (`places`, synthesis; drawn only): each cell's urban share (`U_AG` 8% farming, toward `U_IND` 80% with industry) lives in cities at a region's scale (`R_CITY`, by site, density^`G_CITY` and distance) and a country's (`N_CITY` of it to the `K_NATION` largest, density^`G_NATION`); cities follow their catchments over `T_CITY`. Factories, datacenters and robots sit on industry's footprint (following its workers over `T_MACH`), with a floor over the machines' land. Land use is exclusive, claimed in order: solar farms (the model's), datacenters (`D_DC` 500 W/m²), then cities (`D_URB` 4,000 a km²; rural people past `D_RUR` 500 a km² live in towns) and factories (`D_FAC` 30 W/m²), which share by what each wants when they want more than there is (an ecumenopolis), then fields.
- **The People view** (`globe.js` modes 7 and 8; `folk`, two vec4s a vertex): **Settlement** shows the Relief view's land where people have ever lived and grey terra incognita elsewhere; **Density** shows people as an accent tint by log density and Earth's robots at work (`earthBots`, placed on industry's footprint, the farms and the cities) as a verdigris tint. On both, land uses on the Zones mosaic's tesserae at a rank fixed to the globe (`landRank`), one pattern per kind in two colours: blocks for residences (cities accent, datacenters verdigris), furrowed parcels for fields (farms sap, solar indigo), factories a heavy sienna hatch; lakes over the uses; the veil as in the Air map.
- **The endings' marks**: off-world energy's share of the Sun as collectors round its disc (to the whole Sun), dimming it where Earth is sacrificed; the Moon's city (`moonbase` in `main.js`): residences by the Moon's people, datacenters and factories by compute and power off-world; the L4 and L5 habitats either side of the Moon by theirs; stars lit as their settlements' light comes back (accent; verdigris when misaligned; `starDistances` seeds each star's distance, a few to ~2,500 ly); the sea level falling as the oceans boil, heights measured from the air's datum (`Tectonics.airDatum`).
- **The solar system** (`src/engine/solar.js`, speculation in standard outline): today's on the `earth` seed (`OUR_SYSTEM`); any other seed's its own (`seededSystem`), about the same Sun and with the same Moon (the tides and the day are tuned to it): the home planet at 1 AU, none to three rocky planets inside it and three to six outside (period ratios as Kepler's systems have them), giants and ice giants past the snow line (~2.7 AU) with large moons, a belt of 5 × 10²⁰–2 × 10²² kg in the widest gap from rock to giant (else past the last planet). The swarm's quarry is the innermost rocky planet besides home; the worlds are home's L4, L5 and Moon, the rocky planets past home, the belt's largest body and the giants' moons, each opening by its distance (`opensAt`, 5.6 + 4.6 log₁₀ a, fitted to today's). Nothing is named: the text calls the quarry "the inner planet". The people's worker builds it from the seed (`People(planet, params, system)`).
- **Fig. 9, the system** (`src/render/system.js`, in the People tab once anything is off-world): a plan view on a log scale of distance (0.3–11 AU, or as far as the system reaches), the planets at fixed angles; the swarm's points, the belt's stipple thinning as it's mined, the inner planet shrinking as it's taken, and people at home, on each world and in habitats as patches of the residences' blocks as wide as log₁₀ of their number, with their numbers; named only in today's Solar System.
- **Costs**: a step ~5 ms with one lineage, ~14 with eight (level 6); the pyramids add ~5 ms to a step under `T_STABLE` with the land full (~19 → ~24 ms on earthlike's), and `places` ~13 ms every `DT_PLACES` years; steps up to `DT_EARLY` (2,000 yr) before any lineage tips, `DT_MAX` (500) after, `DT_ECON` (10) from early takeoff.
