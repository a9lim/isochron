# The model

Each layer's governing form, its grid and step, and the targets the constants are calibrated against. Each source file's header holds its layer's forms in full, with its claims marked standard, synthesis or speculation; this file is the map across them.

## Constraints that steer new work

- **One planet, one state, one dial.** Every layer reads and writes the same fields (elevation is one array: the plates raise it, erosion lowers it, ice loads it, the climate reads it), and each coupling is a live flux, because the couplings are the subject. Fig. 2 draws only couplings the code has.
- **The dial is scale separation.** Each layer declares its timescale, stable step, substep budget and closure (what stands in when the dial outruns it). The scheduler (`src/clock.js`) runs it live, summarized or banked, and every layer always advances model time, so the state is consistent at any dial. Drawing crossfades over half a decade of the dial and never switches.
- **Grids nest.** The icosahedral grid has 10·4^k + 2 cells. The engine runs at level 7 (55 km; 6 on phones, tablets and machines of four cores or 4 GB); the energy balance and the gyres at 5, the year's anomaly at 4, the mantle at 4, the tide at 6, the people at 6 and their routes on levels 5 and 4. The transfers are area-weighted and keep integrals.
- **The CPU is the reference.** Every GPU path has a CPU twin the headless tools and the tests run. The plates' CPU frames stay authoritative: the GPU mirrors them, and rifts, merges and initiation rewrite whole plates on the CPU.
- **Rules, not emergence**, for tectonics: rifting and subduction initiation are rules, not mantle convection.
- **Little more geology; life minimal except people.** The about panel carries the explanation, and the scene names and reads out values (the parent's "Inside the scene").
- **People write through the planet's own inputs** (carbon, tillage in the erosion field, land use on the cover, waste heat and sulfate as forcings, the boiled sea), not through new sinks.
- **Soft forms over hard caps** in the people's model: sums over uneven units harmonic, thresholds logistic hazards, floors exponential approaches. States decide nothing; everything they do is a flux.
- **The people's seam rules**, which batches 2b and 3 and the game rely on:
  1. People's state is per cell on their grid. The only world-level arrays are the industrial economy's core (output, capital, energy, compute), the endings and the polity table (capital, cohesion, identity, fiscal capacity, stores, office).
  2. Every flow between cells goes through one transport cost, the route graph.
  3. The world economy reads per-cell inputs and writes per-cell outputs, so it splits by polity by summing over a polity's cells.
  4. The endings' draw reads a state, so a race can replace it without touching the endings' mechanics.

## Threads

The page (`main.js`) keeps the clock and posts each frame's planned steps to the engine's worker (`src/engine/worker.js`), which owns the `Planet` and its own GPU device and runs the climate as an async loop of 10 ms slices. The page's device draws the globe and steps the weather; the chart's worker traces the synoptic chart; the people run at level 6 on their own worker (`src/engine/people-worker.js`), linked to the engine's by a `MessageChannel`. The clock holds while two plate steps are outstanding, so the dial's top is what the engine manages.

## An alternate Earth on this climate (the people's targets)

Sketched again on 2026-11-25 after the moist static energy round, from the settled climate's score (`climate-score.mjs`) and the people's material base on it (each region's arable land, floodplain, water control's room, domesticates, crops and herds at full use, coal, on the cached opening v21); the first sketch, after batch 4c, had a wet Sahel and a green Arabia. Synthesis throughout, the history speculation, Earth's numbers from memory; history's numbers are a sanity range, not the target.

*Where the climate departs from Earth's.* The Sahara is a desert again (~100 mm in its core, ~50 on Earth; its July ~31 °C against 32.5), the Sahel near Earth's (~590 mm), the Maghreb ~300, Arabia dry (~215 mm in its core, ~460 in Yemen's and Oman's hills). The rest of Africa is wetter than Earth's: West Africa ~2.8 m, the Ethiopian highlands ~2.1 m, East Africa ~1.5×, southern Africa ~2.5× (its Highveld ~1,540 mm against ~650, temperate rather than dry). Australia's interior is a semi-arid savanna (~580 mm against ~250), its southeast wet (~1,000) and its north a monsoon savanna as on Earth. India's monsoon falls on the Deccan (~1,640 mm) and spares the Ganges' plain (~460 mm against ~1,000–1,600), whose winters are ~8 K cold; China's Yangtze gets ~0.7 of Earth's rain; Southeast Asia ~1.3×. Tibet's winters are ~12 K cold. Eastern North America's winters are ~5 K mild and its summers ~4 K warm; the American West's summers ~7 K cool; Canada's January ~8 K mild. Beringia's summers stay near freezing (interior Alaska's July ~0 °C against ~13–15), and Europe's are ~3 K cool. Land's rain is ~18% over Earth's.

*Its material base.* By the crops the land could grow, fully farmed at the organic ceiling's start (on the rivers' discharge, 2026-11-26: `Planet.rivers`): Africa ~28% (southern Africa ~7.5%, East Africa ~6%, the Sahel ~4.5%, the Congo ~4.5%, West Africa ~3%, North Africa ~0.5%), the Americas ~36% (the Amazon ~12%, southeastern South America ~7.5%, eastern North America ~6%), Asia ~25% (China ~6%, Southeast Asia ~5%, the steppe ~4%, India ~2.5%, Arabia and Iran ~2%), Oceania ~7.5% (Australia ~6%), Europe ~4%. Read first on the landscape's stale discharge, which gave every desert floodplains, it had North Africa ~6%, Arabia and Iran ~5%, the Sahel ~8%, India ~4% and the Amazon ~8%. Herds are Australia's (~14%), southeastern South America's, the Sahel's and North America's. Domesticates are richest where the seasons are dry: the Nile, southern Africa, the Fertile Crescent, China, Australia, India and the Sahel. Coal lies on low, flat basins: Siberia, the Amazon, Australia, Canada, eastern North America, the Sahel, North Africa and the steppe hold most. This base counts land, not its age or its paddies: the model's fertility reads today's leaching against renewal, so a stable, dry, ancient shield (Australia's, much of Africa's, the Amazon's) farms like young land, and its wet-season crop is the same on a bunded paddy as on a dry field (the people's round 2).

*Corridors and barriers.* The Sahara is a barrier again, crossed along the Nile and its coasts; Arabia is a desert with green hills at its southern edge, so the way out of Africa runs by the Nile and the Red Sea's shores. The Himalaya and a very cold Tibet part India and China. The Americas are joined to Asia only by a Beringia whose summers are near freezing; Australia by the Sunda shelf and Wallacea's straits. The Atlantic is narrowest between West Africa and Brazil.

*How history runs on it.* The first people arise in East Africa and leave it by the Nile and the Red Sea's coast; the archaic peoples hold Eurasia until the first people's knowledge outruns theirs, so people reach southern Asia and Sahul ~65–50 kyr ago and Europe ~45 kyr ago. The Americas wait for people who can live on a cold coast, ~20–12 kyr ago. Wetter southern Africa and Australia hold more foragers than Earth's (~3–15 M at 10,000 BCE). Farming begins ~12–10 kyr ago in the seasonally dry belts: the Fertile Crescent, the Nile, the Sahel, southern Africa's Highveld, India, northern and southern China, later Mesoamerica, the Andes and the eastern woodlands; in Australia only as crops, late if at all, on soils too old to hold many. Bunded paddies in the wet season's floods make the warm, ponding lowlands the densest farmland, several times dry farming's, where crops that began in one carry them (Southeast Asia's, Bengal's and the Deccan's coasts, the Niger's inland delta; the Yangtze, with ~0.7 of Earth's rain, has little room for them); the old, leached shields of the Congo, the Amazon and much of southern Africa hold fewer than their rain suggests. The first states rise ~5.5 kyr ago on the Nile and in Mesopotamia, then on the Indus and the Ganges, in China, and on the Niger. Before industry Asia holds the most people (~30–45%: India ~3–8%, China ~5–12%), Africa ~20–30%, the Americas ~8–15% (~40–80 M before contact), Europe ~4–8%, Oceania ~1–4%. Industry begins where dense farmers, cities, learning and coal meet: most often in China, northern India or the Mediterranean and the Near East, sometimes in Africa's Highveld or the Sahel, by chance elsewhere; rarely in Australia. Ships cross the open ocean late (~1500), most likely first from the Old World's western edge to Brazil; the Americas then meet the Old World's crowd diseases together and lose half or more of their people within a century, and fill again with settlers and captives. The navigators' and the industrial cores' empires hold a large share of the world under unlike rulers by 1913 (Earth's ~31%) until schooling breaks them up. At early takeoff Asia holds ~30–45% of the people, Africa ~20–30%, the Americas ~12–20%, Europe ~4–8%, Oceania ~1–4%.
