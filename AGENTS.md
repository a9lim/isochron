# Isochron

An Earth-like planet on one globe, from plate tectonics down to weather, with every layer coupled to the others. `docs/PLAN.md` is the plan of record: the thesis (one planet, one state, one dial), the nine layers, their couplings, the scene, the figures, the experiments, the phases, and the roadmap ahead (phases 6–10: the year, ice that carves, minimal life, dust, the engine on the GPU). This file describes the code as it is.

It is a submodule of the a9l.im repository at `projects/isochron` (github.com/a9lim/isochron). It is registered on the site (`content/projects/isochron.md`, with its social card) and staged by the parent's build; the parent's dev server serves it. The card (`og-image.webp`; the parent's `tools/og/paint/isochron.py`) is painted from a seeded run of the people on the follow dial's axis, exported by the parent's `tools/og/paint/isochron-run.mjs`: a change to the people's model doesn't repaint it, so re-export and repaint when the card should follow one. The root `AGENTS.md` covers the shared layer, the design system ("Inside the scene") and the build.

## Status

Phases 1 (the tectonic globe), 2 (surface and climate), 3 (ice and carbon) and 4 (ocean and weather) are built, phase 5 (the harmony) in part, and phase 6 (the year) at its core, apart from the items below.

- **Phase 1:** the grid, the clock and scheduler, plates with exact rigid motion, gaps and overlaps, slabs and arcs, collisions and merges, rifting, passive-margin subduction initiation, boundary relaxation, the torque balance, isostasy, solved sea level, the polarity record, and the WebGPU globe with stripes, ridge axes, hillshade, coastline and contours. Plate boundaries are traced into smooth, classified lines and drawn in tectonic-map symbols.
- **Phase 2:** the landscape (routing, stream power, sediment, crustal diffusion), the seasonal energy-balance model, the circulation template, moisture and rain, the soft Köppen zones, the Climate view, the margin's figures (zonal means, hypsometry, polarity) and the zones' key. The dial runs from 1 yr/s to 10 Myr/s; below a few years a second the globe shows the season.
- **Phase 3:** the orbit's seeded cycles (averaged over what the dial passes in ~2 s), ice sheets (shallow ice, degree-day mass balance, the bed's load, the sea's loss), the carbon thermostat, hotspots and large igneous provinces (with rifting through a plume head), the record and the orbit (now Figs. 6 and 7), and the experiments: Earth-like, Snowball, Ice ages, High stand, Thermostat, each with one knob and its number in the caption (since 2026-11-16 the controls: "Scenarios and views").
- **Phase 4:** the wind-driven ocean (Stommel's gyres, surface currents, SST anomalies with upwelling) feeding evaporation and rain; the two-layer QG weather (on the GPU, at 55 km) with surface temperature and moisture tracers; the Weather view as a synoptic chart (isobars, rain stipple, fronts, dry lines, highs and lows), crossfading to the climate's mean chart when the dial outruns it. The dial reaches 2.8 h/s.
- **Phase 5:** Fig. 2, the model diagram, on its own margin tab: every layer a node with its live value, every coupling the code has an arrow weighted by its flux (the weather's feedback to the climate isn't built, so it has no arrow); since 2026-11-16 only what is stepping, and with people their economy ("The margin").
- **Optimization pass (2026-11-15):** the planet runs on a worker, the weather and the season's drawing on the GPU, the chart on its own worker (see "Threads and the GPU"). The engine runs at level 7 (55 km), or level 6 (110 km, a quarter of the work) on phones and tablets (`?level=` overrides); the globe and the weather run at level 7, a coarser engine's fields refined onto the mesh on the GPU (`src/gpu/refine.js`, Loop subdivision); the ice and the climate's moisture run on the engine's own GPU device, the energy balance and the ocean on a 220 km grid at every level. WebGPU is required in the page; the engine's CPU code stays as the headless reference (tools and tests), and `tests/gpu-node.test.mjs` checks the GPU paths against it.
- **Tides and the Moon (2026-11-14):** the tide solved as shallow water on the rotating sphere (CPU reference `src/engine/tides.js`, GPU `src/gpu/tides.js`, re-solved on the engine's worker), its dissipation slowing the spin and receding the Moon (`Moon`), the spin read by the climate, the gyres and the weather; the Tides view (a co-tidal chart); the sky behind the globe (`src/render/sky.js`: stars, the Sun, the Moon); rivers by the month at a slow dial.

## Running and checking

Serve from the parent root with `node docs/sim-overhaul/serve.mjs 8790` (the parent `.claude/launch.json` calls it "sims") and open `/isochron/`. `?seed=<string>` picks the planet; without one the page draws six letters and writes them into the link (`history.replaceState`), so a link always names its planet. `?seed=earth` is today's Earth, from `earth/` ("Today's Earth"); the headless tools take `earth` as a seed too (`openPlanet` in `planet.js` loads its data). Open `/isochron/tests/gpu.html` for the GPU's tests (multigrid convergence, PV conservation, Phillips' criterion, the step's cost; `?l6` times level 6 too); results land in `window.__results`.

```bash
(cd tests && npm install)               # once: WebGPU in node (Dawn), for the GPU parity tests
node --test tests/                       # grid, clock, plates, landscape, climate, ice, carbon, ocean, and GPU ice, moisture, tide, plates and surface against the CPU (the weather's are in tests/gpu.html)
node tools/engine-cost.mjs 7 20 0.5 earthlike gpu   # the engine's plate step by part at level 7 (drop gpu for the CPU's)
node tools/planet-run.mjs earthlike 600 1000 10 '{"CARBON":{"FIXED":220}}'   # the coupled planet: 600 kyr of ice ages (overrides also ICE, REGOLITH, CRUSTFLEX)
node tools/regolith-survey.mjs earthlike 220 10 800 200 20   # the Regolith experiment's survey: the ice's dominant period by window, regolith started 10 m deep
node tools/ice-check.mjs earthlike 80     # ice grown under the climate and orbit
node tools/survey.mjs '{}' 600 b         # 300 Myr of plates and landscape: continents, land, sea level, erosion, speeds ('{"FLEX":true}': with the crust's flexure)
node --test tests/earth.test.mjs         # today's Earth: its opening against the data at levels 6 and 5, its reversals, its orbit today (tools/earth/README.md remakes the data)
node tools/climate-check.mjs earthlike   # the climate on a seeded planet (or `earth`, with its ice sheets held), run until it settles (TRACE=1: each cycle): means, sea ice, zonal profile, zone shares, land seasons (and the seasonal range at 45°, land's summer at 60–70° N), the ground's runoff, the permafrost's share and zones, and the lakes
node tools/plates-survey.mjs '{}' 600 b  # plates alone
node tools/eruption-run.mjs earthlike 1 10 30   # an eruption headless: each size's peak cooling and e-folding against the theory
node tools/pulse-run.mjs earthlike 500 2000 10000   # a carbon pulse headless: each size's share left in the air after 1, 10, 100 kyr against the theory
node tools/dust-check.mjs earthlike 7 20  # the dust on a seeded planet, cycle by cycle (GPU moisture and dust with Dawn): emission, lifetime, where it lands, snow, iron, loess
node tools/iceage-survey.mjs earthlike 220 free 800 20   # the ice ages with every amplifier, CO₂ free (or held) from a baseline: the ice's period, its budget (snow, melt, basal melt, calving, closing against dV/dt), the swings of CO₂ and dust; OVERRIDES='{"ICE":{...}}' for sensitivity runs
node tools/anomaly-fit.mjs earthlike 240 2   # the anomaly's amplitude fitted to the QG weather (Dawn: npm install in tests/): days and weathers a season; prints YEAR.Q
node tools/people/settle.mjs earthlike 4e6 '{}'   # each landmass's crossing in and when it's first settled, a tenth and half of it, against farming's first origin (stops at industry)
node tools/people-run.mjs earthlike 5e6 '{}'   # the people prototype (Roadmap 2.0): hominins placed on the settled planet (cached in tools/people/.cache/), run to AFTER (300) years past the dial, the ending drawn from the seed ('{"ENDING":"2b"}' forces one); events, the hyperbola's fit, the follow dial's wall-clock (QUIET=1: events only; LIVE=1 TRACE=1: a live planet from the first farming origin, each climate cycle logged; ~5–10 min)
for f in main.js colors.js src/*.js src/*/*.js; do node --check "$f"; done
```

`window.__isochron` exposes the page's mirrors of the engine (`P, tec, land, climate, ice, carbon, plumes, year`), the `engine` worker, `wx`, `globe`, `chart`, `camera`, `scheduler`, `setPlaying`, `setView`, `setMap` (a map by name: `relief`, `zones`, `air`, `pressure`, `temperature`, `humidity`, `tides`, `settlement`, `density`), `setDial` (the dial, log₁₀ yr/s), `tick(now)` (one frame, for driving a hidden tab), `prof` (each section's share of a frame, ms), `engineProf` (the worker's: ms per section between fast messages, the climate's CPU, its GPU waits and its longest slice) and `lakes` (the drawn lakes' last fields), `air` (the Air map's streamlines), `lines` and `rivers` (as drawn); `P.years` is the planet's clock, `P.agg.ground` and `P.agg.lakes` the ground's and the lakes' sums, `P.regolith` and `P.flex` the regolith's and the crust's flexure's statistics, `people` (the People experiment: its thread, its last report `msg`, the drawn fields, the link's statistics) and `follow` (the follow dial: `on`, `secs`). `?scenario=people` opens the People scenario, and `&ending=1a` (or `1b`, `2a`, `2b`) forces its ending.

WGSL compiles only in the browser, so check the console (and `tests/gpu.html`) after shader edits. Derivatives (`fwidth`) must stay in uniform control flow, which is why the fragment shader computes both sea and land and then selects.

## How the plates work

- **Plate frames.** Each plate keeps its crust in its own frame, on a full copy of the grid (mask, thickness, felsic fraction, formation time, suture time). World cell i reads plate k at Qₖ⁻¹xᵢ. Motion is exact (a full turn restores every cell), and nothing is resampled while a plate stays whole. Merges are the one resampling: one plate's cells are re-expressed in the other's frame.
- **The frames' extras** (`FRAME_EXTRA`): other layers' state kept with the crust, the regolith (`reg`, m) and the flexure's correction and the column's mass it last saw (`cf`, m; `mq`). They travel as crust does (`copyCell`: relaxation, merges, rifts, initiation, refinement) and new crust takes their defaults (`freshExtras`). The GPU's step doesn't hold them: its edits name a relaxed cell's source frame cell, and the CPU carries the extras from it; a frame cell holding crust made this step (its formation time says so, even when it was consumed and made again within the step) starts fresh (tests/gpu-plates.test.mjs).
- **The step.** Each world cell tests the plates that held it or a neighbour last step:
  - none covers it: new oceanic crust for the plate that left;
  - one covers it: that plate;
  - several cover it: the most buoyant stays on top. The others are consumed only if the plates are closing at that cell (`CLOSING`). An overlap where plates slide past each other is grid rounding, and the loser stays hidden. Without that rule, transforms churn crust and leave false slabs that pull along the boundary.
- **Consumption.** Ocean that goes down leaves slab memory at the cell, inherited along a migrating trench and decaying over `SLAB_TAU`, and builds arc crust on the overriding plate. Continent against continent stacks crust, marks a suture, adds collision resistance, and tallies the pair toward a merge.
- **Forces.**
  - Slab pull acts along each plate's edge only: each edge cell pulls with the longest slab of its plate within one cell. Counting the whole memory band made pull grow with speed and run away. It pulls down-dip, across the trench toward the top plate (standard): along the relative motion, the overriding plate steered the sinking one and reinforced spin. A slab hangs only where the plates close across the boundary by `SLAB_CLOSING` (0.6) of their relative motion (consumption still starts at `CLOSING`'s 0.3: there transform zigzags on the grid hung false slabs, 20–30% of all pull on Earth's opening), only on ocean, and its memory lasts `SLAB_TAU` (15 Myr, about how long its upper-mantle part takes to sink: at 4 a trench at Earth's speeds kept ~40% of a saturated slab, so pull grew with speed).
  - Each slab resists its own sinking (`K_TRENCH`, along its pull, as long as it is; standard in kind, Forsyth and Uyeda's slab resistance; the form synthesis): a plate its slabs drive moves at about their sinking speed, `K_SLAB`/`K_TRENCH` (12.7 cm/yr at full age), whatever its size, so speed follows the share of a plate's edge that subducts rather than that edge's ratio to its area; before, a small plate with a trench ran to `V_MAX`. `K_SLAB` 0.04 against it (0.008 before).
  - Ridge push is the gradient of subsidence within a plate's own ocean.
  - Drag is −c ∫ (1 + KEEL·φ)(I − r rᵀ) dA · ω, regularized by plate area.
  - ω relaxes toward the solved value over `V_RELAX` and is capped at `V_MAX` (20 cm/yr; India reached about that ~60–50 Ma, from memory; at 15 the cap bound 27–94% of the time once each plate was one body, so it shaped the motion rather than the forces).
- **Starting subduction.** Old seafloor founders: at a plate's edge, any cell past `FOUNDER_AGE`; at its own continent's margin, the attached ocean breaks away as a new plate (`maybeInitiate`, at most every `INITIATE_GAP` and below `MAX_LIVE_PLATES`).
- **Plate count.** Too few plates, or one too large (`MAX_PLATE_AREA`, a quarter of the sphere: Earth's Pacific is ~20%, and at a fifth it rifted on Earth's first step), splits the largest along the candidate plane that crosses the most sutures. A plate below `MICROPLATE` joins its largest neighbour.
- **Boundary relaxation.** A cell whose neighbours mostly belong to another plate passes its crust to that plate (`relaxBoundaries`, every step). Nearest-cell rounding otherwise roughens boundaries a cell at a time until they are fractal and dotted with one-cell islands. This is a numerical regularization (a curvature flow at one cell's scale): it moves crust and never makes or destroys any.
- **The ocean** (`seaMask` in `crust.js`, `solveSea`; synthesis). The ocean is the cells below sea level that the open sea reaches: connected through cells below the level, and through straits, cells that were the ocean's and stand less than `SEA.STRAIT` (25 m, the sub-grid relief's half range) above it now, so a sill doesn't open and close with every metre the sea moves. A body of at least `SEA.OCEAN_MIN` (2% of the sphere) or the largest is ocean, and so is one at least `SEA.TRAPPED` (half) floored by oceanic crust: trapped ocean, which the grid closes but straits far narrower than a cell keep joined on Earth (the Mediterranean, the Black Sea). Counted as lakes, such basins 5–6.7 km deep filled with fresh water in humid climates and held ~12 M km³ (32 m of sea) after 40 Myr at level 6, the sea 60 m lower and the planet 1.4 K cooler than before; with them as ocean the sea moved −18 m over those 40 Myr (−59 before) and the lakes held 0.4–2.1 M km³. The rest are basins, land to every layer, which hold lakes or lie dry. The level holds the sea's water (less the ice's and the lakes': `seaWater`) over the ocean alone, by Newton with the basins excluded; where it then covers a basin's sill, the sea floods it from its edge (the basin's lakes, `lakeDepth` × area, going back to the sea) and the level is solved again, lower for it, until none joins; ground it now covers that the sea doesn't reach is excluded and solved again too. A basin the sea leaves (found at the solve's start, at the incoming level) keeps its water: `cutWater` per cell, counted in `lakeWater` until the planet's lakes take it (`Planet.rebuildLakes`), so the level doesn't move; in the spin-up, without lakes, it goes back to the sea. A cut waits for the next solve, so one solve never both reaches and leaves a basin. On the GPU's path the CPU solves after the step (the GPU's level fills every basin): ~3 ms at level 7. On the default seed's opening (level 7) 21 basins are continental, the deepest pits in them to 6.7 km. Tested on a made-up planet (`tests/sea.test.mjs`).
- **One body.** A plate is one connected piece (`resolveFragments`, every `FRAGMENTS` 0.5 Myr of plate time, after the step's rare events, on both paths). An initiation taking all the ocean between a plate's continents, a trench through a neck, a rift's plane or rounding can cut a plate apart; left alone, a piece rides its parent's rotation through the plate around it, its own young crust filling the gap behind and consuming the old seafloor ahead, and the slabs it hangs pull its neighbour all round the ring (before 2026-11-15, pieces covered 4–18% of the surface at level 6, 36–44 of them at level 7, drawing rings and tongues of ridge and trench in open ocean, and a fifth to a half of the opening's pulling trench cells were on their rings). Each plate keeps its largest piece; another of at least `MICROPLATE` becomes a plate (its parent's frame and ω) below `MAX_LIVE_PLATES`, else joins the plate whose body it borders most, re-expressed in its frame. ~1.6 ms a check at level 7.
- **Substeps.** A cell finds its plates among those that held it or a neighbour, so a plate can move about one cell a step; past that, crust running ahead of a plate's edge goes unseen and is hidden instead of consumed (two plates closing at 20 cm/yr each at level 7: 0.98 of the closing consumed up to 1.1 cells a step, 0.77 at 1.8, 0.43 at 3.6, CPU and GPU alike; slabs only grow where crust is consumed). A step whose fastest plate would move more than `STEP_CELLS` (1) cell runs as substeps (`Tectonics.step`, `stepAsync`): about two a 0.5 Myr step at level 7 once a plate passes ~11 cm/yr, none at 1 Myr/s's 0.1 Myr steps, and in the spin-up (boot +0.3 s).
- **Genesis.** The largest continents each seed a plate at their centre, and ocean seeds fill the rest. Plates grow at their own pace, faster over continental crust, so boundaries meet at sea and continents start as passengers with passive margins.
- **Order.** The step visits the cells in index order, and three of its effects follow that order: a slab inherits a neighbour's slab already grown this step (so along an index-ordered run of trench cells it grows several cells in one step), a consumed frame cell is gone for the cells after it, and a founder keeps its later neighbours from foundering. The first matters: at level 7, inheriting from the step's start instead ran the plates at 2.3 against 2.9 cm/yr over 40 Myr, and level 7's slab scaling (√res) was fitted with it. The GPU's step reproduces all three (`src/gpu/plates.js`). An order-independent reference would be cleaner, but level 7 would need refitting first.

## How the landscape works

`Landscape` (`src/engine/landscape.js`) steps after every plate step, on the same crust.
- **Routing.** The ocean's cells are outlets (the plates' mask: a basin below sea level the sea doesn't reach is a depression like any other, filling with sediment). A priority flood fills depressions (lakes) with a small rise so flats still drain, and each cell drains to its steepest neighbour on the filled surface. The outlets head the order, and only those beside dry ground seed the flood (the open sea reaches only more sea, and pushing it through the heap was four fifths of the flood's cost); the flood's pop order follows, a topological order, so accumulation and the solver are single passes. The lakes route the smooth surface with the same flood (`lakes.js`, `reroute`).
- **Erosion.** Stream power with n = 1, dz/dt = −K Qᵐ S, solved implicitly from the outlets upstream (Braun and Willett's scheme), so any step is stable. Q is drainage area weighted by the climate's runoff. Rock removed lowers the surface by only (1 − ρ_crust/ρ_mantle) of its thickness, because the crust rebounds, so erosion takes thickness (`f` in the code is that factor).
- **Sediment** goes downstream with the water. Lakes keep what fits; at the coast it builds a shelf to `SHELF` below sea level and then moves to the deepest neighbouring sea. Deposits mix felsic material into the crust, so a thick enough delta turns oceanic crust continental.
- **The regolith and the ice.** Rivers strip the regolith before rock: the layer thins at the landscape's erosion rate (`erosion`), production against it solved exactly in `src/engine/regolith.js`; the thickness removed here is the same either way. The ice's outwash (half of the till that melts out, carried by its meltwater) waits in `sedIn` and joins the rivers' sediment at the next step where it melted out (`flux.outwash`). `preview(dt)` gives the rates a step would cut without cutting (the regolith's opening).
- **Diffusion.** Continental thickness diffuses between continental cells, faster under thick crust (hillslopes and small rivers below the grid's scale, and the collapse of orogens). It replaces phase 1's non-conservative relaxation of thick crust: erosion, sediment and diffusion move crust and never make or destroy it (tested). Explicit, in substeps that keep κ_max·ds under a fifth of the smallest cell's share (`plan`: ~5 at level 7 for 0.25 Myr, ~9 for 0.5, four times more a level), over a typed edge list built once, on the continental cells packed together (in cache), and written back to the frames, which every world cell then reads back (cells that share a frame cell see its sum).
- **On the CPU (phase 10b).** The flood, accumulation, the implicit solve and the sediment's carry are sequential down the drainage. Their parallel forms would need few passes here (level 7's receiver tree is at most 72 cells deep and Planchon and Darboux's filling converges in 52 Jacobi passes), but the step then needs the order, receivers, fill and lake flags back, and on the engine's device any readback waits behind the ice's steps (Threads and the GPU). The diffusion was ported (a gather per cell, f32, 3.8 mm from the CPU's; in the history, `git log -- src/gpu/landscape.js`): 1.8 ms against 5.4 alone, the same as the CPU's in the worker, overlapped with the rivers or not, so it stayed here. Tests check the flood, the months and the diffusion against their first forms (`tests/landscape.test.mjs`).
- **Water.** Sediment fills basins and fast spreading makes young, shallow seafloor, and both raise the sea. Over `WATER_TAU` (150 Myr) the ocean's volume drifts toward the land fraction set at genesis, a stand-in for the water ridges degas and trenches subduct. Earth's freeboard has held for billions of years by a mechanism still debated. High stands from fast spreading still happen on shorter timescales (seed `c` drowns to 18% land and recovers).

## How the climate works

`Climate` (`src/engine/climate.js`) is a diagnostic of the geography, recomputed in cycles: the energy balance, then winds and rain at six points of the year, then the gyres (after the winds that drive them: before them, the first cycle's saw none, and the currents stayed zero until the fourth), then the zones, then the ground's periodic year (sliced in sixteen, ~6 ms each at level 7). `work(ms)` advances a cycle for a time budget (a generator yields between stages), so the engine's worker spends the time between ticks on it and the climate relaxes toward the current geography. Every stage yields within ~10–15 ms at level 7 (the zones and their zonal means a slice of the cells at a time, the months' and the points' cosines taken once; the dust's sources, packing and routing in pieces; the gyres every 20 iterations), and the worker runs what follows a cycle (`Planet.afterClimateSteps`: the cover's climate, then the sea's inputs, weathering's map and the opening's settle in one piece, so the sea never steps on inputs it hasn't settled to) the same way. `solve(n)` runs whole cycles headless. The climate has **settled** (`stats.settled`, the cycle it did) once two cycles running moved the mean temperature by less than 0.02 K and rain, global and on land, by less than 0.5% (`SETTLE_T`, `SETTLE_P`; at the latest `SETTLE_MAX`, 16): from a cold start that takes 7–9 cycles headless on three seeds and ~10 on the page (~10 s at level 7), and the opening's references wait for it (see "How life works"). The slow part is the cover and the rain feeding each other (trees transpire and darken the land; each cycle closes about half the gap, and the gap moves downwind from cycle to cycle, so Anderson's mixing on the cover, β or the temperature across cycles didn't help); with the cover held, rain settles in three cycles. `tools/climate-check.mjs` runs to the settled climate and tunes on it: cycle 4, where the climate was first tuned, was a transient ~2.5 K warmer and ~15% wetter than where the old constants settled.

## How ice and carbon work

`Planet` (`src/engine/planet.js`) steps the layers together: plates, plumes, the landscape and the crust's flexure per half-Myr plate step; carbon, ice and the regolith every frame; the climate's cycles in the background, each completed one refreshing weathering's map (`carbon.measure`) and, through the climate's version, the ice's mass balance.
- **The orbit** (`OrbitCycles`): eccentricity near 405, 124 and 95 kyr, obliquity near 41 and 54 kyr, precession near 22 kyr, seeded around Earth's values (from memory). The climate sees each term scaled by sinc(W/P) for a window W of what the dial passes in ~2 s, so at plate speed it sees the mean orbit and at kyr/s the cycles whole. The insolation table is rebuilt when the orbit changes.
- **Ice** (`Ice`, stepped on the GPU in the page by `src/gpu/ice.js`: four dispatches a step, the step size from an atomic maximum, never read back mid-run, the count planned from the limit last read with 5% spare; 48 steps a tick at most; the state read back every ~100 ms, the bed's fields and the glacial integrals every ~400 ms): thickness on the surface grid, in the mantle's frame. The flux is the shallow-ice approximation's, −Γ H⁵ |∇s|² ∇s, plus sliding's, explicit with the step their sum's stability allows (~25 yr at 110 km, ~5 at 55 km on hard rock, ~3 on soft till); `advance(years, ms)` steps within a real-time budget and drops the rest, so at plate speed the ice lags the climate rather than the clock (its closure). The mass balance is snow below freezing at the ice's surface (the lapse rate makes a high sheet keep itself cold) less melt by positive degree days, less basal melt. Ice flows only out of a cell that has it (ice below a bare cliff drew on the cliff, since the diffusivity is the ice's: an ice-filled pit beside a bare 2 km peak at 40° made ~160 km³ a year from nothing, melted as fast). The ice's water is taken from the sea (`tec.iceWater`) by its volume above flotation (below it the ice fills basin the sea would: counting all of it overstated marine ice's draw on the sea); the sea is re-solved when that has moved ~10 cm. The climate counts grounded ice as land, its surface as the height, and its albedo as snow's; a marine front's floating ice is sea. Its budget (`stats.snow`, `melt`, `basal`, `calving`, m³ of ice a year) is the CPU's steps' realized flows over the last advance, and closes against the volume's change to rounding (headless; the GPU's `calving` comes back with its integrals, the rest are the instantaneous rates over the ice).

The surface over open sea is sea level and floating ice's its freeboard, s = max(b + H, (1 − ρᵢ/ρw) H) (standard). Before, an open sea cell's surface was its floor, so every coast was a cliff the ice ran down at the speed limit and ice reaching the sea was afloat and gone: 95–98% of the snow left that way, melt 1.5–5%, and the margins answered nothing but the coastline. At 300 m: 227 m/yr in a freezing sea, 1.6 km/yr at 3 K, 2.5 at 5 K. A cap of snow running out onto a 200 m shelf grounds across it in a freezing sea and holds at the coast at 5 K or over 1,500 m of water; the GPU matches the CPU there to 0.1 m of a 5 km dome and its calving to 0.2% (`tests/ice-margins.test.mjs`). - **Carbon** (`Carbon`): outgassing against weathering, stepped through the sea's carbon (see "How life works"; `pCO₂` is the air's). Outgassing scales with crust opened at ridges and ocean closed at trenches, measured from the plates' motion across each boundary (`tec.kinematics`: counting gap cells also counted the grid's rounding, a few cells per boundary per step, so the rate grew as steps shrank), against the spin-up's last 10 Myr, plus provinces' pulses. The arcs' share is split: the carbonate the plates carry down comes back from the sea's ledger (its 40% of what's subducted, ~half the opening's outgassing), so `mantle()` is the ridges' and the arcs' own carbon (the arcs' share less the ledger's opening return), and the Outgassing control scales it. Its weathering reference is taken once the climate has run three cycles and the landscape has stepped (before phase 7 the opening's erosion was zero until then, the supply a quarter, and the reference came out 2.7× low: headless and at slow dials, CO₂ crashed to ~60 ppm at the first landscape step; the regolith's production and the landscape's preview fill it from the opening now); until then the map is the opening's and so is the CO₂ it saw. Weathering is the climate's map (runoff^0.65 × e^{(T − 15)/Tₑ} × fresh-rock supply, 0.25 + 0.75 P/(P + E_HALF) with P the regolith's production, × the cover's factor, summed over ice-free land), times the response to CO₂ since the climate last solved (the sensitivity through Tₑ, and pCO₂^0.3 directly). `equilibrium(V)` is the analytic balance (tested on the planet).
- **Plumes** (`Plumes`): ten hotspots fixed in the mantle build crust on whatever passes (seamount chains) and lift swells (`tec.swell`). Plume heads arrive as a Poisson process (every ~30 Myr): a flood of basalt over a disc ~1,100 km across within 1 Myr, 8,000 Pg of carbon, a new hotspot, and, under a large continent, a rift through the plume (`tec.rift(k, point)`).

## How life works

Enough life to make the environment robust, no more (a9). Two models, each tested alone, wired in by `Life` (`src/engine/life.js`).
- **The cover** (`Vegetation`, `src/engine/vegetation.js`; VECODE's forms, synthesis): per land cell, trees, grass and bare ground, the vegetated share relaxing over 3 years and the trees' share of it over 200, each exactly over any step, toward VECODE's equilibrium from degree-days above 0 °C (the climate's twelve monthly temperatures, each month's days spread by 4 K), the year's rain and a cold limit (not the soil's water, the module's option: the ground's water follows the rain, so scaling the rain by it counted aridity twice, and over ~40 cycles the default seed's bare share grew to 42% of land against 25% without, and the planet cooled 0.8 K more); NPP by Lieth's Miami model with CO₂ fertilization, carbon in trees, grass and soil (respiration by a Q10), solved in closed form, so NPP − respiration = ΔC to rounding. It lives in the mantle's frame, as the ice does. Its climate is set after each climate cycle, before weathering's map is measured (~16 ms at level 7); it steps from `Planet.fast` at most ten times a second of the dial and at least a year at a time (~2.3 ms at level 7), so past its τ it is its climate's (the closure), and its carbon's trade goes to the carbon cycle's next step. At the opening the cover sits at its balance with each cycle until the climate has settled (the closure; `Life.closed`), with the air held at the opening's CO₂ (`Carbon.holdOpening`); the sea then settles on the planet (below), so the land's store (~2,100–2,300 Pg in plants and soil; 45–48% trees, 18–21% bare on three seeds, converged) is full before anything is measured.
- **The cover's feedbacks** (standard): land's albedo from VECODE's surface albedos at half their contrast about grass (bare +0.065, trees −0.035), under snow masked by trees (snowy forest down to 0.40 against open snow's 0.62: the boreal forest's warmth); land's evaporation, bare ground (0.4) and grass at the ground's β, trees (1.2) at √β (deeper roots); weathering, bare rock at a quarter of forest's. Retuned so the opening's climate holds (`CLIMATE.ALB_COVER` 0.03 on snow-free land absorbs the cover's darker land and the forests' snow masking, ~1.2 K together; `LAND_EVAP_COVER` 0.52): `tools/climate-check.mjs` now attaches the cover. Its first cycles are a transient (the cover and the rain feed each other); the converged climate, which the tool now runs to, was retuned ("How the climate works").
- **The sea's carbon** (`OceanCarbon`, `src/engine/oceancarbon.js`, after LOSCAR and the three-box models): the air; a warm surface box (85% of the sea), a cold one (15%) and the deep, each with DIC, alkalinity and phosphate; carbonate chemistry with constants checked against PyCO2SYS; gas exchange, a 20 Sv overturning and mixing, the biological pump; carbonate buried on warm shallow shelves ((Ω − 1)^1.7) and on the deep floor above the compensation depth (a bioturbated layer per 150 m bin of the seafloor's hypsometry, dissolving below saturation), riding the plates into the trenches, 40% of it back at arcs. Backward Euler in seven unknowns, any step stable; ~0.05 ms a tick. Its inputs from the planet (`Life.seaInputs`, each cycle): the cold box is the coldest 15% of the sea by annual surface temperature (the climate's and the gyres' anomaly; on the opening planet −1.8 °C, the warm box ~20.5 °C), its open water the share not under sea ice; warm shelves are sea under ~200 m (soft, 100–300) with water above ~18 °C (soft); productivity `bio` is the rivers' sediment and the coasts' upwelling against the opening's; carbonate weathering goes with land's runoff. It settles on these at the end of the cover's closure (the cycle the climate settles in; the air held at the opening's CO₂ until then), the references taken there; weathering's waits for the landscape's first step too. The opening (default seed): saturation at ~2.2 km, compensation at ~4.1 km, the shelves burying 0.13 Pg C/yr of carbonate and the deep 0.12, the arcs returning 0.048 (the mantle the other 0.052 of the 0.1 outgassed).
- **The export, drawn** (synthesis): each surface box's export production spread over its water by what feeds it, the warm box's by a background, the coasts' upwelling and the rivers' mouths, the cold box's evenly over its open water, blended across the cold box's edge; g C m⁻² yr⁻¹ per sea cell, posted with the climate (in the zones' free slot, which the globe reads). On the opening planet the gyres' ~4, coasts and the cold sea 12–56.
- **Pulse** (`src/engine/pulse.js`): the share of a pulse left in the air, against a twin of the sea copied at the pulse and stepped with the same inputs; the theory is the closed sea's partition (1 kyr), the compensated sea's (10 kyr: CO₃ restored, pCO₂ ∝ HCO₃², each carbon taken up bringing a carbonate from the floor), then weathering's decay of the excess at the compensated partition (100 kyr). See "Scenarios and views".
- **No green margin.** Started under full forest and under bare ground, the planet's climate and cover converge to one state on three seeds within ~40 cycles (the moisture and the ground are slow to converge, so for ~20 cycles they look bistable), so there is no Green margin experiment.

## How dust works

Mineral dust is the thread between the ice, the water and life (`docs/PLAN.md`, phase 9): the balance is `Dust` (`src/engine/dust.js`, tested alone), wired in by `Aeolian` (`src/engine/aeolian.js`) inside the climate's cycle. Each of the six points of the year packs its sources after its winds and rain (`Climate.phase` calls `aeolian.phase(p, T)` after the moisture, while `climate.carry` is that point's carrying wind); after the six (and the zones' months) the balance is solved, on the engine's GPU in the page (`src/gpu/dust.js`, 240 Jacobi passes warm, 960 cold) and by Gauss–Seidel headless (`CPU_SWEEPS` 32 warm, ×4 cold, or to `CPU_TOL`), then routed (`route`: `finish` at each cell's albedo, then `effects`). Everything routed is the last cycle's.
- **The balance** (`dust.js`; standard forms, synthesis as built): GOCART's emission C S u²(u − u_t) as its expectation over gusts about the template's wind (a Rice distribution of speed, tabulated), two size bins (fine 30%, coarse 70%) carried by the moisture's wind and eddy mixing, settled (0.1 and 1 cm/s over a 3 km layer) and washed out (14% of the column per mm of rain), steady at each point of the year on level 6 at most (the engine's cells receive what they emitted, exactly); optical depth and a top-of-atmosphere forcing −20 τ (1 − α/0.45) W/m², cooling over dark ground and sea, warming over snow.
- **Sources** (synthesis): a land cell's strength is the union of three shares, × the open ground at that point of the year (no snow by the albedo's ramp, no lake water, no ice past 10 m) × its loose grains, 1 − e^{−h/5 cm} with h the regolith (bare rock gives nothing):
  - bare, dry ground: the cover's bare share (the zones' arid share before the cover is known) × a taper on the soil's relative water at that point of the year from 0.35 to 0.55 (the closure's months; GOCART's cutoff at a wetness of 0.5, softened), whose water also raises the threshold as GOCART's u_t0 (1.2 + 0.2 log₁₀ w) above w = 0.1 (from memory);
  - dry lake beds: the lakes' salt flats, weighted 3× (speculation: Earth's largest sources are dry lakes and closed basins' alluvium);
  - outwash plains: 1 − e^{−q/1 m/yr} where the ice's meltwater leaves it at q (`outwash`, through the regolith's update, eased over 1 kyr), whatever the soil's water.
  On the settled opening (level 7) ~18 M km² of source, 99.4% of the emission from bare, dry ground, 0.6% from salt flats, none from outwash.
- **The gusts** (speculation): the template's highs and lows have fixed depths, so a glacial would blow no harder; the gusts stand for the weather, so their rms (`DUST.GUST`, 2.5 m/s) scales with each hemisphere's equator-to-60° contrast at that point of the year against 26 K, the settled opening's (Eady's growth rate goes as the meridional gradient, standard), blended across the tropics: 2.5 m/s at the opening.
- **Calibration**: one constant, `DUST.C` = 6.5e-12 kg s² m⁻⁵ (GOCART's 1e-9 calibrated down: the template's winds are steadier and the source map broader than Ginoux's basins), at level 7 on the default seed's settled climate (it settles at the tenth cycle): 1,570 Tg/yr, a lifetime of 2.2 days (Earth's ~4, from memory; the template's drizzle washes it out fast), 61% washed out, mean τ 0.010, forcing −0.06 W/m²; 56% lands on land (most back on its sources), 44% at sea, 53% between the equator and 30° N on this seed. Before the climate settles the emission climbs with its cycles (the cover's bare share; see "How life works"); after, it holds within 1%.
- **Dusty snow and ice** (from memory, rough): the year's deposition over its snowfall is the dust in the snow (at least 20 mm of snow a year), which darkens the surface by 0.02 (c/100 ppm)^0.7, at most 0.2; the climate's planetary albedo sees half of it (as the cover's contrasts) on land snow, sea ice and the ice sheets (`climate.dustDark`, in `albedo`). The ice's degree-day factors rise by half the darkening over the clean surface's absorptance (snow's at α 0.8, ice's at 0.5: `ice.dustDDF`, on the GPU in the climate buffer's spare slots), so dirty margins melt faster. At the opening the snow holds ~13 ppm on average and ~500 near the deserts; the ice sheets darken by ~2e-4.
- **Iron** (Martin's hypothesis; the form synthesis): ~3.5% of dust is iron, ~2% of it soluble (from memory; ~9 Gmol/yr to the sea at the opening). The sea's cold box is its high-nutrient, low-chlorophyll water (it keeps a preformed ~0.8 of its phosphate), so the dust reaching it, against what reached it when the sea settled, raises its uptake as (1 + K) r/(r + K), K = 1 (`OC.in.iron`, `src/engine/oceancarbon.js`; the warm box strips its nutrients whatever the iron). An uptake 1.5× draws CO₂ down 10 ppm in 5 kyr (closed). The reference refreshes until the sea settles (`carbon.settled`, which waits for the climate's `settled`), so it is the settled climate's: on the default seed the factor holds within 1% of 1 over the cycles after.
- **Loess and the crust's budget**: the sources lose their net deflation (emission less what lands back) from their regolith, at most what it holds, and what was taken is laid down where the dust settled, in proportion: loess into the regolith on land, sediment on the sea's floor (`budget`, applied in the regolith's update with the ice's till; the crust's mass is kept to f32 rounding, tested). ~1,100 Tg/yr moves at the opening. The regolith's opening includes the deflation in its steady state (`deflation`), so the hottest sources start thin; where the wind outpaces weathering a source is held to its production by the loose-grain factor.
- **Drawn** (the Climate view's Air map): the year's mean load as an ochre stipple over land, sea and ice alike, denser where more is carried (16 px apart at ~0.01 g/m², 4 px at ~2). It rides the zones' last free slot (15). The key's Dust.

- **The ice-age survey** (`tools/iceage-survey.mjs`, level 6, GPU moisture and dust; after 24 cycles spun up with CO₂ held at the baseline, the sea, the cover and the references settling there; 800 kyr, analysed 200–800 kyr in 200 kyr windows). The summer's land temperature at 60–70° N answers the orbit at 0.06 K per W/m² of solstice insolation at 65° N with the whole orbit (0.01–0.02 through the 20 kyr window, which keeps 10% of precession and 64% of obliquity).   - **CO₂ and dust swing without the ice.** Through the whole orbit, dust swings ×2.1–3.0 with precession (45–55% of its variance at 19–24 kyr), through the cover's bare share (r 0.9: the monsoon's rain greening and browning the deserts), and with CO₂ free the iron it brings the cold sea moves CO₂ 30–70 ppm peak to peak in 200 kyr windows (sd 10–13 ppm; CO₂ follows the iron factor at r −0.73 to −0.81 ~9 kyr later); the ice's highest fifth holds 14–27 ppm less than its lowest, as precession's cold phases are the dusty ones. ## How the ocean and the weather work

- **Gyres** (`Ocean`, on the climate's coarse grid: the stress restricted, the SST anomaly from the energy balance's coarse mean, ψ and the anomaly published back on the surface's grid in `fine`; ~0.1 s a solve at 220 km, 1.1 s at 110 km): the depth-integrated flow under the annual-mean wind stress, steady, Stommel's form on the unit sphere, ε∇²ψ + ∂ψ/∂λ = a² curl τ/(2Ωρ₀), coasts at ψ = 0 (islands too), ε set for a two-cell western boundary layer. BiCGSTAB with a Jacobi preconditioner, warm-started, 120 iterations a cycle and the gyres only every third climate cycle (the basins change slowly). Surface currents u = k × ∇ψ/H (H = 400 m). The SST anomaly T′ solves upwinded u·∇(T + T′) + λT′ = κ∇²T′ + upwelling, where upwelling cools coasts the Ekman drift leaves. Moisture uses the SST (T + T′) for the sea's saturation, and sea ice forms on it (in the energy balance's albedo, its share of the year under ice, and the season drawn), so currents shape the ice edge: with the gyres at ~12 Sv the anomaly runs −6 to +1.7 K and the edge's spread in latitude went ±1.4° → ±2.0°; it stays near a latitude circle, as diffusive energy balances' ice edges do. Tested against Sverdrup's interior and the western boundary current in a box basin.
- **Weather** (`GpuWeather`, `src/gpu/weather.js`): two-layer QG on the GPU, on the drawn mesh (level 7, 55 km), its forcing refined from the engine's grid, L_d = 800 km. PV is carried by fluxes that are differences of ψ at Voronoi corners (exactly non-divergent), 20% upwinded, with ∇⁴ hyperviscosity scaled with the cell's 3.2 power (with the fourth power, the finer grid's noise outgrew it). Adams–Bashforth 2 at 200 s (800 s at level 5, halving with the cell: the advective limit with the upper layer's ~115 m/s jets; 300 s blew up on day 1). The upper layer starts with 0.7 of the climate's shear and the forcing builds the rest: starting with all of it, storms grew explosively and the transient's jets (~110 m/s on day 3) sat at the step's limit and sometimes passed it; with half of it, the storms took ~15 days to reach their equilibrium (the lower layer's vorticity at 30–70° rising to its range of 1.2–1.8 × 10⁻⁵/s, the upper jets to 75–110 m/s, as the relaxation's 15 days built the shear back), and with 0.7 they reach it in ~4, with no more than the run's own spread. The lower layer's state is q₂ less its orography, whose PV is carried alongside, so the ground can change under the weather. Inversion each step: the barotropic Poisson and baroclinic Helmholtz solves at once (a vec2) by one V-cycle of multigrid on levels 7 down to 2 (`src/gpu/hierarchy.js`: weighted Jacobi, two sweeps each way; restriction gathers a coarse cell's six midpoints; prolongation averages a midpoint's two parents; level 2 solved exactly by a precomputed inverse), warm-started. Each cycle cuts the error tenfold, and warm-started the error is the step's change in ψ, so one leaves a tenth of it (two cost 0.6 ms a step against 0.36, with the same statistics over 30 days and in the anomaly's fit). The barotropic solve screens at 1/k₀ ≈ 32,000 km, which fixes ψ's constant (a ~2% touch on the largest waves). Forcing: the baroclinic ψ relaxes over 15 days toward SHEAR · (f/f₀) · (T − T̄) from the climate's temperature at the season; Ekman friction on the lower layer; damping in the tropics in flux form, so PV's integral is conserved (to 1e-8 over 10 days). Tracers on the lower layer: θ relaxed to the climate's over 5 days, and moisture from the sea, raining past 90% of saturation. 35 dispatches and ~0.33 ms of GPU a step on an M5 Max (timestamps), ~145 ms a model day; the page gives it 12 ms of each frame, so it runs live to ~5 days a second. The spin isn't compiled in: Ω reaches the kernels through f₀ in their uniforms, and `setSpin` sets it with L_d and the solve's screening (the coarsest level's inverse recomputed), starting the weather again. A kernel writes the globe's weather fields (eddy pressure over the template, its zonal mean interpolated between 3° bands, rain, temperature, humidity, blended with the climate's mean) into the mesh's vertex buffer each frame, from the last two steps blended by how far the clock is into the next (ψ before the last step is kept for it), so at a slow dial the steps don't land a frame at a time. Tested on the device (`tests/gpu.html`): storms grow above Phillips' criterion and die below; PV is conserved; multigrid converges.
- **The chart** (`chart.js`, traced in `chart-worker.js` from the weather read back every ~120 ms and restricted to the engine's grid: tracing on the 55 km mesh cost ~650 ms a chart against ~100): sea-level pressure is the climate's template at the season plus the storms (ρfψ₂ less its zonal mean), smoothed, drawn as isobars at whole hPa (every 4, heavier every 20); rain as stipple. Counts on the grid's lines (smoothing passes, chain lengths, the rings of a high or low) scale with the level, so the chart reads alike at every resolution. Highs and lows are extremes of the pressure eased to 220 km over ~440 km, at least 1 hPa from their surroundings. Ends that nearly meet, meet (`joinEnds`): each end of a front, dry line or shear line moves onto the nearest end of another within ~320 km, or onto the nearest point of one within ~190 km, the last points tapering, so fronts' pieces, occlusions and their remnants, and dry and shear lines running into fronts join with a kink. On the page each chart is linked to the last (`linkCharts`: lines of a kind by nearest middle, centres within ~400 km) and drawn morphing from it over the time between charts (`chartFrame`), new lines fading in and old ones out. Fronts are the zero of ∇TFP·n̂ on θ where |∇θ| and TFP are in their upper ranges and the low-level flow is cyclonic (fronts lie in troughs), traced on triangles, chained, smoothed, typed by v·n̂ against 0.45 of the chart's median |v·n̂| (low-level flow runs nearly along the isotherms here, so an absolute threshold called every front stationary), then held in runs (a majority over ±8 points, runs under 10 points absorbed) with their symbols on one side per run. The locator's zero must be a maximum of TFP along n̂ (a minimum drew a second front beside each). Fronts are then split into stretches of one type; a stretch lying mostly within ~290 km of a longer one of its type is the same front found twice and goes; a cold stretch within ~380 km of a warm one has caught it, and the matched parts merge along their midline into an occluded front (mulberry, triangles and semicircles alternating on one side). Troughs (umber, dashed), ridges (umber zigzag) and subtropical shear lines (prussian, dash-dot) are ridge lines of −p, p and cyclonic vorticity, found by the Hessian's eigenvector (aligned per triangle), long and not round; troughs a front already marks aren't drawn. The fields (θ, moisture, wind, vorticity) and thresholds are averaged over successive charts, and pieces whose ends meet and line up are bridged, so fronts move rather than flicker. Dry lines are the same on the relative humidity the Humidity chart draws (the column's water over wsat(θ)), over land, from the top quarter of its gradients, where θ's gradient is under 1.25× the fronts' threshold, pieces bridged and thresholds averaged as fronts' are (on the column's water, the top 15% and no bridging, the chart found 0–1). The weather runs only in its view, waits for the climate's first cycle, spins up 5 days at twice its share of the frame (~0.9 s, the first chart ~1 s after the view opens; 12 days took ~3 s at 20–25 fps), and crossfades to the climate's mean between ~3 and ~30 days a second; where it is wholly the mean it waits, spun up.

## How the tides and the Moon work

- **The tide** (`Tides`, `src/engine/tides.js`; `GpuTides` in the page): linear shallow water on the rotating sphere on a C grid over the Voronoi cells (height on cells, the velocity normal to each edge between two sea cells, coasts closed), forced by the semidiurnal equilibrium tide of a Moon over the equator (cos²φ e^{2iλ}, ¾(m/M)(R/a)³R = 0.27 m today), with the body tide (α = 1 + k₂ − h₂ ≈ 0.69) and self-attraction and loading as β ≈ 0.9 on the sea's height (standard values, from memory). Coriolis acts on each edge through the other edges of its two cells, with weights from Perot's reconstruction of the cells' velocities antisymmetrized in the energy's norm (Σ ρHu²ℓd), so it does no work: TRiSK's linear form, ½(1 + H′/H) (Thuburn and others 2009; standard, from memory; on Perot's weights, synthesis). Perot's averaged rotation alone is antisymmetric only at uniform depth: where one cell's edges spanned a shelf and a deep sea it made energy (its worst mode gaining 3.6e-4/s against 2.6e-4/s of drag), and a deep cell beside a shelf grew without bound within 10–50 Myr of moving coasts (606 m, 1,500 TW, the spin through zero; since the tides' first commit). Heun's step for the rotation, forward–backward for the waves, drag implicit; on the GPU five dispatches a step, the corrector reading only the predictor's velocities. Drag: bottom friction C_D·U₀/H (0.0025 m/s) and internal-tide drag ½κh²N/H (3e-8 against the depth's step² between cells, capped at a quarter of the depth: a continental slope's whole drop as roughness walled the shelves off and spun up ~150 spurious amphidromes). Run to periodic (32 cycles cold, 5 warm: a basin mode rings for days) and the harmonic read over the last cycle; dissipation is the drag's work, 2ρrH⟨u²⟩ per edge's diamond. Tested: on a still global ocean the forced degree-2 response is analytic (within 1%); mass is kept to 1e-16; Coriolis's power vanishes to 1e-12 over a sea of cliffs; the GPU matches the CPU to 0.001% (0.41 s cold and 72 ms warm at level 6); `tests/moon-long.test.mjs` runs the worker 50 Myr at level 6 at the page's 1 Myr/s cadence (24 solves, 2.9–6.0 TW; the old Coriolis reached 19 TW and climbing).
- **At level 6 always** (the engine's level-6 cells, which nest first in a level-7 grid): shelves resolve at 110 km (at level 5 the lunar semidiurnal tide dissipated 1.5 TW, at level 6 4.2), and the Moon then recedes alike at every engine level. On the opening planet: median amplitude 0.28 m, a resonant gulf to ~6 m, ~20 deep-water amphidromes, 4.0 TW in the lunar semidiurnal tide at both engine levels (Earth's ~2.5; ~58% on shelves, as Earth's), ~5.8 TW in all, so this planet's Moon recedes ~6 cm/yr and its day lengthens ~3.8 ms a century (Earth's 3.8 cm/yr and ~2.1 ms, from memory). Over 40 Myr at 1 Myr/s the dissipation wanders 3–6 TW as the basins move and drifts down (the Moon receding ~5 cm/yr on average, the day ~3.2–3.5 ms a century; on the page 3.9–6.3 TW over 32 Myr). Its ocean is deeper than Earth's (median 5.6 km), which the solver takes as given; the constants are Earth's, not fitted to Earth's total.
- **The Moon** (`Moon`): each plate step, the lunar tides' torque slows the spin (C dΩ/dt) and recedes the Moon (L = μ√(GMa)); the solar tide slows the spin too. The lunar tides' dissipation is the semidiurnal's × 1.25 (the diurnal tides; from memory), the solar's (0.46)² of it. Between solves the tide keeps a constant time lag (standard: Mignard's and Hut's weak-friction tide): torque k(Ω − n), so dissipation ∝ (Ω − n)², k set by the last solve (k = P/(Ω − n)² there) and scaled with (R/a)⁶; holding the dissipation instead made the torque P/(Ω − n) unbounded toward the synchronous spin. The spin steps by backward Euler, so Ω′ is a weighted mean of Ω, n and n☉ and no step carries it past the synchronous spins or below zero, whatever the dissipation (at 10⁹ TW the system lands on the dual-synchronous state, a 47-day day with the Moon at 554,000 km, as Earth's would; tested); the orbit takes the torque's impulse whole, so angular momentum is kept exactly. A solve that comes back non-finite is dropped and the next starts cold (warm starts would carry it on); the worker re-solves every ~2 Myr (the coasts move) or when 2(Ω − n) has moved 0.3% (the basins' resonances move with it). With Earth's dissipation (3.7 TW) the Moon recedes 3.8 cm/yr and the day lengthens 2.1 ms a century (tested). `CLIMATE.OMEGA` follows the spin: the Hadley edge, the template's winds, the gyres' β and the weather (Ω and L_d ∝ 1/Ω set in its uniforms, `setSpin`; it starts again under the new spin when the Weather view opens if the spin has moved 0.3% since it started, ~7 Myr at today's tide). Headless (no GPU), the Moon stays put.
- **The Tides view** (`globe.js`, mode 5): the sea's tint by the semidiurnal amplitude (co-range), co-tidal lines every lunar hour drawn as the zeros of sin 6G from the harmonic's complex sixth power (no seam where the phase wraps), fading into each amphidrome, and a sienna stipple, its density the dissipation per area. Below ~6 h/s the tint is the sea's height now (the lunar and solar semidiurnal tides, so springs and neaps beat over 14.8 days) and a heavier line marks high water. The tide's clock is the sky's: its phase is 2(θ − the Moon's right ascension), so the bulge sits under the Moon. The record figure carries the day's length; Fig. 2 a Tides node (the basins in, the spin out to the climate, the ocean and the weather).
- **The sky** (`sky.js`, on the overlay outside the globe's disc): a seeded star field fixed in space, drawn through a perspective whose scale zoom doesn't change (stars at infinity), turning about the spin axis by the sidereal angle θ where a day takes a few seconds or more (below ~6 h/s), held still above; the Sun on the ecliptic at its longitude for the date (the orbit's calendar, at the orbit's obliquity), shown below ~0.25 yr/s; the Moon at its longitude (advancing at its mean motion) shown below ~5 days/s, lit toward the Sun (an almanac's ○ full and ● new), both at their true sizes ×5 so a nearer Moon looks larger; in the People scenario the people's city on the Moon (below). Ink on the paper; the dark theme's paper is the night. Sun and Moon don't light the globe (its hillshade keeps the cartographic light).

## How the year works

`Year` (`src/engine/year.js`) is stepped from `Planet.fast` (so the worker and `Planet.run` both run it), with the dial's rate: the worker's ticks carry it, and headless a fast step stands for a tenth of a second.
- **The stepper** (synthesis). Whole months, each step computed when the clock enters it, to its end, so the page draws the state between the step's two ends by its own clock. A step is a month while a month takes at least ~1/30 s of the dial, then longer (4 months at 10 yr/s, 40 at 100), at most 30 a second; after a jump of up to 20 years (`JUMP`) it covers the gap in one, and a longer jump (a plate step by `.` at a slow dial, a stall) is the closure's whatever the dial (it had made one step of 6 million months, the ground looping each). `live` = 1 − smoothstep over log₁₀ rate 1.75 → 2.25 (56–180 yr/s): consumers weigh the live state by it (the season's anomaly, the rivers', the record's, the veil's marks). Outrun, nothing steps: the veil decays with the clock (no eruptions), the ground drops its live state, and back from it each cell takes the OU's exact transition over the gap (`resume`: the anomaly decays by e^{−BΔ/C} and the noise refills the closure's spread; the tag's response decays alike).
- **The ground's hook.** `year.soil` (the `Ground`), when set, is called once a step while live: `soil.step({ years, m0, months, live, dT, rf })`, the step's first calendar month and its length, and the anomaly on the engine's grid held over the step (`fineAnomaly`: T′ in K and the rain's factor, both weighted by `live`; ~1.6 ms a step at level 7), and `soil.closure({ years, live })` each advance while `live` < 1. (The month-by-month hooks it replaced built each month's inputs on the CPU, ~2 ms a month at level 7, 1.3 s a second at 56 yr/s; the lakes are slower and step from the planet, see "How the ground and the lakes work".)
- **The anomaly** (standard: Hasselmann; North's diffusive form): C ∂T′/∂t = −B T′ + ∇·D∇T′ + F on a 440 km grid (level 4, 2,562 cells), C from the climate's land and sea ice (restricted), B the energy balance's, D = 0.03. F is the weather's heat flux as noise, white at the month and correlated over 1,500 km (a Gaussian kernel over level-3 nodes), plus the veil's forcing less the eruption rate's mean. The veil's is exact in time (`forced`): each source (the veil at the step's start, each eruption from its moment) decays at the veil's rate, its response is the convolution of two exponentials, and it enters as the forcing that, held over the step, gives that response (against the analytic one, within 0.3% at steps of 1 to 60 months; averaged at the step's two ends, 3-year steps had cooled twice as much). Each step is each cell's exact OU transition, then the diffusion by preconditioned CG (16 iterations; 0.6 ms a month at level 7, 1.3 ms for 40). Its amplitude by latitude (`YEAR.Q`) is fitted to the QG weather's monthly-mean θ over land (`tools/anomaly-fit.mjs`, two weathers of 240 days in January and July, to a fixed point): 1.0 K at 20–30°, 2.0 K at 40–60°, 1.6 K at 60–70°, 0.9 K poleward. The QG weather damps the tropics (its 0.09 K at 0–10° and 0.34 K at 10–20° are the model's, not the planet's), so only bands from 20° are fitted, the nodes within 20° held at 20°'s, and the tropics' spread follows from their heat capacity (0.9–1.0 K on land; Earth's tropical land ~0.5–1 K, from memory); the pole's node is held at 80°'s, so seven bands set seven nodes. Refitted 2026-11-15: after the climate's and the plates' retunes the old fit had the model's land spread at 1.5–3× the weather's at 0–30° and 0.6–0.8× at 50–80°. Sea anomalies last C/B ≈ 3.8 yr, land ones weeks; land's monthly spread is ~2.6× the sea's.
- **The rain's anomaly** (synthesis): z = ρ T′/σ + √(1 − ρ²) η per cell, ρ −0.3 over land and +0.3 at sea (from memory), η its own noise; a cell's monthly log rain spreads by 0.45 (MAP/1000 mm)^−0.3. It scales the ground's rain, and through the ground the rivers' discharge and the lakes' inflow (mean-preserving, before the ground's live state: the climate's runoff by runoff's elasticity of 2). Held over a step of k months, η stands for the step's mean, its spread 1/√k of a month's, and the factor's mean-keeping term takes z's variance ρ² + (1 − ρ²)/k (without it, a year's step lost ~6% of the rain).
- **The closure**: each cell's stationary spread, the local OU's q/√(2(B + D·40)C̃) with C̃ the heat capacity smoothed over six passes and blended 0.7 into the cell's (fitted to the live model: land within ~7% rms on three seeds), and the share a year's mean keeps. The zones read it at every dial.
- **Volcanoes** (`src/engine/volcanoes.js`; standard effects, synthesis as a process): a seeded Poisson process, sizes N(>M) ∝ M⁻¹ in Pinatubos (each decade ten times rarer). Each source's arrivals are unit exponentials of its own hazard (rate × time) carried across steps, so a changing rate is taken step by step and the closure, which doesn't erupt, neither stores arrivals nor replays them; each eruption is injected at its moment within the step. Arcs: from 0.1 to 30, ≥ 0.1 every 10 yr at the opening's convergence (`carbon.arc / carbon.arc0`), placed by the plates' arc memory (`tec.arc`); hotspots: ≥ 0.1 every 400 yr each, to 3; an erupting province: ≥ 1 every 60 yr, to 30. The veil is optical depth in 5° bands: 0.15 global per Pinatubo (from memory), injected over ±4°, spread in latitude (0.5 rad²/yr) and decaying over 1 yr; forcing −25 W/m² per unit optical depth everywhere (from memory). The rate's mean forcing (−0.3 W/m² at the opening) against the opening's is `CLIMATE.F_VOLC`, in the energy balance at every dial: zero at the opening, whose mean is in the climate's tuning; a flood adds its pulses' mean.
- **Drawn.** The anomaly shifts the season's temperature on the GPU (`src/gpu/season.js`, between the step's two ends), so the snow line and the sea ice's edge move from year to year; the rivers by the month carry their routed rain anomaly (a factor per cell of each river's chain); the record's temperature carries the global mean. An eruption is a red triangle (the map's volcano, the mantle at the surface), sized by its sulfate, for 1.5 s once the clock passes it (Relief and Climate); the veil is graphite hatching along the circles of latitude in the Air map, 16 px apart at τ 0.02 to 4 px at τ 1, below ~10 yr/s. Fig. 2 has Year (the anomaly's global mean, or its spread when outrun) and Volcanoes (the veil's optical depth): plates and plumes erupt, the veil forces the year (live) and the climate (its mean), the climate sets the year's heat capacity, and the year's spread is the zones' widths.

## How the ground and the lakes work

- **The ground** (`src/engine/soil.js`; standard: Manabe's bucket; synthesis around it): soil water to a capacity (150 mm on land, none at sea or under grounded ice, which owns its water; when it is cut, the water past it runs off as the next month's quick flow, on the CPU and the GPU alike), evaporating at the potential times β = min(1, S/0.75 S_max), overflowing from its saturated share w⁴ (HBV's and the Arno scheme's), draining at 0.4 S_max w⁴ a month; the snowpack by degree days on the month's spread of days (as the ice's), shutting evaporation off; one ground-heat store, insulated by snow, whose annual mean below 0 °C is permafrost (its share of a cell a logistic over `PERMA_W` 1 K, the spread of the ground's mean within a cell, σ ≈ 1.7 K by the zones' σ/1.7, synthesis: Earth's zones by that share, continuous, discontinuous, sporadic and isolated, hold ~47/19/17/17% of its permafrost region, from memory, and 1 K gives the opening 43/18/19/20 where 0.5 K gave 63/13/12/13; on the opening planet (level 6) 6.6% of land, 4.6% continuous; nothing reads the share but the drawing); and groundwater, a linear store the drainage recharges and that empties over 4 months (synthesis), whose outflow is the baseflow. Potential evaporation is Budyko's scale by the month, 55/12 (T + 4) mm. Its periodic year under the climate's months is the climate's closure (`Climate.groundYear`, each cycle, warm, ~2 years a cell; ~40 ms at level 7 in eight slices): the climate's runoff by month and in the year, the lakes' open-water balance (rain less potential evaporation), and β at each of the moisture's six points of the year (snow cover included). On the opening planet land's runoff is 49% of its rain; the cold and permafrost cells run ~15% below Budyko's (its floor), the rest within ~1%. The rivers' months swing a median 32× in a year (with the drainage straight to the rivers, 750×; `monthlyRunoff` had 8×, with a fixed third of the runoff as baseflow).
- **Live** (`src/engine/ground.js`, `Ground`, the year's `soil`): stepped by the year's steps on the engine's GPU (`src/gpu/soil.js`: the climate's months resident, uploaded once a cycle (~5 ms at level 7); each step uploads the anomaly alone, held over the step, and dispatches its months, ~0.01 ms a month of GPU; the last twelve months are recorded by calendar month) or headless on the CPU (`cpuLive`; ~13 ms a month at level 7, so the worker doesn't). Its calendar runs on from the handover month by month, never skipping one. Handover from the closure: the periodic year's state at the step's first month, then a year of the climate's months alone, which repeats the periodic year (tested to 0.05 mm a month on the GPU) and fills the record; outrun (`live` = 0), the live state is dropped. The worker reads back the last twelve months' aggregates, the rivers' month's runoff and the bank (below) ~10 times a second (2–4 ms), headless once a year of the clock; each handover and each drop is a new epoch, and a read from an earlier one is discarded. The bank is the water the live ground has let go, mm per cell over the months it stepped (summed on the GPU and emptied by each read), with the span of the clock it covers. Consumers weigh the live state by `live`: the lakes' runoff, Fig. 2, and the climate's β, by the live slow mean (5 years) against the closure's (at most 3×), so a drought's dry soil evaporates less into the next cycle.
- **Rivers by the month** run on the live ground's runoff that month where it has one (else the climate's under the rain's anomaly), routed down the rivers' drainage: on the opening planet (level 6, 30 yr/s) their annual cycle lags the catchment's rain by a median 0.20 months (`monthlyRunoff`'s: −0.02), 21% of them by half a month or more (11%), 27 of 243 peak a month after the rain's peak (2 of 249).
- **Lakes** (`src/engine/lakes.js`; standard hydrology; synthesis): routed on the smooth surface, the ground plus its ice (so ice dams them, and ice holds none), not the landscape's rough one (whose roughness makes one-cell ponds), to the ocean (the plates' mask: a basin below sea level the sea doesn't reach is a depression, a Caspian or a Dead Sea where its balance leaves it partly dry), and rebuilt after each landscape step or when the sea has left or reached a basin (at most every 0.5 s of the wall clock; a basin the sea left comes with its water, `extra`) and when the ice has moved 3.5e-5 km·sr of water (at most every 100 years of the clock and 1 s of the wall clock; a rebuild costs ~20 ms at level 7, and at the opening the ice moved that much every other frame). Each rebuild carries the water over (an ice dam's loss releases what it held, exactly). The planet steps them each fast step (`stepLakes`: backward Euler, at most 20 times a second of the dial; 0.13 ms a step at level 7) below 10⁴ yr/s: where the globe shows the season (below `LAKES_SEASON`, 1.4 yr/s) a month a step at most, under that month's rain and potential evaporation on open water (`monthlyPE`, on the depressions' cells), so a closed lake fills in its wet season and shrinks in its dry and a spilling one can sink below its sill; above it a year at least, under the year's, so the drawn lakes don't flicker through seasons the globe doesn't show (with quarter-year steps they had) and sets them at their balance above (`equilibrium`, 0.5 ms; down the depression hierarchy each node's smaller child is solved first and the other with its overflow, so each basin once: solving both and one again took 2^depth, and a 78-deep chain of nested basins hung the engine), and at the opening at their balance with each climate cycle until the climate has settled (the first, from a cold start, holds half the lakes the third does). Each cell's ground below the grid's scale spans [z, z + ramp], its wet share rising linearly with the level, and the ramp is the cell's relief, its mean height difference to its neighbours, within 5–50 m (`RAMP_MIN`, `RAMP`): a fixed 50 m had drawn a closed lake's seasons as ~1% of its area, and on the default seed (level 6) lake shores' relief runs 6–18 m at the 10th–50th percentile. Over two years at 0.5 yr/s (level 6) the lakes then close and open with the seasons (18–26 closed through the year, against a steady count with quarter-year steps), the closed ones swing up to ~7% in area (a 118k km² lake 5%), and dry-season salt rings their shores; most of the area is spilling or steep-sided lakes that barely move, as Earth's large lakes don't (the big seasonal swings, a Tonle Sap's or a Poyang's, are floodplains and rivers' backflow, not built). What they hold is off the sea's (`Planet.syncLakes`, `tec.lakeWater`): at the settled opening (level 7) ~1.15 M km³, the sea 3.2 m lower for it; sea level is measured from there (`Planet.zeroSea`, once the opening's last cycle has its lakes at their balance; the page's record moves with it). Forcing: the ground's runoff (the closure's, and by `live` the live ground's bank, of which each step takes the share up to the clock, so the lakes receive the volumes the soil let go; at their balance the live last twelve months', which carry the year's anomaly with its own statistics; read as a rate over the lakes' own steps, the last twelve months' had lost ~16% of the soil's runoff into the lakes' catchments) and the climate's rain less potential evaporation on open water (no live anomaly on the lakes' own surface: held over a step, a month's anomaly would stand for a season's). The climate's next cycle reads the lakes' wet share over about the last year (`wetY`, snapshot at its start) for evaporation and albedo (the month's, at a slow dial, kept the opening from settling until its 16th cycle), and a lake's level for its share of the cell's height (`Climate.geography`, relaxed halfway each cycle: a lake 3 km deep in a 6.7 km pit, read on the floor, was ~20 K too warm and evaporated at the saturation's cap, and unrelaxed, a closed lake's level and its air's warmth rang through the opening's cycles). On the opening planet at level 7 the lakes cover ~2.0 M km² (~90 lakes, ~20 closed; before the basins and the floors, ~0.4 M km² and ~70 lakes); once level 7's landscape steps (6 Myr) ~1,800–1,950 lakes cover 5.4–5.7 M km² holding 1.1–2.7 M km³ (before, the same count over 2.2–2.8 M km² holding ~0.1 M km³).
- **Rivers and lakes** (`src/render/rivers.js`, `drainage`): the rivers' drainage is the landscape's, except that in a closed lake's depression each cell drains to its lowest neighbour in the depression (on the lakes' surface) and the lake's cells are sinks, and where it leaves a spilling lake's depression it carries the lake's outflow against its inflow, both over about the last year (`regInY`/`regOutY`; a region spills that passes on more than a hundredth of its inflow: the rivers are traced from the year's discharge, and by the last month's step a lake below its sill in the dry season read as closed, so the drained rivers lost ~10% of what reached the coast). So a river below a closed lake is dry, and below a spilling one carries the overflow; over a year the drained rivers reach the coast with the direct runoff and the lakes' overflow within ~1% (the two routings' catchments differ). The rivers retrace when a lake opens or closes over its year; the landscape's erosion keeps its own Q (through the lakes, over its rough routing).

## How the people work

The model is `src/engine/people.js` (its header and `docs/PLAN.md`, Roadmap 2.0, hold the design and the record of its runs); headless it runs on a cached opening (`tools/people-run.mjs`) or beside a live planet (`LIVE=1`, `tools/people/couple.mjs`). On the page it is the People scenario:
- **Three threads.** The page starts the people's worker (`src/engine/people-worker.js`) and hands it and the engine's worker the two ends of a `MessageChannel`. The people run at level 6 whatever the engine's level (their constants are fitted there; a level-7 step would cost four times as much). After each climate cycle the engine sends their fields (`fieldsOf` restricted by `src/grid/transfer.js`: area-weighted, the rivers' discharge and the lakes' share by the most within a cell) and a few times a second the air's CO₂ and the regolith; the people send back ~10 times a second their farmed shares and tillage a cell, the carbon burned less what capture took back, their forcing and the sea's boiled share, and the engine applies them (`PeopleLink`, prolonged to its grid) around each fast step: the carbon pulsed (drawn down no lower than 100 ppm), tillage added to the erosion field the regolith's step reads, land use on the cover's tree balance as each cycle sets it (`Life.onCover`), the forcing as `CLIMATE.F_PEOPLE` (beside `F_VOLC` in both energy balances: the year resets `F_VOLC` each step), and `Tectonics.boiled` (the sea's water less what's boiled, `seaWater()`, on the CPU's and the GPU's sea level). The headless coupler uses the same link.
- **The clock.** The page holds the planet at its opening while it loads (no ticks: the engine's climate runs its cycles back to back) and starts the people's thread once the climate has settled (`climate.stats.settled`, ~cycle 9, ~8 s after load, so the cover, the lakes and the sea are at their balance first; a9: "the scenario should start when hominids evolve"); the first hominins are placed on the first fields that come, and the clock runs from their first report; their rates are re-read with each cycle. Their first report opens the People view (unless the reader has picked a view) and shows its button, Fig. 8's tab and the follow dial's switch. Each tick the page sends the engine it also sends the people; they owe those years and step at their own pace (`People.nextDt`), a step shorter than it would take only once the owed years have waited 50 ms, and report what they still owe; the page holds its clock while they're owed more than a quarter second's worth.
- **The follow dial.** While it holds the dial, the rate is set so a doubling or halving of what changes fastest takes 10 seconds (`follow.secs`, a9; `paceOf`: over the last 20 steps, each quantity from its first entry above its floor within them; the minds, the farmed land from 10³ km², the energy from 1 GW and AI's labour from 10⁹ worker-equivalents, each revolution's own growth, and from early takeoff the people on Earth and off it), within the dial's range and what the people keep up with (a step's years over its cost, on 80% of their thread), and before the first tip at least 5 kyr/s, eased over 0.6 s apart from the dial's input (which snaps to its step). With the minds alone each revolution got only the minds' few doublings and the first hominins' spread (doubling every ~35 years) most of the first minute. On the page (earthlike, headless Chrome, 3 s a doubling, v2.4): load to farming ~40 s, farming to industry ~16, industry to early takeoff ~30, early takeoff to the dial ~15–20; `tools/people-run.mjs`'s estimate (the page's thread at ~80 kyr/s) gives hominins ~28 s, foragers 32–63, farming ~30, industry ~50, early takeoff to the dial 33–48 on seeds `b` and `c` (before v2.3: 56–65, 3–7, 19–27, 22–26, 8–9). Taking the dial by hand (or `[`/`]`) ends it; Follow beside the dial (or F) starts it again, except in the Weather group, whose range it would leave.
- **Crossing the sea** (synthesis). The graph's sea edges are every crossing of up to `HOPS` (6) sea cells from each coast, and past them each group of landmasses those join to its `K_LONG` (4) nearest other groups across open sea, to `LONG_HOPS` (40, ~4,400 km at level 6), then in Borůvka's rounds whatever is still apart: before, a landmass 7 cells off was never reached (a9's first run reached space without settling its Australia), and with each group's one nearest alone a 10 M km² continent whose nearest was an islet nobody settled went unreached to takeoff (a9's fourth play; the page reached 90.5% of the land by takeoff then, 95.6% now, the rest islands under 0.7 M km²). Across the sea the spread is a flux only between coasts both settled; an empty shore is settled by a voyage (`voyages`), a founding party of `FOUNDERS` (50) at a rate a year of `S_SEA` (1e-3) × the departure's crowding × e^{z/Z_V} × e^{−km/reach} per crossing, reach = `L_SEA0` + `L_SEA` z (40 + 400 z km: rafts, then boats). A continuous flux, however faint, had seeded every shore within reach the moment boats existed (the people grow from a millionth of a person), so the distance didn't count. Farming and industry spread across a crossing by e^{−km/reach} too. Cold opens faster with culture (`K_COLD` 12: −10 °C winters at the tip, −22 at z 2; the Upper Palaeolithic on the mammoth steppe and in Beringia, from memory): with 5, the Beringia-like crossings (−15 to −30 °C at both ends) waited for industry's culture. On the cached openings (`tools/people/settle.mjs`): earthlike's continents are first settled 0.1–1.75 Myr against farming's 1.82, b's and c's within a few kyr of farming (their gates are cold straits and stepping-stone islets), remote islands (5–17 sea cells) in the farming era, everything habitable by industry; early takeoff then has 8.4–11 B people (earthlike 11.1: the cold land), 25–29 TW, 425–445 Pg C burned, 241–289 years after industry.
- **Where people and machines are** (`places`, synthesis; drawn only, so the dynamics and calibration don't see it). Cities follow their catchments over `T_CITY` (100 years), so they persist where they began. Industry's footprint follows its workers (the drawn people × industry's share) over `T_MACH` (30 years) while Earth has people, and holds when it has none; factories (output's, food's and capture's power), datacenters (compute's) and the robots at work (the physical work the AI drives, `Ap`, in worker-equivalents) are the world's by Earth's share of the energy, spread on the footprint (datacenters toward the cities, robots onto the farms too) with a floor over the machines' land: living land people have reached, and the land the people's cells don't hold ("Fields and the land"); the floor is `MACH_FLOOR` of the power, or as much as the machines would need of all their land at their densities (a spent Earth's heat budget, ~500 PW, is more than factories at 30 W/m² could cover the planet with). Before (a9's fourth play) the floor covered all living land, and factories stood on a continent no one had reached. Solar farms are the model's ("Solar farms"). **Land use is exclusive** (a9): each use takes the land its footprint density gives, claimed in order (cities `D_URB` 4,000 residents a km²; solar farms, the model's; factories `D_FAC` 30 W/m² and datacenters `D_DC` 500 W/m² together, packing denser where the room runs out, factories first, to a common density that fits, so a full cell splits by power; then the fields as far as room is left, so factories still crowd them out past the dial, which a9 likes; densities from memory), together at most the cell. A cell people have never reached but their machines stand on counts as reached on the Settlement map. Datacenters stay small: 0.14 TW of compute at early takeoff is ~280 km² for the planet and ~2,000 km² at the dial (less than a tessera; Earth's ~50 GW of datacenters, from memory, is a few hundred km²), and past the dial compute is a few percent of the machines' power (most goes into making things), so ~0.7 M km² a year past it and ~3.5 M ten years past (earthlike, 2a). ## How the runaway works

`Runaway` (`src/engine/runaway.js`), on the planet beside the carbon; standard physics, synthesis as built.
- **The limit.** A moist column's outgoing longwave saturates as its vapour grows opaque, toward the Simpson–Nakajima limit, ~282 W/m² (Goldblatt and others 2013, from memory). The energy balance's OLR is A + BT; its global mean leaves that line at `T_K` (25 °C, sea-level mean) along a parabola, C¹ at both ends, reaching the limit at the plateau (~48 °C at A, B as tuned). The departure enters both energy balances as a uniform forcing, `CLIMATE.F_OLR` (`Climate.olrCorrection` before each step, from the last step's mean absorbed sunlight, which the annual mean's global balance gives exactly: `olrMeasure`; the GPU rewrites its forcing when it changes, `writeForcing`). A uniform forcing shifts the annual mean uniformly, so the pattern stays the diffusive balance's: the closure is the global mean's (the moist tropics would saturate first; the transport exports their heat). Below `T_K` the correction is zero, so the opening and every tuning are untouched.
- **Past it.** With the sunlight absorbed and the forcing H at or past the limit, the excess N = H − L_RUN boils the sea (N × the Earth's area over E_BOIL a year, 3.7 × 10²⁷ J for all of it; `step`, each fast step), and the energy balance's mean is set to the steam's surface: the saturation temperature at the vapour's pressure (Clausius–Clapeyron from 100 °C at 1 bar, within ~12 K of the steam tables up to the critical point at 221 bar; the ocean as vapour is 270 bar), then rising linearly to `T_POST` (1,227 °C, ~1,500 K, Goldblatt and others, from memory) as the rest goes supercritical, and held there once it's all in the air. N < 0 rains the steam back at the same rate (hydrogen's escape isn't counted). `Tectonics.boiled` is the share in the air; the sea re-solved each thousandth and each plate step under steam.
- **What else knows.** Once all but the sea's last hundredth has boiled, the air alone carries what the volcanoes and the land give it (`OceanCarbon.step`). The boiled water's dissolved carbon degasses to the air and its alkalinity and phosphate stay as salts (`OceanCarbon.boilTo`: the boxes and their gas exchange shrink with the water, so what's left keeps its chemistry; the last hundredth stays). Weathering, silicate and carbonate, needs liquid water on the ground (`in.wet`, falling from 1 at a 60 °C mean to 0 at 100 °C; before it, weathering's map frozen at the hot, wet onset drew a 1,226 °C planet's CO₂ to 15 ppm). The plants' cover fails past an annual mean of 38–46 °C (`VEG.T_HOT`; Lieth's NPP rises with warmth without limit). The moisture's saturation was already clamped at 45 °C on both paths, and the regolith's production reads at most 45 °C. Drawn: a pale wash over the globe, its opacity 0.8 (1 − e^{−p/30 bar}) (Venus's surface is hidden under 92 bar; this one keeps a little ground showing); the captions' steam and the share of the sea in the air; Fig. 2's climate → sea level.
- **Measured.** Headless (level 5, earthlike, the people's forcing held): +20 W/m² settles at 25.5 °C, +35 at 36 °C, +45 at the limit (47 °C, N ≈ 0.1: the cover's die-off brightening the land held it there); +1,000 W/m² boils ~4% of the sea a decade, the surface 184 °C at 11 bar and 265 °C at 56 bar. On the page (ending 1a): 36% boiled at +93 years (102 bar, 281 °C), all of it by ~+240, the surface at 1,226 °C under 270 bar.

Both scenarios run at 1 Myr/s or slower. The climate runs alongside as an async loop, a 10 ms slice of its cycle at a time (once settled, a new cycle waits for 30 years of the clock or 4 s unless a plate step asks for one: at 20 yr/s its cycles back to back had kept the thread ~65% busy for inputs that barely moved, `CYCLE_YEARS`) (every stage yields within ~10–15 ms at level 7), between slices waiting on a timer for the next one, or for the pump to end (spinning through macrotasks for it kept the thread busy and made a channel each), awaiting the GPU where the cycle yields a promise (`solveAsync` is the headless form), then what follows a cycle (`Planet.afterClimateSteps`) the same way, before the `climate` message. It posts `surface` after steps or when the sea moves (the drawn fields, from `src/gpu/surface.js`, the boundaries and rivers traced, the plates' rotations, the crust's statistics), `climate` after each cycle (harmonics, zones, ocean, the export production, the dust's load and statistics, the permafrost's share as drawn), `tides` after each solve of the tide (the harmonic and the dissipation per unit equilibrium tide, prolonged to the engine's grid), `year` after each of the year's steps (the anomaly at the step's end on the engine's grid, the step's two ends, the veil's bands, the eruptions), `lakes` when they've changed, at most four times a second (`lakeFields`: the drawn depth and salt, ~3 ms at level 7), `cover` when the plants' cover has changed, at most twice a second (eight colour weights a cell, ~6.6 ms at level 7), and `fast` ~10 times a second (with the Moon's distance, the spin and the dissipation) (ice, with its flow as drawn: the direction as long as sliding's share; carbon, plumes, the regolith's and the crust's flexure's statistics, the year's rates, forcing and tagged eruption, the rivers' anomaly, the record's samples, the model figure's sums). Ticks carry the dial's rate, which the year's live weight reads. The page's mirrors (`mirror.js`, and plain objects in `main.js`) have the engine's shapes, so drawing and figures read them unchanged. Experiments' parameters go over as a `params` message; `CARBON.FIXED: 'now'` holds CO₂ where it stands.
- **The shown moment.** Surfaces queue on the page as they arrive (irregularly: a landscape step makes some slower), and the globe shows a moment τ that follows them a step and a half behind at the engine's pace (measured over the last 3 s of arrivals), nudged ±50% by how far it is from that aim and eased over 0.3 s; it eases into the newest when nothing waits. Between the two surfaces either side of τ the globe blends (`setFields` modes: step, reset, refresh), boundaries turn with their plates to τ and morph, and rivers morph over the landscape's step.
- **The plates on the GPU** (`src/gpu/plates.js`, from `Tectonics.stepAsync` through `Planet.plateStepAsync`, which runs the plumes first so their crust and swells go up with the step). Its sea level fills every cell below it; the CPU then solves the ocean's over its mask (`solveSea`, "How the plates work"). The CPU's plate frames stay authoritative: the landscape and the plumes write them through (owner, ref), and merges, rifts and initiation rewrite whole plates on the CPU; a piece made a plate goes up whole, and a joined piece's frame cells go up one by one (`dirty.cells`, the `patch` kernel). The GPU keeps a mirror of every frame (crust as h, φ, formation and suture time per frame cell; masks as a bit per plate per frame cell) and the world's memories (slabs, arcs, collision fronts). Each step sends up the top cells' h and φ as the CPU's frames hold them, the rotations, the ice's load and the swells. 36 dispatches follow at level 7: two decide passes, the slabs' chain, writes, relaxation, sampling, decay, elevation, the kinematics, the sea level (one workgroup's Newton), foundering and the per-plate sums. One read back brings down owner and ref, the crust on top, elevation, the sea level and land, the sums (ω is solved on the CPU in f64), counters, collision tallies, and every frame cell the step changed, as edits applied to the CPU's frames. The memories come down only before a merge or an initiation; afterwards the touched plates, owners and memories go back up. A frame cell two world cells reach is written once, by the lowest index among neighbours. The step isn't repeatable run to run, though (found 2026-11-15, and already so before that day's changes): from one state, three runs agree after one step, differ in elevation (not owners) after the second, and in owners by the fifth, so a seed reproduces the opening but not the page's run after it; the cause (a float reduction's order, or a race in a pass that feeds the elevation) isn't found yet. One step from one state agrees with the CPU's at 99.99% of cells; over 40–60 Myr the GPU's statistics differ from the CPU's no more than a CPU nudged by 3e-3 in ω (tests/gpu-plates.test.mjs). The pump awaits the step, one pump at a time; while pumps run back to back the climate takes a 10 ms slice every 100 ms (it may fill a pump's wait), and between pumps it runs freely. The surface’s fields are prepared on the device too (`src/gpu/surface.js`, from the engine’s arrays uploaded each time, so it doesn’t matter which side last stepped them); the lakes’ fields stay on the CPU (`lakeFields`, posted only when the lakes change).
- **The GPU** (one device, `src/gpu/device.js`): the globe draws; the weather steps and writes the globe's weather buffer; the season writes its climate buffer. Kernels declare only the bindings their entry uses (`kernel()` binds by name under the 'auto' layout), at most eight storage buffers each.
- **The engine's device is busy at fast dials.** At level 7 at the dial's top each tick's ice advance (up to 48 steps) keeps it working ~16 ms (measured in node, `onSubmittedWorkDone` after the submit), and every readback the pump awaits queues behind that: the plates' step waits ~4–7 ms more than alone, the crust's flexure likewise, and a 1.8 ms kernel took ~10. A second device doesn't help (the hardware is shared: 3.8 ms of wait). So a port pays only where its work outweighs a readback's wait, or where nothing waits for it. The landscape, the lakes' routing and the tracing of lines and rivers stay on the worker's CPU (phase 10b, "How the landscape works").
- **The chart's worker** (`src/render/chart-worker.js`) gets ψ₂, θ and moisture read back from the GPU with the template's pressure and derives wind, vorticity and the eddy pressure before tracing; one read and one trace are in flight at a time.

## Drawing the climate

- **The coast** (`coastField` in `src/render/surface.js`, and `src/gpu/surface.js`): the globe draws the sea where a field signed like the eased relief is negative, the relief itself except at and beside a basin below sea level that the sea doesn't reach, where it is eased again with the basin's floor 5 m above the sea, so the basin draws as land however low it lies, its relief and hillshade its own (the vertex field's fourth slot, which had carried an unused lake depth); and land at or beside a lake (a cell a quarter wet or more, now or in the lakes' ~3 kyr memory: `lakeHeld`) stands at least 5 m above it. The eased relief draws a low cell beside deep sea as sea, as it does ~40% of coastal land cells, and where that cell was a lake's rim the lake met the sea it doesn't reach (27 of 94 rims at 94 Myr on the default seed). The rivers' mouths and the normals' exaggeration read it too.
- **Relief view** (the Relief group's Surface map). Land takes the plants' cover: trees, grass and bare ground, split by warmth into eight classes (`COLOURS` in `vegetation.js`: tropical, temperate and boreal forest, savanna, steppe, tundra, desert, rock) drawn in the biomes' natural colours (`biome` in the shader), blended in the vertex shader from a storage buffer of eight weights a vertex (a ninth vertex buffer is past the limit; refined like the zones), with rock toward the summits; umber height alone until the climate has run. The cover lags its climate, so after a deglaciation bare ground and grass come before the forest, and the Climate view (the climate) and the Relief view (the plants) can disagree. Snow and sea ice are a light fill with a hard edge (the contour of the snow-and-ice share at ½, antialiased by its derivative) and a hairline, the land's snow on land and the sea's ice at sea, each up to the coast (see "Snow and ice"), ice sheets the same with a stronger outline and contours of the ice's surface every 500 m. Lakes are drawn from their water (`lakeFields`): a smoothed field signed so its zero is where a cell is half under water (+50 m-equivalent on water, −50 on dry land, a water cell kept at least a tenth of that above zero after the smoothing so a lake of two or three cells still draws, and a rim beside the sea held dry), with a hard shore and its hairline, from two cells up (one cell would draw as a hexagon), their surface shaded as the sea's (the bed's relief at a third: at the land's full exaggeration a lake's bed drew as dark blobs under its water and grey ones under its ice), frozen as ice on water ("Snow and ice"), and a tint by √(depth / 6 km) carried apart (the depth in the shore's field had carried a deep lake's water a cell or more past its shore: the opening's lakes drew at 1.2–1.7 times their wet area on three seeds, over 64–100 dry cells, 5–15 of them across a rim into the sea; now 0.95–0.99 and none), eased over 0.4 s when they change (in their own vertex attributes, packed into the tide's buffer: a pipeline takes eight vertex buffers; eased in the vertex shader, which leaves their varying room for the sea's ice and the tint). Salt flats, the share of a cell a closed lake has dried from over the last ~3 kyr (at their balance, also the fringe a 50% wetter year would flood), are a pale crust under short graphite strokes (6.5 px, 0.8 px wide, in the patterns' triplanar, octave-blended frame) with a hard edge, in the Relief view; on the opening planet they are few (~10k km²). Permafrost (the climate's closure's share, `climate.soil.permafrost`, carried a cell past the land onto the sea and grounded ice so the share interpolated toward a coast or an ice margin stays the land's: `drawnPermafrost` in the worker; in the tide's buffer's free slot) is a violet hatch (4.2 px apart, 0.8 px wide, at 0.7 of the ink, over a 0.07 wash) on the Climate view's tesserae, each frozen with probability the share, so the soft share reads as patchiness, as the zones' mosaic does (a9: the bespoke polygon nets for permafrost and salt were off; both now speak the zones' patterns' vocabulary). It is drawn over snow (the ground stays frozen under it; at fast dials most of it lies under the year's snow), under lakes, frozen over or not, and under the ice sheets' fill, in the Relief view; the key's Permafrost. Rivers above 1,500 m³/s are drawn in the sea's ink, opaque over the land, its farms and cities (a9; translucent, the colours under them had shown through and their joins darkened; on the Zones' mosaic and the charts the ink is mixed toward the paper instead), their weight the discharge, as the edges of a graph of the flow (`traceRivers`): where a confluence's second inflow carries at least `NODE` (0.4) of the first's discharge (within about a step of line weight, ×2.85, so neither reads as the one that runs on), it is a node, where every inflow ends on one point and a new river starts; where one dominates it runs on and the smaller ends on its smoothed course (rivers big in their wettest month alone are traced too). On the opening planet (level 7) 59 of 93 confluences are nodes, 310 rivers. Short streams are dropped by their branch's longest path upstream (three cells at level 6), so a kept stream never feeds a dropped one. Rivers are relaxed toward their neighbours (four passes at level 6, eight at level 7; corner-cutting alone kept the grid's straight runs between corners) and corner-cut three times at level 6 and twice at level 7 (~14 km apart either way), with the ends at a node held on its point and a tributary's end moved onto its stem's smoothed course; a river ends where the drawn relief crosses sea level along its course, so on the coast the globe draws (halfway to the sea's cell, 2% of river points lay over drawn sea; now 0.4%); they stop at lakes as the globe draws them (the worker passes the lakes' drawn field, `lakeDraw`, to `traceRivers`: a lake too small or shallow to be drawn doesn't break a river), running into the lake's cell, and a river leaving a lake starts at the lake's cell that drains into it (a river into a closed depression's sink runs to the sink's cell too); the page then cuts them where the drawn shore crosses them (`lakeAlong` in `overlay.js`: the lakes' drawn field interpolated over the grid's triangles at each river point, as the shader interpolates it, recomputed when rivers or lakes arrive; the segment crossing the shore ends on it): traced on cells, a river had run on past the smoothed shore, or left a stretch inside a lake. Where the globe shows the season (below ~1.4 yr/s) each river's weight is its discharge that month: the ground's runoff that month (its periodic year's, and live its own: see "How the ground and the lakes work") routed down the rivers' drainage (`Landscape.monthly` with `drainage`, no travel time), which ends at closed lakes; the year's discharge, which picks the rivers, is the months' mean. On the opening planet (level 6) 239 of 243 rivers swing over 3× in a year (213 over 10×), and high-latitude rivers peak with the melt; a river under a tenth of its mean (and 450 m³/s) that month is a dotted bed, an intermittent stream as maps draw it. The sea's tint turns a milky teal, as a bloom looks from orbit (toward verdigris, with a pale turquoise wash for the cells' scattering; a9: viridian read too green), by log export production (the phytoplankton's: none at ~5 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹, full past 25; the sea's median is ~4, its top tenth ~11), so upwelling coasts, river mouths and the ice edge carry faint teal halos. The deep sea's relief is eased twice more than the coast's, so isobaths are curves. The young-crust tint at ridge axes is gone: the smoothed ridge rule draws the axis, and the tint drew the grid's cells.
- **Motion between steps.** Plate steps are a tenth of a second's worth of the dial (50 kyr to 0.5 Myr; the landscape steps every 0.25 Myr). The globe's fields are kept for the last two steps (`setFields(…, fromStep)`), and the vertex shader blends them (`blend`) over the time from one step's arrival from the engine to the next's (the dial's time for the step, or the gap between arrivals if the engine is slower), so the surface moves continuously (each step moves the crust less than half a cell). Boundaries turn with the mean of their two plates' rotations to the displayed moment, and each new boundary morphs from its predecessor's course (`placeLine` in `overlay.js`; `matchLines` pairs them by plate pair and nearest middle; each new point corresponds to its nearest point on the old line, so points move across a line, not along it). Rivers turn with their plate from the last retrace and don't morph (sliding from course to course wiggled as the routing changed; a9): at a retrace each new river's points take the alpha of a drawn course within `SAME` (0.15 cells) of them and the old points they cover go (`succeedRivers`), so an unchanged river stays and a changed one crossfades only where it moved, as the shown moment crosses the step that brought the retrace (within 0.4 s of the wall clock), a retrace mid-fade carrying the alphas on. At 1 Myr/s a retrace moves 2–27% of river points (median ~21%) and, once a Myr when the crust's flexure lands, ~70%: routing on the filled flats flips with metres of change (with the flexure every step, ~55% at every retrace with a landscape step; with none, ~19%). Symbols are pinned to the line (`along.js`): each point carries u, a coordinate along the line in radians, which a retrace inherits from its predecessor at the corresponding point (smoothed over ~±250 km, a tenth of each retrace's stretch let go, points past the old ends carried on at arc length), and symbols sit at fixed values of u, so as a line moves they move across it with it and its ends advance or retreat over symbols that stay put (counted from the line's start, 16% of trench teeth and 42% of the chart's symbols jumped more than a fifth of a gap along their line per retrace; pinned, under 1%). The morph runs u from the inherited value to the relaxed one, so nothing jumps when a retrace arrives. The marks are a lattice in u: at the usual zoom every 16th node shows, at the symbol's gap on screen; where a line is foreshortened half of them shrink away an octave at a time, and zoomed in past the symbols' scale the next octave grows in. Dashes, dots and the ridge's zigzag follow u too. Boundaries take six Laplacian passes at level 6 (more on finer grids), so a re-traced boundary barely shifts. Which side stays on top reads in runs (a running mean over ±3 corners, then runs shorter than ~4 cells take their neighbours' side), and each retrace keeps half its predecessor's value at the corresponding point, so a trench's teeth don't switch sides. On the weather chart every line drifts whole with its mean wind (turned about r × v̄, so it keeps its shape) and every centre with its own, for up to three model hours between charts (every 150 ms), and each new chart corrects it. Chart lines are relaxed (Laplacian, ends held) before corner-cutting.
- **Ice streams** (Relief): where sliding carries more than half the ice's flow and it moves at tens of m/yr, short prussian dashes along the flow, one to each point of a staggered lattice fixed to the globe and scaled with the zoom (`flowDash`, octave-blended like the other patterns), as glaciological velocity maps draw streams: each dash is placed by its own point, so a flow that turns turns its dashes without the moiré a line pattern made. The flow comes as the ice's `vel` with its direction scaled by the sliding share (`drawnFlow` on the worker). At the opening few beds are soft (2–6% of the ice streams, the margins of the high-latitude sheets); on a 10 m regolith 58%, falling to 23% over 40 kyr as the beds strip. The key's Ice stream.
- **Snow and ice** are drawn from the snow-and-ice share and the ice's thickness smoothed twice over neighbours (edges are curves; one-cell patches melt away), eased over ~0.8 s when the climate updates (`src/gpu/season.js`, each frame on the GPU); a lake is frozen where the land's snow field, read on the lake at its surface's height, passes ½, and drawn so in the sea ice's ink with its edge's hairline under the lake's shore line, in the Relief view, the Zones map and the People view's Settlement map (the model freezes a lake's share by ramp(−Ts, 0, 4.5), a quarter degree colder than the snow's ½; through the year the two disagree on under 3% of lake vertices on the default seed). A frozen lake had been drawn as snowy ground, the lake gone but its shore line, and in the Zones and People maps it vanished into the mosaic and the land's uses. The land's snow and the sea's ice are two fields (by the climate's land), each smoothed among its own side's cells and carried two rings across the coast onto the other side's as the mean of the neighbours that hold it, and the shader reads the land's on land and the sea's at sea, so each runs to the coast however the drawn coast falls between the cells. As one field smoothed across the coast, a bay's ice took the summer's bare land around it and opened short of the shore, and a cell the drawn coast put on the other side of the climate's had its snow and ice dropped: on the default seed at 98 Myr, a quarter into the year, 124 of 411 iced coast edges opened before the coast; 5 now, where the ice's own edge meets the coast.
- **Collision** (a convergent boundary where no ocean sinks) has open sawteeth, as a thrust belt, and no arc.
- **Symbols scale with the globe**: trench teeth, arcs, transform arrows, hotspots, rivers' weight, fronts and dry lines, by the globe's radius on screen against 380 px, within ×0.75–3.5. Hotspots are red ringed points and an erupting province a red stippled disc.

- **Weather view** (the Weather group). Three charts (`wxMode`): pressure (isobars, rain), temperature (a prussian-to-sienna wash, isotherms every 5 °C, 0 °C heavier) and humidity (ochre-to-verdigris wash for the column's relative humidity, a line every 20%, rain); the Weather group's maps. The trades, the westerlies and the polar easterlies read where they should, mirrored in the south.
- **The season.** Where a year lasts long enough to watch, below ~0.8 yr/s, the globe shows snow and ice at the planet's clock (`years`; the plates still step in banked half-Myr), and the year's anomaly below ~1 yr/s; above ~3 yr/s, the share of the year under them and no anomaly; between, a blend (`seasonShown`, `anomalyShown`). ## Drawing the boundaries

`traceBoundaries` (`src/render/lines.js`) walks the Voronoi edges between plates into polylines that run between triple junctions; each polyline separates one pair of plates. It smooths each line on the sphere (Laplacian passes, six at level 6 and more on finer grids, then Chaikin corner-cutting, junctions fixed: two rounds at level 6 and one at level 7, so points lie ~27 km apart at either; two at level 7 doubled every line's points, and the page's cost of placing, matching and drawing them, for nothing seen). Every point is classified from the two plates' rotations there: the relative velocity across the line against the velocity along it, averaged along the line, so a boundary reads in long runs. Each point also keeps which side stays on top and whether the sinking side is ocean. The overlay places symbols along each line (pinned to it, `along.js`; spaced evenly on screen at the usual zoom), following geologic-map convention:
- trenches: a rule with sawteeth on the overriding plate, and red arc marks on that side where ocean sinks;
- ridges: a red rule, with the globe tinting crust younger than 1 Myr;
- transforms: a lighter rule with paired half-arrows for the sense of slip;
- inactive boundaries: a dotted rule.

Lines are retraced after each plate step (~6 ms). Each frame the page places each line (`placeLine`: its course turned with its plates and morphed from its predecessor's, in one pass, into the line's own buffer; the turn kept for the symbols' left side, turned only where a mark needs it) unless its cap (`capOf`: its points' and its predecessor's mean direction and widest angle) lies wholly behind the globe (`capFaces`), and reprojects it. A line holds only its predecessor, which a retrace without a step needs; held further back, the chain held every line ever traced (~265 generations after a minute at the top).
