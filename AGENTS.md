# Isochron

An Earth-like planet on one globe, from plate tectonics down to weather, with every layer coupled to the others. `docs/PLAN.md` is the plan of record: the thesis (one planet, one state, one dial), the nine layers, their couplings, the scene, the figures, the experiments, the phases, and the roadmap ahead (phases 6–10: the year, ice that carves, minimal life, dust, the engine on the GPU). This file describes the code as it is.

It is a submodule of the a9l.im repository at `projects/isochron` (github.com/a9lim/isochron). It is registered on the site (`content/projects/isochron.md`, with its social card) and staged by the parent's build; the parent's dev server serves it. The root `AGENTS.md` covers the shared layer, the design system ("Inside the scene") and the build.

Open `/isochron/tests/gpu.html` for the GPU's tests (multigrid convergence, PV conservation, Phillips' criterion, the step's cost; `?l6` times level 6 too); results land in `window.__results`.

## How the plates work

- **Plate frames.** Each plate keeps its crust in its own frame, on a full copy of the grid (mask, thickness, felsic fraction, formation time, suture time). World cell i reads plate k at Qₖ⁻¹xᵢ. Motion is exact (a full turn restores every cell), and nothing is resampled while a plate stays whole. Merges are the one resampling: one plate's cells are re-expressed in the other's frame.
- **The frames' extras** (`FRAME_EXTRA`): other layers' state kept with the crust, the regolith (`reg`, m) and the flexure's correction and the column's mass it last saw (`cf`, m; `mq`). They travel as crust does (`copyCell`: relaxation, merges, rifts, initiation, refinement) and new crust takes their defaults (`freshExtras`). The GPU's step doesn't hold them: its edits name a relaxed cell's source frame cell, and the CPU carries the extras from it; a frame cell holding crust made this step (its formation time says so, even when it was consumed and made again within the step) starts fresh (tests/gpu-plates.test.mjs).
- **The step.** Each world cell tests the plates that held it or a neighbour last step:
  - none covers it: new oceanic crust for the plate that left;
  - one covers it: that plate;
  - several cover it: the most buoyant stays on top. The others are consumed only if the plates are closing at that cell (`CLOSING`). An overlap where plates slide past each other is grid rounding, and the loser stays hidden. Without that rule, transforms churn crust and leave false slabs that pull along the boundary.
- **Consumption.** Ocean that goes down leaves slab memory at the cell, inherited along a migrating trench and decaying over `SLAB_TAU`, and builds arc crust on the overriding plate. Continent against continent stacks crust, marks a suture, adds collision resistance, and tallies the pair toward a merge.
- **Forces.**
  - Slab pull acts along each plate's edge only: each edge cell pulls with the longest slab of its plate within one cell. Counting the whole memory band made pull grow with speed and run away.
  - Ridge push is the gradient of subsidence within a plate's own ocean.
  - Drag is −c ∫ (1 + KEEL·φ)(I − r rᵀ) dA · ω, regularized by plate area.
  - ω relaxes toward the solved value over `V_RELAX` and is capped at `V_MAX`.
- **Starting subduction.** Old seafloor founders: at a plate's edge, any cell past `FOUNDER_AGE`; at its own continent's margin, the attached ocean breaks away as a new plate (`maybeInitiate`, at most every `INITIATE_GAP` and below `MAX_LIVE_PLATES`).
- **Plate count.** Too few plates, or one too large, splits the largest along the candidate plane that crosses the most sutures. A plate below `MICROPLATE` joins its largest neighbour.
- **Boundary relaxation.** A cell whose neighbours mostly belong to another plate passes its crust to that plate (`relaxBoundaries`, every step). Nearest-cell rounding otherwise roughens boundaries a cell at a time until they are fractal and dotted with one-cell islands. This is a numerical regularization (a curvature flow at one cell's scale): it moves crust and never makes or destroys any.
- **Genesis.** The largest continents each seed a plate at their centre, and ocean seeds fill the rest. Plates grow at their own pace, faster over continental crust, so boundaries meet at sea and continents start as passengers with passive margins.
- **Order.** The step visits the cells in index order, and three of its effects follow that order: a slab inherits a neighbour's slab already grown this step (so along an index-ordered run of trench cells it grows several cells in one step), a consumed frame cell is gone for the cells after it, and a founder keeps its later neighbours from foundering. The first matters: at level 7, inheriting from the step's start instead ran the plates at 2.3 against 2.9 cm/yr over 40 Myr, and level 7's slab scaling (√res) was fitted with it. The GPU's step reproduces all three (`src/gpu/plates.js`). An order-independent reference would be cleaner, but level 7 would need refitting first.

## How the landscape works

`Landscape` (`src/engine/landscape.js`) steps after every plate step, on the same crust.
- **Routing.** Cells below sea level are outlets. A priority flood fills depressions (lakes) with a small rise so flats still drain, and each cell drains to its steepest neighbour on the filled surface. The outlets head the order, and only those beside dry ground seed the flood (the open sea reaches only more sea, and pushing it through the heap was four fifths of the flood's cost); the flood's pop order follows, a topological order, so accumulation and the solver are single passes. The lakes route the smooth surface with the same flood (`lakes.js`, `reroute`).
- **Erosion.** Stream power with n = 1, dz/dt = −K Qᵐ S, solved implicitly from the outlets upstream (Braun and Willett's scheme), so any step is stable. Q is drainage area weighted by the climate's runoff. Rock removed lowers the surface by only (1 − ρ_crust/ρ_mantle) of its thickness, because the crust rebounds, so erosion takes thickness (`f` in the code is that factor).
- **Sediment** goes downstream with the water. Lakes keep what fits; at the coast it builds a shelf to `SHELF` below sea level and then moves to the deepest neighbouring sea. Deposits mix felsic material into the crust, so a thick enough delta turns oceanic crust continental.
- **The regolith and the ice.** Rivers strip the regolith before rock: the layer thins at the landscape's erosion rate (`erosion`), production against it solved exactly in `src/engine/regolith.js`; the thickness removed here is the same either way. The ice's outwash (half of the till that melts out, carried by its meltwater) waits in `sedIn` and joins the rivers' sediment at the next step where it melted out (`flux.outwash`). `preview(dt)` gives the rates a step would cut without cutting (the regolith's opening).
- **Diffusion.** Continental thickness diffuses between continental cells, faster under thick crust (hillslopes and small rivers below the grid's scale, and the collapse of orogens). It replaces phase 1's non-conservative relaxation of thick crust: erosion, sediment and diffusion move crust and never make or destroy it (tested). Explicit, in substeps that keep κ_max·ds under a fifth of the smallest cell's share (`plan`: ~5 at level 7 for 0.25 Myr, ~9 for 0.5, four times more a level), over a typed edge list built once, on the continental cells packed together (in cache), and written back to the frames, which every world cell then reads back (cells that share a frame cell see its sum).
- **On the CPU (phase 10b).** The flood, accumulation, the implicit solve and the sediment's carry are sequential down the drainage. Their parallel forms would need few passes here (level 7's receiver tree is at most 72 cells deep and Planchon and Darboux's filling converges in 52 Jacobi passes), but the step then needs the order, receivers, fill and lake flags back, and on the engine's device any readback waits behind the ice's steps (Threads and the GPU). The diffusion was ported (a gather per cell, f32, 3.8 mm from the CPU's; in the history, `git log -- src/gpu/landscape.js`): 1.8 ms against 5.4 alone, the same as the CPU's in the worker, overlapped with the rivers or not, so it stayed here. Tests check the flood, the months and the diffusion against their first forms (`tests/landscape.test.mjs`).
- **Water.** Sediment fills basins and fast spreading makes young, shallow seafloor, and both raise the sea. Over `WATER_TAU` (150 Myr) the ocean's volume drifts toward the land fraction set at genesis, a stand-in for the water ridges degas and trenches subduct. Earth's freeboard has held for billions of years by a mechanism still debated. High stands from fast spreading still happen on shorter timescales (seed `c` drowns to 18% land and recovers).

## How the climate works

`Climate` (`src/engine/climate.js`) is a diagnostic of the geography, recomputed in cycles: the energy balance, then winds and rain at six points of the year, then the gyres (after the winds that drive them: before them, the first cycle's saw none, and the currents stayed zero until the fourth), then the zones, then the ground's periodic year (sliced in eight, ~5 ms each at level 7). `work(ms)` advances a cycle for a time budget (a generator yields between stages), so the engine's worker spends the time between ticks on it and the climate relaxes toward the current geography. `solve(n)` runs whole cycles headless. The climate has **settled** (`stats.settled`, the cycle it did) once two cycles running moved the mean temperature by less than 0.02 K and rain, global and on land, by less than 0.5% (`SETTLE_T`, `SETTLE_P`; at the latest `SETTLE_MAX`, 16): from a cold start that takes 7–9 cycles headless on three seeds and ~10 on the page (~10 s at level 7), and the opening's references wait for it (see "How life works"). The slow part is the cover and the rain feeding each other (trees transpire and darken the land; each cycle closes about half the gap, and the gap moves downwind from cycle to cycle, so Anderson's mixing on the cover, β or the temperature across cycles didn't help); with the cover held, rain settles in three cycles. `tools/climate-check.mjs` runs to the settled climate and tunes on it: cycle 4, where the climate was first tuned, was a transient ~2.5 K warmer and ~15% wetter than where the old constants settled.
- **Energy balance** (standard), on level 5 (220 km cells) whatever the engine's level (at 110 km the cold cycle's three solves cost ~2 s of a level-7 opening, and the temperature is smooth at that scale), with `transfer.js` restricting forcing down and prolonging temperature up (through the levels between): C ∂T/∂t = S(1 − α) − (A + BT) + 5.35 ln(CO₂/CO₂₀) + ∇·D∇T, solved as the annual mean and two harmonics, each by complex-symmetric conjugate gradients (COCG), warm-started. T is the sea-level temperature; the surface is colder by 6.5 K/km. Albedo depends on the temperature through the year (snow below +1 °C on land, sea ice below −1.5 °C), so absorbed sunlight is re-sampled at 24 points of the year from the last solution: a fixed point, which keeps the ice–albedo feedback and its hysteresis. **The fixed point** (`energyBalance`): plain steps of it converge slowly or not at all (the annual mean's ice–albedo feedback contracts by 0.5–0.9 a step, and the seasonal harmonics flip, successive changes correlating at about −1: with one step a cycle the seasons alternated between two states ~0.4 K rms apart for as long as the climate ran), so each step is mixed by Anderson's method (standard; Walker and Ni) over the last three: G(T) less the combination of the last steps' changes in G that best cancels the residual, in the norm of T's variance over the year and the globe. A cycle steps until a step moves T by less than 0.02 K rms (`EBM_TOL`), at most 2 warm and 6 cold (`EBM_MAX`, `EBM_MAX_COLD`); the memory carries over between cycles while the residual is above the tolerance, restarts where the memory explains less than a fifth of the residual (the map has moved: the orbit, CO₂, the geography) or a step grows it, and is dropped once a step lands within it, so a settled climate takes plain steps. A step costs ~113 ms at level 7 (the 24 samples of albedo ~90, the solves ~20): cold ~0.7 s for six (three plain steps left ~1.5 K), warm one or two. The diffusivity (synthesis) falls toward the poles as D(1 − 0.6 sin²φ), D = 1.0 at the equator (`D_LAT` on each edge's weight; the polar air's stable layering carries less heat per kelvin): a uniform 0.45, tuned to cycle 4, left the converged planet 2–3 K cold with 60° 5–6 K colder than Earth's and 16–23% of the sea under ice; a uniform 0.75 fixed the sea ice but made the polar cap a flat −7 °C (Earth's Arctic ~−18, from memory); the shape gives 70–80° at −9 to −14 °C and 60° near 0. `A` is 205.5 (North's 203.3 plus 2.2 W/m², synthesis) to hold the mean near 14 °C under that transport. The seasonal harmonics use a smaller diffusivity (`D_SEASON`, 0.085) than the mean: with one diffusivity, continents smaller than ~5,000 km get the ocean's seasons, a known weakness of diffusive EBMs (0.1 left 55–65° continents ~3 K short of Earth's summers and D climates at 15% of land).
- **Circulation** (synthesis): a template. The ITCZ is the warmest latitude in each 10° sector of longitude (penalized away from the equator), the Hadley edge scales as √ΔT/Ω (Held and Hou), and surface pressure is a sum of the ITCZ's trough, the subtropical highs, the subpolar lows and the polar highs, plus −0.75 hPa per kelvin of each place's departure from its latitude's mean (smoothed): thermal lows over hot land, highs over cold land. The wind solves r u + f k×u = −∇p/ρ.
- **Moisture** (synthesis): column water W, steady, ∇·(uW) − ∇·(κ∇W) = E − P, upwinded, each cell's nonlinear balance by Newton: in the page by 160 Jacobi passes on the GPU (`src/gpu/moisture.js`, warm-started), headless by Gauss–Seidel in four sweep orders, at least `SWEEPS` (6 warm) and on in fours until the last four moved no column by 0.1% of its saturation (`MOIST_TOL`, at most `MOIST_MAX` 48 warm, four times as many cold): six alone stopped ~4% of the rain short of the converged balance the GPU reaches and closed the gap only over ~10 cycles; now 8 sweeps a settled cycle, 24–48 while the climate moves, ~190 cold (~3 s at level 6, ~12 s at level 7: headless only), and rain is within 0.4% of a 2e-4 tolerance's. The carrying wind takes the geostrophic part and 40% of the frictional drift (the drift is shallower than the moist layer). Evaporation pulls W toward saturation (Clausius–Clapeyron); over land at `LAND_EVAP` (0.56) of the sea's times the ground's evaporative efficiency β at that point of the year (the soil's wetness and snow cover, the closure's months, by the live ground's slow mean when the year is live), and over a lake's share of a cell as the sea does (×0.15 frozen, below 0 °C). Rain is (W_s/60) e^{15.6(r − 0.603)} mm/day at column humidity r (the shape of Bretherton and others' fit; constants from memory, unchecked), plus lifting up slopes, plus a light background suppressed where the air sinks. Rain shadows come from air that has already rained out.
- **Zones** (synthesis): monthly temperature and rain from the harmonics; Köppen's thresholds (Peel and others' version, from memory) as logistics, giving a probability over 14 classes. Each logistic's width is the year's spread at the cell (`Year.spreadFine`: a month's T′, a year's log rain, as σ/1.7, with floors of 0.5 K and 0.1 in quadrature), so a class's probability is the share of years in it; without a year (headless climate alone), 1.5 K and 0.25. Runoff is the ground's (see "How the ground and the lakes work"), and goes back to the landscape.
- **Feedbacks:** dust darkens snow, sea ice and the ice sheets and adds its forcing to the absorbed sunlight (see "How dust works"); the plants' cover sets land albedo (trees mask snow) and its evaporation (see "How life works"), the zones' until the cover is known (deserts brighter, forests darker); lakes mix it toward open water's (or ice's, below 0 °C); the ground sets land's evaporation and runoff; runoff sets erosion.
- **Routing roughness:** water routes over the surface plus ±25 m of roughness fixed to each plate's own cells (`LAND.ROUGH`), so flats drain in branching networks rather than straight parallel lines; erosion still uses the true surface, and a cell the roughness routes uphill doesn't cut.

## How ice and carbon work

`Planet` (`src/engine/planet.js`) steps the layers together: plates, plumes, the landscape and the crust's flexure per half-Myr plate step; carbon, ice and the regolith every frame; the climate's cycles in the background, each completed one refreshing weathering's map (`carbon.measure`) and, through the climate's version, the ice's mass balance.
- **The orbit** (`OrbitCycles`): eccentricity near 405, 124 and 95 kyr, obliquity near 41 and 54 kyr, precession near 22 kyr, seeded around Earth's values (from memory). The climate sees each term scaled by sinc(W/P) for a window W of what the dial passes in ~2 s, so at plate speed it sees the mean orbit and at kyr/s the cycles whole. The insolation table is rebuilt when the orbit changes.
- **Ice** (`Ice`, stepped on the GPU in the page by `src/gpu/ice.js`: four dispatches a step, the step size from an atomic maximum, never read back mid-run, the count planned from the limit last read with 5% spare; 48 steps a tick at most; the state read back every ~100 ms, the bed's fields and the glacial integrals every ~400 ms): thickness on the surface grid, in the mantle's frame. The flux is the shallow-ice approximation's, −Γ H⁵ |∇s|² ∇s, plus sliding's, explicit with the step their sum's stability allows (~25 yr at 110 km, ~5 at 55 km on hard rock, ~3 on soft till); `advance(years, ms)` steps within a real-time budget and drops the rest, so at plate speed the ice lags the climate rather than the clock (its closure). The mass balance is snow below freezing at the ice's surface (the lapse rate makes a high sheet keep itself cold) less melt by positive degree days, less basal melt. Ice afloat calves, and the ice's water is taken from the sea (`tec.iceWater`); the sea is re-solved when that has moved ~10 cm. The climate counts grounded ice as land, its surface as the height, and its albedo as snow's.
  - **The bed's temperature** (standard: Robin's steady column, from memory): heat conducted up from the bed against cold snow carried down, the vertical speed falling linearly from the accumulation a (the net balance, where positive) to nothing at the bed: T_b = T_s + (G + τ_b ū_d) R/k with R = l √(π/2) erf(H/(√2 l)), l = √(κH/a), κ = 36 m²/yr (R = H where the ice ablates). T_s is the annual mean at the ice's surface (no warmer than 0 °C), k = 2.1 W/m/K; τ_b ū_d is the deformation's heat (the column's dissipation, mostly in its lowest layers, so taken at the bed: synthesis), against a melting point falling 0.87 K per km of ice (from memory). The thawed share is a logistic of T_b − T_pm over 1 K. G (`geo`) comes from the plates every 0.5 Myr (`geothermal`: GDH1's 0.51/√age on the ocean, 50–75 mW/m² by age on continents, from memory). Robin alone froze the opening's beds (3–4% thawed: its accumulation is high, a median 0.6 m/yr); with the deformation's heat ~70% thaw, as conduction alone had.
  - **Sliding** (standard: Weertman-type): u_b = C τ_b³ × thawed, τ_b = ρᵢgH|∇s|, so the sliding flux is a diffusion of the deformation's form, C(ρᵢg)³H⁴|∇s|², and the two add. C is per cell from the bed's softness (`soft`, 0 hard rock to 1 soft till, through `setSoftness`; log-linear from `C_HARD` 1e-14 to `C_SOFT` 1e-12 m yr⁻¹ Pa⁻³, synthesis), from the regolith's depth (`src/engine/regolith.js`). Where thawed, basal melt is (G + τ_b ū_d + τ_b u_b − k(T_pm − T_s)/R)/ρᵢL; friction feeds the melt, not the thawed share. **A speed limit** (synthesis): where the depth-mean speed would pass `U_MAX` (3 km/yr) the flux holds there, and the sliding with it. The shallow-ice approximation fails at a cliff onto the sea or a pit of ice between peaks, and one such cell sliding at ~640 km/yr set the whole ice's explicit step (~0.01 yr), so the page's ice stalled. Outputs: `ub` (m/yr), `thaw`, `Tb` (°C, CPU), `mb` (m of ice a year), and `stats.sliding`, `thawed`, `streaming` (the ice's share sliding faster than it deforms).
  - **Glacial erosion** (standard rule: ė = K_g u_b; K_g = 2e-6, calibrated: Humphrey and Raymond's 1e-4 eroded the opening's ice at 10–19 mm/yr, and 2e-6 gives Hallet's ice-sheet order, ~0.06–0.1 mm/yr at 220 ppm, from memory): rock taken under sliding ice is carried as debris (`debris`, m of rock per cell) with the ice's fluxes at the upwind cell's concentration, and melted out as till where the ice ablates, in proportion to the ablated share (all of it where the ice vanishes or calves). Meltwater (surface and basal) runs down the ice's surface to the first ice-free cell (`route`). `takeGlacial()` hands on and zeroes the integrals since the last take: `eroded` and `till` (m of rock per cell) and `outwash` (m³ of water per cell); Σ eroded = Σ till + Δ Σ debris exactly. The regolith applies them to itself and the crust.
  - **The bed** (standard: ELRA, Le Meur and Huybrechts, from memory) relaxes over 3 kyr toward the plate's flexural response to the ice's load (`attachFlexure`: `Flexure` on the CPU, `GpuFlexure` on the GPU, whose w the bed kernel reads in place; Tₑ from the plates by `elasticThickness` every 0.5 Myr, or fixed), once an advance: `w += (−w_eq − w)(1 − e^{−dt/3 kyr})`, `w` negative down. The CPU re-solves by two warm V-cycles when the load has moved 1% (and until converged), the GPU by one warm V-cycle in the step's pass every 30 model years. A forebulge (~12 m at ~2α beyond a 3 km sheet's margin on a 90 km plate) rises round each sheet and collapses after it. With no flexure attached, the bed relaxes toward local isostasy, −(ρᵢ/ρₘ) H. Either way the crust takes it as `tec.load`. Attached in the planet (and on the engine's GPU in the page): at 220 ppm the opening's ice settles near 12 M km³ (30 m of sea) against the local bed's 23 and rising after 600 kyr, since ~15 M km² of ice-free ground round the sheets sinks ~35–48 m and the bed under them stands ~100 m higher, so the sheets drain to lower, warmer margins (ELRA's real behaviour; not retuned away).
- **The regolith** (`Regolith`, `src/engine/regolith.js`; standard: Heimsath's soil production, from memory; synthesis as built): a weathered layer in m of rock equivalent, part of the crust's h, kept in each plate's frame (`reg`). Production P₀e^{−h/h₀} (h₀ 0.5 m, P₀ 0.1 mm/yr at 15 °C and 0.3 m/yr of runoff, by runoff^0.25 e^{(T−15)/30 K}: weathering's form but gentler, since frost and drying break rock in cold and dry places too), none under ice or the sea; the rivers' erosion E strips it first (`dh/dt = P₀e^{−h/h₀} − E` solved exactly, u = e^{h/h₀} relaxing to P₀/E, so h* = h₀ ln(P₀/E), tested); the ice's erosion takes it before rock and the crust loses both; till melting out is regolith again, less the half its meltwater carries off (`OUTWASH`) to the landscape's `sedIn`. h is f32, so what rounding leaves of the ice's change waits in `resid`. It sets the ice's bed softness (1 − e^{−h/1 m}), the soil's water capacity (40 mm on bare rock to 260 mm as e^{−h/1.2 m} fills: the opening's land mean 151 mm against the fixed 150 it replaces; through `climate.regolith` in `geography`), and weathering's supply (`carbon.measure`: the production rate in E_HALF's place; at steady state production is the erosion, so the rule reads as before, and where the ice strips the layer it jumps to P₀). The opening's layer is OPEN_MYR (30 Myr) of production against the rates the rivers would cut (`land.preview`), re-made with each climate cycle until the climate has settled; START ≥ 0 sets a depth instead. On the opening (level 6): mean ~1.0 m, 8% bare (steep and very cold land), 1–2 m in the tropics and mid-latitudes; under the 220 ppm ice the beds strip to softness ~0.05–0.1 within ~200 kyr. It steps from `Planet.fast`, at most every 250 ms in the page (~8 ms at level 7), the ice's integrals as they come back.
- **The crust's flexure** (`CrustFlex`, `src/engine/crustflex.js`; standard plate, synthesis as built): crust.js's columns are compensated as made, and flexing them whole moved margins by kilometres, so the flexure is an incremental correction to the Airy elevation in the frames (`cf`, m): each plate step, Δq = g(ρh − mq) per column since it was last seen (erosion, deposition, stacking, arcs, basalt, till; motion carries `mq` and is no load), then c ← c e^{−dt/τ} + (Δq/ρₘg − w(Δq, Tₑ))(τ/dt)(1 − e^{−dt/τ}), added to the elevation (`tec.flexc`, km; `updateElevation`, and the GPU's step through its load term, the moved crust's swapped in after). Tₑ is the plates' (`elasticThickness`) every 0.5 Myr; τ = ∞ on oceans, 50 Myr on continents (speculation); |c| ≤ `C_MAX` 2 km, the plate's strength (isostatic anomalies rarely pass ~±200 mGal, from memory): the grid gathers a collision's stacking into one cell, which an elastic plate alone held up as a needle 15–20 km high within 2.5 Myr. It reduces to Airy at Tₑ → 0 and τ → 0 (tested). Over 20 Myr at level 7 the land rms is ~200 m, foreland basins and held-up ranges at the cap, land 27.2–27.4%; plate speeds, land and the sea level stay within the runs' noise (level 6, 100 Myr, two seeds). On the CPU at level 6 a solve is ~20 ms; in the page, on the engine's GPU, twelve V-cycles from cold (each step's load is new; 2e-5 of the deflection at level 7) and ~10 ms of the CPU a plate step at level 7, ~70 ms with the GPU's queue.
- **Organic carbon:** burial scales with the sediment the rivers deliver to the sea, oxidation of old organic carbon with the rock they erode, each a fifth of the reference outgassing at the opening (`CARBON.ORG`).
- **Carbon** (`Carbon`): outgassing against weathering, stepped through the sea's carbon (see "How life works"; `pCO₂` is the air's). Outgassing scales with crust opened at ridges and ocean closed at trenches, measured from the plates' motion across each boundary (`tec.kinematics`: counting gap cells also counted the grid's rounding, a few cells per boundary per step, so the rate grew as steps shrank), against the spin-up's last 10 Myr, plus provinces' pulses. The arcs' share is split: the carbonate the plates carry down comes back from the sea's ledger (its 40% of what's subducted, ~half the opening's outgassing), so `mantle()` is the ridges' and the arcs' own carbon (the arcs' share less the ledger's opening return), and the Thermostat's knob scales it. Its weathering reference is taken once the climate has run three cycles and the landscape has stepped (before phase 7 the opening's erosion was zero until then, the supply a quarter, and the reference came out 2.7× low: headless and at slow dials, CO₂ crashed to ~60 ppm at the first landscape step; the regolith's production and the landscape's preview fill it from the opening now); until then the map is the opening's and so is the CO₂ it saw. Weathering is the climate's map (runoff^0.65 × e^{(T − 15)/Tₑ} × fresh-rock supply, 0.25 + 0.75 P/(P + E_HALF) with P the regolith's production, × the cover's factor, summed over ice-free land), times the response to CO₂ since the climate last solved (the sensitivity through Tₑ, and pCO₂^0.3 directly). `equilibrium(V)` is the analytic balance (tested on the planet).
- **Plumes** (`Plumes`): ten hotspots fixed in the mantle build crust on whatever passes (seamount chains) and lift swells (`tec.swell`). Plume heads arrive as a Poisson process (every ~30 Myr): a flood of basalt over a disc ~1,100 km across within 1 Myr, 8,000 Pg of carbon, a new hotspot, and, under a large continent, a rift through the plume (`tec.rift(k, point)`).

## How life works

Enough life to make the environment robust, no more (a9). Two models, each tested alone, wired in by `Life` (`src/engine/life.js`).
- **The cover** (`Vegetation`, `src/engine/vegetation.js`; VECODE's forms, synthesis): per land cell, trees, grass and bare ground, the vegetated share relaxing over 3 years and the trees' share of it over 200, each exactly over any step, toward VECODE's equilibrium from degree-days above 0 °C (the climate's twelve monthly temperatures, each month's days spread by 4 K), the year's rain and a cold limit (not the soil's water, the module's option: the ground's water follows the rain, so scaling the rain by it counted aridity twice, and over ~40 cycles the default seed's bare share grew to 42% of land against 25% without, and the planet cooled 0.8 K more); NPP by Lieth's Miami model with CO₂ fertilization, carbon in trees, grass and soil (respiration by a Q10), solved in closed form, so NPP − respiration = ΔC to rounding. It lives in the mantle's frame, as the ice does. Its climate is set after each climate cycle, before weathering's map is measured (~16 ms at level 7); it steps from `Planet.fast` at most ten times a second of the dial and at least a year at a time (~2.3 ms at level 7), so past its τ it is its climate's (the closure), and its carbon's trade goes to the carbon cycle's next step. At the opening the cover sits at its balance with each cycle until the climate has settled (the closure; `Life.closed`), with the air held at the opening's CO₂ (`Carbon.holdOpening`); the sea then settles on the planet (below), so the land's store (~2,100–2,300 Pg in plants and soil; 45–48% trees, 18–21% bare on three seeds, converged) is full before anything is measured.
- **The cover's feedbacks** (standard): land's albedo from VECODE's surface albedos at half their contrast about grass (bare +0.065, trees −0.035), under snow masked by trees (snowy forest down to 0.40 against open snow's 0.62: the boreal forest's warmth); land's evaporation, bare ground (0.4) and grass at the ground's β, trees (1.2) at √β (deeper roots); weathering, bare rock at a quarter of forest's. Retuned so the opening's climate holds (`CLIMATE.ALB_COVER` 0.03 on snow-free land absorbs the cover's darker land and the forests' snow masking, ~1.2 K together; `LAND_EVAP_COVER` 0.52): `tools/climate-check.mjs` now attaches the cover. Its first cycles are a transient (the cover and the rain feed each other); the converged climate, which the tool now runs to, was retuned ("How the climate works").
- **The sea's carbon** (`OceanCarbon`, `src/engine/oceancarbon.js`, after LOSCAR and the three-box models): the air; a warm surface box (85% of the sea), a cold one (15%) and the deep, each with DIC, alkalinity and phosphate; carbonate chemistry with constants checked against PyCO2SYS; gas exchange, a 20 Sv overturning and mixing, the biological pump; carbonate buried on warm shallow shelves ((Ω − 1)^1.7) and on the deep floor above the compensation depth (a bioturbated layer per 150 m bin of the seafloor's hypsometry, dissolving below saturation), riding the plates into the trenches, 40% of it back at arcs. Backward Euler in seven unknowns, any step stable; ~0.05 ms a tick. Its inputs from the planet (`Life.seaInputs`, each cycle): the cold box is the coldest 15% of the sea by annual surface temperature (the climate's and the gyres' anomaly; on the opening planet −1.8 °C, the warm box ~20.5 °C), its open water the share not under sea ice; warm shelves are sea under ~200 m (soft, 100–300) with water above ~18 °C (soft); productivity `bio` is the rivers' sediment and the coasts' upwelling against the opening's; carbonate weathering goes with land's runoff. It settles on these at the end of the cover's closure (the cycle the climate settles in; the air held at the opening's CO₂ until then), the references taken there; weathering's waits for the landscape's first step too. The opening (default seed): saturation at ~2.2 km, compensation at ~4.1 km, the shelves burying 0.13 Pg C/yr of carbonate and the deep 0.12, the arcs returning 0.048 (the mantle the other 0.052 of the 0.1 outgassed).
- **The export, drawn** (synthesis): each surface box's export production spread over its water by what feeds it, the warm box's by a background, the coasts' upwelling and the rivers' mouths, the cold box's evenly over its open water, blended across the cold box's edge; g C m⁻² yr⁻¹ per sea cell, posted with the climate (in the zones' free slot, which the globe reads). On the opening planet the gyres' ~4, coasts and the cold sea 12–56.
- **Pulse** (`src/engine/pulse.js`): the share of a pulse left in the air, against a twin of the sea copied at the pulse and stepped with the same inputs; the theory is the closed sea's partition (1 kyr), the compensated sea's (10 kyr: CO₃ restored, pCO₂ ∝ HCO₃², each carbon taken up bringing a carbonate from the floor), then weathering's decay of the excess at the compensated partition (100 kyr). See "The experiments".
- **No green margin.** Started under full forest and under bare ground, the planet's climate and cover converge to one state on three seeds within ~40 cycles (the moisture and the ground are slow to converge, so for ~20 cycles they look bistable), so there is no Green margin experiment.

## How dust works

Mineral dust is the thread between the ice, the water and life (`docs/PLAN.md`, phase 9): the balance is `Dust` (`src/engine/dust.js`, tested alone), wired in by `Aeolian` (`src/engine/aeolian.js`) inside the climate's cycle. Each of the six points of the year packs its sources after its winds and rain (`Climate.phase` calls `aeolian.phase(p, T)` after the moisture, while `climate.carry` is that point's carrying wind); after the six (and the zones' months) the balance is solved, on the engine's GPU in the page (`src/gpu/dust.js`, 240 Jacobi passes warm, 960 cold) and by Gauss–Seidel headless (`CPU_SWEEPS` 32 warm, ×4 cold, or to `CPU_TOL`), then routed (`route`: `finish` at each cell's albedo, then `effects`). Everything routed is the last cycle's.
- **The balance** (`dust.js`; standard forms, synthesis as built): GOCART's emission C S u²(u − u_t) as its expectation over gusts about the template's wind (a Rice distribution of speed, tabulated), two size bins (fine 30%, coarse 70%) carried by the moisture's wind and eddy mixing, settled (0.1 and 1 cm/s over a 3 km layer) and washed out (14% of the column per mm of rain), steady at each point of the year on level 6 at most (the engine's cells receive what they emitted, exactly); optical depth and a top-of-atmosphere forcing −20 τ (1 − α/0.45) W/m², cooling over dark ground and sea, warming over snow.
- **Sources** (synthesis): a land cell's strength is the union of three shares, × the open ground at that point of the year (no snow by the albedo's ramp, no lake water, no ice past 10 m) × its loose grains, 1 − e^{−h/5 cm} with h the regolith (bare rock gives nothing):
  - bare, dry ground: the cover's bare share (the zones' arid share before the cover is known) × a taper on the soil's relative water at that point of the year from 0.35 to 0.55 (the closure's months; GOCART's cutoff at a wetness of 0.5, softened), whose water also raises the threshold as GOCART's u_t0 (1.2 + 0.2 log₁₀ w) above w = 0.1 (from memory);
  - dry lake beds: the lakes' salt flats, weighted 3× (speculation: Earth's largest sources are dry lakes and closed basins' alluvium);
  - outwash plains: 1 − e^{−q/1 m/yr} where the ice's meltwater leaves it at q (`outwash`, through the regolith's update, eased over 1 kyr), whatever the soil's water.
  On the settled opening (level 7) ~18 M km² of source, 99.4% of the emission from bare, dry ground, 0.6% from salt flats, none from outwash.
- **The gusts** (speculation): the template's highs and lows have fixed depths, so a glacial would blow no harder; the gusts stand for the weather, so their rms (`DUST.GUST`, 2.5 m/s) scales with each hemisphere's equator-to-60° contrast at that point of the year against 26 K, the settled opening's (Eady's growth rate goes as the meridional gradient, standard), blended across the tropics: 2.5 m/s at the opening.

## How the ocean and the weather work

- **Gyres** (`Ocean`, on the climate's coarse grid: the stress restricted, the SST anomaly from the energy balance's coarse mean, ψ and the anomaly published back on the surface's grid in `fine`; ~0.1 s a solve at 220 km, 1.1 s at 110 km): the depth-integrated flow under the annual-mean wind stress, steady, Stommel's form on the unit sphere, ε∇²ψ + ∂ψ/∂λ = a² curl τ/(2Ωρ₀), coasts at ψ = 0 (islands too), ε set for a two-cell western boundary layer. BiCGSTAB with a Jacobi preconditioner, warm-started, 120 iterations a cycle and the gyres only every third climate cycle (the basins change slowly). Surface currents u = k × ∇ψ/H (H = 400 m). The SST anomaly T′ solves upwinded u·∇(T + T′) + λT′ = κ∇²T′ + upwelling, where upwelling cools coasts the Ekman drift leaves. Moisture uses the SST (T + T′) for the sea's saturation, and sea ice forms on it (in the energy balance's albedo, its share of the year under ice, and the season drawn), so currents shape the ice edge: with the gyres at ~12 Sv the anomaly runs −6 to +1.7 K and the edge's spread in latitude went ±1.4° → ±2.0°; it stays near a latitude circle, as diffusive energy balances' ice edges do. Tested against Sverdrup's interior and the western boundary current in a box basin.
- **Weather** (`GpuWeather`, `src/gpu/weather.js`): two-layer QG on the GPU, on the drawn mesh (level 7, 55 km), its forcing refined from the engine's grid, L_d = 800 km. PV is carried by fluxes that are differences of ψ at Voronoi corners (exactly non-divergent), 20% upwinded, with ∇⁴ hyperviscosity scaled with the cell's 3.2 power (with the fourth power, the finer grid's noise outgrew it). Adams–Bashforth 2 at 200 s (800 s at level 5, halving with the cell: the advective limit with the upper layer's ~115 m/s jets; 300 s blew up on day 1). The upper layer starts with half the climate's shear and the forcing builds the rest: starting with all of it, storms grew explosively and the transient's jets (~110 m/s on day 3) sat at the step's limit and sometimes passed it; now winds climb to ~50–65 m/s with no spike. The lower layer's state is q₂ less its orography, whose PV is carried alongside, so the ground can change under the weather. Inversion each step: the barotropic Poisson and baroclinic Helmholtz solves at once (a vec2) by two V-cycles of multigrid on levels 7 down to 2 (`src/gpu/hierarchy.js`: weighted Jacobi, two sweeps each way; restriction gathers a coarse cell's six midpoints; prolongation averages a midpoint's two parents; level 2 solved exactly by a precomputed inverse), warm-started. Each cycle cuts the error tenfold. The barotropic solve screens at 1/k₀ ≈ 32,000 km, which fixes ψ's constant (a ~2% touch on the largest waves). Forcing: the baroclinic ψ relaxes over 15 days toward SHEAR · (f/f₀) · (T − T̄) from the climate's temperature at the season; Ekman friction on the lower layer; damping in the tropics in flux form, so PV's integral is conserved (to 1e-8 over 10 days). Tracers on the lower layer: θ relaxed to the climate's over 5 days, and moisture from the sea, raining past 90% of saturation. 66 dispatches and ~0.55 ms of GPU a step on an M5 Max (timestamps), ~240 ms a model day; the page gives it 12 ms of each frame, so it runs live to ~3 days a second. A kernel writes the globe's weather fields (eddy pressure over the template, its zonal mean interpolated between 3° bands, rain, temperature, humidity, blended with the climate's mean) into the mesh's vertex buffer each frame, from the last two steps blended by how far the clock is into the next (ψ before the last step is kept for it), so at a slow dial the steps don't land a frame at a time. Tested on the device (`tests/gpu.html`): storms grow above Phillips' criterion and die below; PV is conserved; multigrid converges.
- **The chart** (`chart.js`, traced in `chart-worker.js` from the weather read back every ~120 ms and restricted to the engine's grid: tracing on the 55 km mesh cost ~650 ms a chart against ~100): sea-level pressure is the climate's template at the season plus the storms (ρfψ₂ less its zonal mean), smoothed, drawn as isobars at whole hPa (every 4, heavier every 20); rain as stipple. Counts on the grid's lines (smoothing passes, chain lengths, the rings of a high or low) scale with the level, so the chart reads alike at every resolution. Highs and lows are extremes of the pressure eased to 220 km over ~440 km, at least 1 hPa from their surroundings. Ends that nearly meet, meet (`joinEnds`): each end of a front, dry line or shear line moves onto the nearest end of another within ~320 km, or onto the nearest point of one within ~190 km, the last points tapering, so fronts' pieces, occlusions and their remnants, and dry and shear lines running into fronts join with a kink. On the page each chart is linked to the last (`linkCharts`: lines of a kind by nearest middle, centres within ~400 km) and drawn morphing from it over the time between charts (`chartFrame`), new lines fading in and old ones out. Fronts are the zero of ∇TFP·n̂ on θ where |∇θ| and TFP are in their upper ranges and the low-level flow is cyclonic (fronts lie in troughs), traced on triangles, chained, smoothed, typed by v·n̂ against 0.45 of the chart's median |v·n̂| (low-level flow runs nearly along the isotherms here, so an absolute threshold called every front stationary), then held in runs (a majority over ±8 points, runs under 10 points absorbed) with their symbols on one side per run. The locator's zero must be a maximum of TFP along n̂ (a minimum drew a second front beside each). Fronts are then split into stretches of one type; a stretch lying mostly within ~290 km of a longer one of its type is the same front found twice and goes; a cold stretch within ~380 km of a warm one has caught it, and the matched parts merge along their midline into an occluded front (mulberry, triangles and semicircles alternating on one side). Troughs (umber, dashed), ridges (umber zigzag) and subtropical shear lines (prussian, dash-dot) are ridge lines of −p, p and cyclonic vorticity, found by the Hessian's eigenvector (aligned per triangle), long and not round; troughs a front already marks aren't drawn. The fields (θ, moisture, wind, vorticity) and thresholds are averaged over successive charts, and pieces whose ends meet and line up are bridged, so fronts move rather than flicker. Dry lines are the same on the relative humidity the Humidity chart draws (the column's water over wsat(θ)), over land, from the top quarter of its gradients, where θ's gradient is under 1.25× the fronts' threshold, pieces bridged and thresholds averaged as fronts' are (on the column's water, the top 15% and no bridging, the chart found 0–1). The weather runs only in its view, waits for a warm climate, spins up ~12 days, and crossfades to the climate's mean between ~3 and ~30 days a second.

## How the tides and the Moon work

- **The tide** (`Tides`, `src/engine/tides.js`; `GpuTides` in the page): linear shallow water on the rotating sphere on a C grid over the Voronoi cells (height on cells, the velocity normal to each edge between two sea cells, coasts closed), forced by the semidiurnal equilibrium tide of a Moon over the equator (cos²φ e^{2iλ}, ¾(m/M)(R/a)³R = 0.27 m today), with the body tide (α = 1 + k₂ − h₂ ≈ 0.69) and self-attraction and loading as β ≈ 0.9 on the sea's height (standard values, from memory). Coriolis acts on each edge through the other edges of its two cells, with weights from Perot's reconstruction of the cells' velocities antisymmetrized in the energy's norm (Σ ρHu²ℓd), so it does no work: TRiSK's linear form, ½(1 + H′/H) (Thuburn and others 2009; standard, from memory; on Perot's weights, synthesis). Perot's averaged rotation alone is antisymmetric only at uniform depth: where one cell's edges spanned a shelf and a deep sea it made energy (its worst mode gaining 3.6e-4/s against 2.6e-4/s of drag), and a deep cell beside a shelf grew without bound within 10–50 Myr of moving coasts (606 m, 1,500 TW, the spin through zero; since the tides' first commit). Heun's step for the rotation, forward–backward for the waves, drag implicit; on the GPU five dispatches a step, the corrector reading only the predictor's velocities. Drag: bottom friction C_D·U₀/H (0.0025 m/s) and internal-tide drag ½κh²N/H (3e-8 against the depth's step² between cells, capped at a quarter of the depth: a continental slope's whole drop as roughness walled the shelves off and spun up ~150 spurious amphidromes). Run to periodic (32 cycles cold, 5 warm: a basin mode rings for days) and the harmonic read over the last cycle; dissipation is the drag's work, 2ρrH⟨u²⟩ per edge's diamond. Tested: on a still global ocean the forced degree-2 response is analytic (within 1%); mass is kept to 1e-16; Coriolis's power vanishes to 1e-12 over a sea of cliffs; the GPU matches the CPU to 0.001% (0.41 s cold and 72 ms warm at level 6); `tests/moon-long.test.mjs` runs the worker 50 Myr at level 6 at the page's 1 Myr/s cadence (24 solves, 2.9–6.0 TW; the old Coriolis reached 19 TW and climbing).
- **At level 6 always** (the engine's level-6 cells, which nest first in a level-7 grid): shelves resolve at 110 km (at level 5 the lunar semidiurnal tide dissipated 1.5 TW, at level 6 4.2), and the Moon then recedes alike at every engine level. On the opening planet: median amplitude 0.28 m, a resonant gulf to ~6 m, ~20 deep-water amphidromes, 4.0 TW in the lunar semidiurnal tide at both engine levels (Earth's ~2.5; ~58% on shelves, as Earth's), ~5.8 TW in all, so this planet's Moon recedes ~6 cm/yr and its day lengthens ~3.8 ms a century (Earth's 3.8 cm/yr and ~2.1 ms, from memory). Over 40 Myr at 1 Myr/s the dissipation wanders 3–6 TW as the basins move and drifts down (the Moon receding ~5 cm/yr on average, the day ~3.2–3.5 ms a century; on the page 3.9–6.3 TW over 32 Myr). Its ocean is deeper than Earth's (median 5.6 km), which the solver takes as given; the constants are Earth's, not fitted to Earth's total.
- **The Moon** (`Moon`): each plate step, the lunar tides' torque slows the spin (C dΩ/dt) and recedes the Moon (L = μ√(GMa)); the solar tide slows the spin too. The lunar tides' dissipation is the semidiurnal's × 1.25 (the diurnal tides; from memory), the solar's (0.46)² of it. Between solves the tide keeps a constant time lag (standard: Mignard's and Hut's weak-friction tide): torque k(Ω − n), so dissipation ∝ (Ω − n)², k set by the last solve (k = P/(Ω − n)² there) and scaled with (R/a)⁶; holding the dissipation instead made the torque P/(Ω − n) unbounded toward the synchronous spin. The spin steps by backward Euler, so Ω′ is a weighted mean of Ω, n and n☉ and no step carries it past the synchronous spins or below zero, whatever the dissipation (at 10⁹ TW the system lands on the dual-synchronous state, a 47-day day with the Moon at 554,000 km, as Earth's would; tested); the orbit takes the torque's impulse whole, so angular momentum is kept exactly. A solve that comes back non-finite is dropped and the next starts cold (warm starts would carry it on); the worker re-solves every ~2 Myr (the coasts move) or when 2(Ω − n) has moved 0.3% (the basins' resonances move with it). With Earth's dissipation (3.7 TW) the Moon recedes 3.8 cm/yr and the day lengthens 2.1 ms a century (tested). `CLIMATE.OMEGA` follows the spin: the Hadley edge, the template's winds, the gyres' β and the weather (which takes Ω when built, with L_d ∝ 1/Ω, and is rebuilt when the Weather view opens if the spin has moved 0.3%). Headless (no GPU), the Moon stays put.
- **The Tides view** (`globe.js`, mode 5): the sea's tint by the semidiurnal amplitude (co-range), co-tidal lines every lunar hour drawn as the zeros of sin 6G from the harmonic's complex sixth power (no seam where the phase wraps), fading into each amphidrome, and a sienna stipple, its density the dissipation per area. Below ~6 h/s the tint is the sea's height now (the lunar and solar semidiurnal tides, so springs and neaps beat over 14.8 days) and a heavier line marks high water. The tide's clock is the sky's: its phase is 2(θ − the Moon's right ascension), so the bulge sits under the Moon. The record figure carries the day's length; Fig. 7 a Tides node (the basins in, the spin out to the climate, the ocean and the weather).
- **The sky** (`sky.js`, on the overlay outside the globe's disc): a seeded star field fixed in space, drawn through a perspective whose scale zoom doesn't change (stars at infinity), turning about the spin axis by the sidereal angle θ where a day takes a few seconds or more (below ~6 h/s), held still above; the Sun on the ecliptic at its longitude for the date (the orbit's calendar, at the orbit's obliquity), shown below ~0.25 yr/s; the Moon at its longitude (advancing at its mean motion) shown below ~5 days/s, lit toward the Sun (an almanac's ○ full and ● new), both at their true sizes ×5 so a nearer Moon looks larger. Ink on the paper; the dark theme's paper is the night. Sun and Moon don't light the globe (its hillshade keeps the cartographic light).

## How the year works

`Year` (`src/engine/year.js`) is stepped from `Planet.fast` (so the worker and `Planet.run` both run it), with the dial's rate: the worker's ticks carry it, and headless a fast step stands for a tenth of a second.
- **The stepper** (synthesis). Whole months, each step computed when the clock enters it, to its end, so the page draws the state between the step's two ends by its own clock. A step is a month while a month takes at least ~1/30 s of the dial, then longer (4 months at 10 yr/s, 40 at 100), at most 30 a second; after a jump of up to 20 years (`JUMP`) it covers the gap in one, and a longer jump (a plate step by `.` at a slow dial, a stall) is the closure's whatever the dial (it had made one step of 6 million months, the ground looping each). `live` = 1 − smoothstep over log₁₀ rate 1.75 → 2.25 (56–180 yr/s): consumers weigh the live state by it (the season's anomaly, the rivers', the record's, the veil's marks). Outrun, nothing steps: the veil decays with the clock (no eruptions), the ground drops its live state, and back from it each cell takes the OU's exact transition over the gap (`resume`: the anomaly decays by e^{−BΔ/C} and the noise refills the closure's spread; the tag's response decays alike).
- **The ground's hook.** `year.soil` (the `Ground`), when set, is called once a step while live: `soil.step({ years, m0, months, live, dT, rf })`, the step's first calendar month and its length, and the anomaly on the engine's grid held over the step (`fineAnomaly`: T′ in K and the rain's factor, both weighted by `live`; ~1.6 ms a step at level 7), and `soil.closure({ years, live })` each advance while `live` < 1. (The month-by-month hooks it replaced built each month's inputs on the CPU, ~2 ms a month at level 7, 1.3 s a second at 56 yr/s; the lakes are slower and step from the planet, see "How the ground and the lakes work".)
- **The anomaly** (standard: Hasselmann; North's diffusive form): C ∂T′/∂t = −B T′ + ∇·D∇T′ + F on a 440 km grid (level 4, 2,562 cells), C from the climate's land and sea ice (restricted), B the energy balance's, D = 0.03. F is the weather's heat flux as noise, white at the month and correlated over 1,500 km (a Gaussian kernel over level-3 nodes), plus the veil's forcing less the eruption rate's mean. The veil's is exact in time (`forced`): each source (the veil at the step's start, each eruption from its moment) decays at the veil's rate, its response is the convolution of two exponentials, and it enters as the forcing that, held over the step, gives that response (against the analytic one, within 0.3% at steps of 1 to 60 months; averaged at the step's two ends, 3-year steps had cooled twice as much). Each step is each cell's exact OU transition, then the diffusion by preconditioned CG (16 iterations; 0.6 ms a month at level 7, 1.3 ms for 40). Its amplitude by latitude (`YEAR.Q`) is fitted to the QG weather's monthly-mean θ over land (`tools/anomaly-fit.mjs`), the equator held at 10°'s. Sea anomalies last C/B ≈ 3.8 yr, land ones weeks; land's monthly spread is ~2.6× the sea's.
- **The rain's anomaly** (synthesis): z = ρ T′/σ + √(1 − ρ²) η per cell, ρ −0.3 over land and +0.3 at sea (from memory), η its own noise; a cell's monthly log rain spreads by 0.45 (MAP/1000 mm)^−0.3. It scales the ground's rain, and through the ground the rivers' discharge and the lakes' inflow (mean-preserving, before the ground's live state: the climate's runoff by runoff's elasticity of 2). Held over a step of k months, η stands for the step's mean, its spread 1/√k of a month's, and the factor's mean-keeping term takes z's variance ρ² + (1 − ρ²)/k (without it, a year's step lost ~6% of the rain).
- **The closure**: each cell's stationary spread, the local OU's q/√(2(B + D·40)C̃) with C̃ the heat capacity smoothed over six passes and blended 0.7 into the cell's (fitted to the live model: land within ~7% rms on three seeds), and the share a year's mean keeps. The zones read it at every dial.
- **Volcanoes** (`src/engine/volcanoes.js`; standard effects, synthesis as a process): a seeded Poisson process, sizes N(>M) ∝ M⁻¹ in Pinatubos (each decade ten times rarer). Each source's arrivals are unit exponentials of its own hazard (rate × time) carried across steps, so a changing rate is taken step by step and the closure, which doesn't erupt, neither stores arrivals nor replays them; each eruption is injected at its moment within the step. Arcs: from 0.1 to 30, ≥ 0.1 every 10 yr at the opening's convergence (`carbon.arc / carbon.arc0`), placed by the plates' arc memory (`tec.arc`); hotspots: ≥ 0.1 every 400 yr each, to 3; an erupting province: ≥ 1 every 60 yr, to 30. The veil is optical depth in 5° bands: 0.15 global per Pinatubo (from memory), injected over ±4°, spread in latitude (0.5 rad²/yr) and decaying over 1 yr; forcing −25 W/m² per unit optical depth everywhere (from memory). The rate's mean forcing (−0.3 W/m² at the opening) against the opening's is `CLIMATE.F_VOLC`, in the energy balance at every dial: zero at the opening, whose mean is in the climate's tuning; a flood adds its pulses' mean.
- **Drawn.** The anomaly shifts the season's temperature on the GPU (`src/gpu/season.js`, between the step's two ends), so the snow line and the sea ice's edge move from year to year; the rivers by the month carry their routed rain anomaly (a factor per cell of each river's chain); the record's temperature carries the global mean. An eruption is a red triangle (the map's volcano, the mantle at the surface), sized by its sulfate, for 1.5 s once the clock passes it (Relief and Climate); the veil is a graphite stipple in the Relief view, its density the optical depth, below ~10 yr/s. Fig. 7 has Year (the anomaly's global mean, or its spread when outrun) and Volcanoes (the veil's optical depth): plates and plumes erupt, the veil forces the year (live) and the climate (its mean), the climate sets the year's heat capacity, and the year's spread is the zones' widths.

## How the ground and the lakes work

- **The ground** (`src/engine/soil.js`; standard: Manabe's bucket; synthesis around it): soil water to a capacity (150 mm on land, none at sea or under grounded ice, which owns its water; when it is cut, the water past it runs off as the next month's quick flow, on the CPU and the GPU alike), evaporating at the potential times β = min(1, S/0.75 S_max), overflowing from its saturated share w⁴ (HBV's and the Arno scheme's), draining at 0.4 S_max w⁴ a month; the snowpack by degree days on the month's spread of days (as the ice's), shutting evaporation off; one ground-heat store, insulated by snow, whose annual mean is permafrost (soft); and groundwater, a linear store the drainage recharges and that empties over 4 months (synthesis), whose outflow is the baseflow. Potential evaporation is Budyko's scale by the month, 55/12 (T + 4) mm. Its periodic year under the climate's months is the climate's closure (`Climate.groundYear`, each cycle, warm, ~2 years a cell; ~40 ms at level 7 in eight slices): the climate's runoff by month and in the year, the lakes' open-water balance (rain less potential evaporation), and β at each of the moisture's six points of the year (snow cover included). On the opening planet land's runoff is 49% of its rain; the cold and permafrost cells run ~15% below Budyko's (its floor), the rest within ~1%. The rivers' months swing a median 32× in a year (with the drainage straight to the rivers, 750×; `monthlyRunoff` had 8×, with a fixed third of the runoff as baseflow).
- **Live** (`src/engine/ground.js`, `Ground`, the year's `soil`): stepped by the year's steps on the engine's GPU (`src/gpu/soil.js`: the climate's months resident, uploaded once a cycle (~5 ms at level 7); each step uploads the anomaly alone, held over the step, and dispatches its months, ~0.01 ms a month of GPU; the last twelve months are recorded by calendar month) or headless on the CPU (`cpuLive`; ~13 ms a month at level 7, so the worker doesn't). Its calendar runs on from the handover month by month, never skipping one. Handover from the closure: the periodic year's state at the step's first month, then a year of the climate's months alone, which repeats the periodic year (tested to 0.05 mm a month on the GPU) and fills the record; outrun (`live` = 0), the live state is dropped. The worker reads back the last twelve months' aggregates, the rivers' month's runoff and the bank (below) ~10 times a second (2–4 ms), headless once a year of the clock; each handover and each drop is a new epoch, and a read from an earlier one is discarded. The bank is the water the live ground has let go, mm per cell over the months it stepped (summed on the GPU and emptied by each read), with the span of the clock it covers. Consumers weigh the live state by `live`: the lakes' runoff, Fig. 7, and the climate's β, by the live slow mean (5 years) against the closure's (at most 3×), so a drought's dry soil evaporates less into the next cycle.
- **Rivers by the month** run on the live ground's runoff that month where it has one (else the climate's under the rain's anomaly), routed down the rivers' drainage: on the opening planet (level 6, 30 yr/s) their annual cycle lags the catchment's rain by a median 0.20 months (`monthlyRunoff`'s: −0.02), 21% of them by half a month or more (11%), 27 of 243 peak a month after the rain's peak (2 of 249).
- **Lakes** (`src/engine/lakes.js`; standard hydrology; synthesis): routed on the smooth surface, the ground plus its ice (so ice dams them, and ice holds none), not the landscape's rough one (whose roughness makes one-cell ponds), and rebuilt after each landscape step (at most every 0.5 s of the wall clock) and when the ice has moved 3.5e-5 km·sr of water (at most every 100 years of the clock and 1 s of the wall clock; a rebuild costs ~20 ms at level 7, and at the opening the ice moved that much every other frame). Each rebuild carries the water over (an ice dam's loss releases what it held, exactly). The planet steps them each fast step (`stepLakes`: backward Euler, at most 20 times a second of the dial and at least a quarter year a step; 0.13 ms a step at level 7) below 10⁴ yr/s and sets them at their balance above (`equilibrium`, 0.5 ms), and at the opening at their balance with each climate cycle until the climate has settled (the first, from a cold start, holds half the lakes the third does). Forcing: the ground's runoff (the closure's, and by `live` the live ground's bank, of which each step takes the share up to the clock, so the lakes receive the volumes the soil let go; at their balance the live last twelve months', which carry the year's anomaly with its own statistics; read as a rate over the lakes' own steps, the last twelve months' had lost ~16% of the soil's runoff into the lakes' catchments) and the climate's rain less potential evaporation on open water (no live anomaly on the lakes' own surface: held over a step, a month's anomaly would stand for a season's). The climate's next cycle reads the lakes' wet share (`lakeWet`, snapshot at its start) for evaporation and albedo. On the opening planet at level 7 the lakes cover ~600–750k km² (100–130 lakes, 10–20 closed); over 200 years at 30 yr/s (level 6) their area moves 704–743k km² and the closed lakes' 14–317k km² as the largest switches between closed and spilling.
- **Rivers and lakes** (`src/render/rivers.js`, `drainage`): the rivers' drainage is the landscape's, except that in a closed lake's depression each cell drains to its lowest neighbour in the depression (on the lakes' surface) and the lake's cells are sinks, and where it leaves a spilling lake's depression it carries the lake's outflow against its inflow (each region's in and out in `regIn`/`regOut`). So a river below a closed lake is dry, and below a spilling one carries the overflow; over a year the drained rivers reach the coast with the direct runoff and the lakes' overflow within ~3% (the two routings' catchments differ). The rivers retrace when a lake opens or closes; the landscape's erosion keeps its own Q (through the lakes, over its rough routing).

## Threads and the GPU

- **The engine's worker** (`src/engine/worker.js`) owns the `Planet` (level 7; level 6 on phones and tablets), and its own GPU device for the ice, the moisture, the dust, the tide, the ground, the plates' step and the surface's fields, and the two plates of flexure (`GpuFlexure`: the ice's bed, which reads its w in place, and the crust's correction, read back each plate step). The page keeps the clock: each frame it plays, it posts a tick (the years, the plate steps its scheduler planned, the orbit's window); the worker runs them all. Plate steps are planned no shorter than the engine can keep up with (it reports its cost per step, ~70–100 ms at level 7 with the landscape's, drawing's and the climate's shares), and the clock holds while two steps are outstanding, so the top of the dial runs at what the engine manages (at level 7, in node with phase 6's layers, ~5.5 Myr/s, and ~0.9 at a dial of 1 Myr/s, whose steps are 0.1 Myr; with the plates on the CPU ~3.8 and ~0.7; at level 6 the whole dial). Every experiment runs at 1 Myr/s or slower. The climate runs alongside as an async loop, a 10 ms slice of its cycle at a time, awaiting the GPU where the cycle yields a promise (`solveAsync` is the headless form). It posts `surface` after steps or when the sea moves (the drawn fields, from `src/gpu/surface.js`, the boundaries and rivers traced, the plates' rotations, the crust's statistics), `climate` after each cycle (harmonics, zones, ocean, the export production, the dust's load and statistics), `tides` after each solve of the tide (the harmonic and the dissipation per unit equilibrium tide, prolonged to the engine's grid), `year` after each of the year's steps (the anomaly at the step's end on the engine's grid, the step's two ends, the veil's bands, the eruptions), `lakes` when they've changed, at most four times a second (`lakeFields`: the drawn depth and salt, ~3 ms at level 7), `cover` when the plants' cover has changed, at most twice a second (eight colour weights a cell, ~6.6 ms at level 7), and `fast` ~10 times a second (with the Moon's distance, the spin and the dissipation) (ice, with its flow as drawn: the direction as long as sliding's share; carbon, plumes, the regolith's and the crust's flexure's statistics, the year's rates, forcing and tagged eruption, the rivers' anomaly, the record's samples, the model figure's sums). Ticks carry the dial's rate, which the year's live weight reads. The page's mirrors (`mirror.js`, and plain objects in `main.js`) have the engine's shapes, so drawing and figures read them unchanged. Experiments' parameters go over as a `params` message; `CARBON.FIXED: 'now'` holds CO₂ where it stands.
- **The shown moment.** Surfaces queue on the page as they arrive (irregularly: a landscape step makes some slower), and the globe shows a moment τ that follows them a step and a half behind at the engine's pace (measured over the last 3 s of arrivals), nudged ±50% by how far it is from that aim and eased over 0.3 s; it eases into the newest when nothing waits. Between the two surfaces either side of τ the globe blends (`setFields` modes: step, reset, refresh), boundaries turn with their plates to τ and morph, and rivers morph over the landscape's step.
- **The plates on the GPU** (`src/gpu/plates.js`, from `Tectonics.stepAsync` through `Planet.plateStepAsync`, which runs the plumes first so their crust and swells go up with the step). The CPU's plate frames stay authoritative: the landscape and the plumes write them through (owner, ref), and merges, rifts and initiation rewrite whole plates on the CPU. The GPU keeps a mirror of every frame (crust as h, φ, formation and suture time per frame cell; masks as a bit per plate per frame cell) and the world's memories (slabs, arcs, collision fronts). Each step sends up the top cells' h and φ as the CPU's frames hold them, the rotations, the ice's load and the swells. 36 dispatches follow at level 7: two decide passes, the slabs' chain, writes, relaxation, sampling, decay, elevation, the kinematics, the sea level (one workgroup's Newton), foundering and the per-plate sums. One read back brings down owner and ref, the crust on top, elevation, the sea level and land, the sums (ω is solved on the CPU in f64), counters, collision tallies, and every frame cell the step changed, as edits applied to the CPU's frames. The memories come down only before a merge or an initiation; afterwards the touched plates, owners and memories go back up. A frame cell two world cells reach is written once, by the lowest index among neighbours, so the step is deterministic. One step from one state agrees with the CPU's at 99.99% of cells; over 40–60 Myr the GPU's statistics differ from the CPU's no more than a CPU nudged by 3e-3 in ω (tests/gpu-plates.test.mjs). The pump awaits the step, one pump at a time; while pumps run back to back the climate takes a 10 ms slice every 100 ms (it may fill a pump's wait), and between pumps it runs freely. The surface’s fields are prepared on the device too (`src/gpu/surface.js`, from the engine’s arrays uploaded each time, so it doesn’t matter which side last stepped them); the lakes’ fields stay on the CPU (`lakeFields`, posted only when the lakes change).
- **The GPU** (one device, `src/gpu/device.js`): the globe draws; the weather steps and writes the globe's weather buffer; the season writes its climate buffer. Kernels declare only the bindings their entry uses (`kernel()` binds by name under the 'auto' layout), at most eight storage buffers each.
- **The engine's device is busy at fast dials.** At level 7 at the dial's top each tick's ice advance (up to 48 steps) keeps it working ~16 ms (measured in node, `onSubmittedWorkDone` after the submit), and every readback the pump awaits queues behind that: the plates' step waits ~4–7 ms more than alone, the crust's flexure likewise, and a 1.8 ms kernel took ~10. A second device doesn't help (the hardware is shared: 3.8 ms of wait). So a port pays only where its work outweighs a readback's wait, or where nothing waits for it. The landscape, the lakes' routing and the tracing of lines and rivers stay on the worker's CPU (phase 10b, "How the landscape works").
- **The chart's worker** (`src/render/chart-worker.js`) gets ψ₂, θ and moisture read back from the GPU with the template's pressure and derives wind, vorticity and the eddy pressure before tracing; one read and one trace are in flight at a time.

## The experiments

Each starts from the planet's opening (choosing one reloads with `?exp=`), sets the dial and the view, and has one knob; the caption carries its measured number.
- **Earth-like**: the opening.
- **Snowball**: sunlight (82–106%), CO₂ held at its value when chosen; the ice line. Headless on the default seed, stepping the sun down with the climate settled at each step: 98% holds the ice line at 68°, 96% at 59°, 95% slides toward the equator over tens of cycles, 94% freezes (−34 °C); turning the sun back up to 104% doesn't thaw it (the ice–albedo hysteresis). (Before the retune it froze at 94% from an opening that was itself 23% sea ice; the page's 88% was read off the transient.)
- **Ice ages**: CO₂ held (100–450 ppm, 180 to start), 10 kyr/s; the ice's volume and its dominant period (periodogram of the record over 400 kyr). Since the retune the default seed's sheets are small: ~1.4 M km³ at 220 ppm, 1.5 at 180, 2.5 at 100 (3–6 m of sea; before, 14 M km³ at 220, on high latitudes 5–6 K too cold). Its land at 55–75° has summers near Earth's, too warm for sheets to start; the ice's side (its mass balance, or a seed with polar land) is open work.
- **High stand**: mantle heat (scales slab pull, ridge push and the speed cap); sea level and land.
- **Thermostat**: outgassing (0.25–4×); CO₂ against its analytic balance. Headless at 2×: 651 ppm against 647 at 1 Myr, 681 against 682 at 3 Myr (before the retune 596 against 593).
- **Eruption**: sulfate (0.1–30 Pinatubos, on a log knob; each size set erupts again once the knob rests), 1 yr/s, one eruption at the most active tropical arc once the climate is warm; its peak global cooling and e-folding time against the theory (independent boxes with each cell's heat capacity under the veil's decaying global forcing). The number is the eruption's own response, a tagged veil and response carried beside the rest (the model is linear), so the noise and other eruptions don't blur it. Headless on the default seed, 1 Pinatubo cools 0.39 K at its peak 1.3 yr after (Pinatubo's was ~0.4–0.5 K, from memory), e-folding 3.75 yr; the theory says 0.44 K at 0.8 yr, 3.4 yr: the diffusion ties land's fast response to the sea's. (Before the retune, with more sea ice and so less heat capacity, 0.41 K and 3.4 yr.) Linear in size (−3.9 K at 10; the largest are overstated: optical depth is linear in sulfate here).
- **Pulse**: carbon (500–10,000 Pg C, on a log knob; each size set pulses again once the knob rests), 3.2 kyr/s, Climate view; one pulse into the air once the sea has settled; the share still in the air after 1, 10 and 100 kyr against the theory (the closed sea, the compensated sea, then weathering: "How life works"). Headless on three seeds alike within ~1 point (`tools/pulse-run.mjs`): 500 Pg leaves 16.5–16.7%, 6.6–7.4%, 2.5–2.6% (theory 15.8–15.9, 5.1–5.2, 3.1); 2,000 Pg 24%, 7.1–7.5%, 2.6% (20.9–21.0, 5.3, 3.1–3.2); 10,000 Pg 51%, 11.1–11.5%, 2.6% (45.5, 6.2–6.3, 3.4). At 1 kyr the deep isn't quite ventilated, at 10 kyr compensation isn't quite done (for the largest pulses the floor's layer runs short), and at 100 kyr the theory, seeing the compensated pCO₂ from the start, misses the early weathering. After the retune (default seed): 500 Pg 16.9%, 6.8%, 2.6% (theory 16.9, 5.3, 3.2), 2,000 Pg 24.3%, 7.2%, 2.6% (22.1, 5.5, 3.2). The tail e-folds over ~140 kyr, against Archer's ~300 (from memory): reported, not tuned.

## Drawing the climate

- **Relief view.** Land takes the plants' cover: trees, grass and bare ground, split by warmth into eight classes (`COLOURS` in `vegetation.js`: tropical, temperate and boreal forest, savanna, steppe, tundra, desert, rock) drawn in the biomes' natural colours (`biome` in the shader), blended in the vertex shader from a storage buffer of eight weights a vertex (a ninth vertex buffer is past the limit; refined like the zones), with rock toward the summits; umber height alone until the climate has run. The cover lags its climate, so after a deglaciation bare ground and grass come before the forest, and the Climate view (the climate) and the Relief view (the plants) can disagree. Snow and sea ice are a light fill with a hard edge (the contour of the snow-and-ice share at ½, antialiased by its derivative) and a hairline, ice sheets the same with a stronger outline and contours of the ice's surface every 500 m. Lakes are drawn from their water (`lakeFields`): a smoothed field signed so its zero is where a cell is half under water (50 m plus the water's depth past it, down to −50 m on dry land, so a shallow lake's shore holds against the smoothing), with a hard shore and its hairline, from two cells up (one cell would draw as a hexagon), eased over 0.4 s when they change (in their own vertex attribute, packed into the tide's buffer: a pipeline takes eight vertex buffers). Salt flats, the share of a cell a closed lake has dried from over the last ~3 kyr (at their balance, also the fringe a 50% wetter year would flood), are a pale crust cracked into polygons (the borders of a jittered lattice's cells, in the patterns' triplanar, octave-blended frame, cracks of constant width on screen) with a hard edge, in the Relief view; on the opening planet they are few (~10k km²). Rivers above 1,500 m³/s and three cells long are drawn in the sea's ink, their weight the discharge: main stems run continuously (at each confluence the larger river runs on; rivers big in their wettest month alone are traced too), relaxed toward their neighbours (four passes at level 6, eight at level 7; corner-cutting alone kept the grid's straight runs between corners) and corner-cut three times, with each tributary's end moved onto its stem's smoothed course; a river ends where the drawn relief crosses sea level along its course, so on the coast the globe draws (halfway to the sea's cell, 2% of river points lay over drawn sea; now 0.4%); they break at lakes. Where the globe shows the season (below ~12 yr/s) each river's weight is its discharge that month: the ground's runoff that month (its periodic year's, and live its own: see "How the ground and the lakes work") routed down the rivers' drainage (`Landscape.monthly` with `drainage`, no travel time), which ends at closed lakes; the year's discharge, which picks the rivers, is the months' mean. On the opening planet (level 6) 239 of 243 rivers swing over 3× in a year (213 over 10×), and high-latitude rivers peak with the melt; a river under a tenth of its mean (and 450 m³/s) that month is a dotted bed, an intermittent stream as maps draw it. The deep sea's relief is eased twice more than the coast's, so isobaths are curves. The young-crust tint at ridge axes is gone: the smoothed ridge rule draws the axis, and the tint drew the grid's cells.
- **Motion between steps.** Plate steps are a tenth of a second's worth of the dial (50 kyr to 0.5 Myr; the landscape steps every 0.25 Myr). The globe's fields are kept for the last two steps (`setFields(…, fromStep)`), and the vertex shader blends them (`blend`) over the time from one step's arrival from the engine to the next's (the dial's time for the step, or the gap between arrivals if the engine is slower), so the surface moves continuously (each step moves the crust less than half a cell). Boundaries turn with the mean of their two plates' rotations, and rivers with their plate's, to the same displayed moment (`turned` in `overlay.js`), and each new line morphs from its predecessor's course (`matchLines` pairs them by plate pair or plate and nearest middle, rivers strictly: both ends near and lengths within 40%; each new point corresponds to its nearest point on the old line, so points move across a line, not along it; `morphed` blends by the step's φ, rivers by the landscape's). Symbols are pinned to the line (`along.js`): each point carries u, a coordinate along the line in radians, which a retrace inherits from its predecessor at the corresponding point (smoothed over ~±250 km, a tenth of each retrace's stretch let go, points past the old ends carried on at arc length), and symbols sit at fixed values of u, so as a line moves they move across it with it and its ends advance or retreat over symbols that stay put (counted from the line's start, 16% of trench teeth and 42% of the chart's symbols jumped more than a fifth of a gap along their line per retrace; pinned, under 1%). The morph runs u from the inherited value to the relaxed one, so nothing jumps when a retrace arrives. The marks are a lattice in u: at the usual zoom every 16th node shows, at the symbol's gap on screen; where a line is foreshortened half of them shrink away an octave at a time, and zoomed in past the symbols' scale the next octave grows in. Dashes, dots and the ridge's zigzag follow u too. Rivers with no predecessor fade in and those with no successor fade out. Boundaries take six Laplacian passes, so a re-traced boundary barely shifts. Which side stays on top reads in runs (a running mean over ±3 corners, then runs shorter than ~4 cells take their neighbours' side), and each retrace keeps half its predecessor's value at the corresponding point, so a trench's teeth don't switch sides. On the weather chart every line drifts whole with its mean wind (turned about r × v̄, so it keeps its shape) and every centre with its own, for up to three model hours between charts (every 150 ms), and each new chart corrects it. Chart lines are relaxed (Laplacian, ends held) before corner-cutting.
- **Ice streams** (Relief): where sliding carries more than half the ice's flow and it moves at tens of m/yr, short prussian dashes along the flow, one to each point of a staggered lattice fixed to the globe and scaled with the zoom (`flowDash`, octave-blended like the other patterns), as glaciological velocity maps draw streams: each dash is placed by its own point, so a flow that turns turns its dashes without the moiré a line pattern made. The flow comes as the ice's `vel` with its direction scaled by the sliding share (`drawnFlow` on the worker). At the opening few beds are soft (2–6% of the ice streams, the margins of the high-latitude sheets); on a 10 m regolith 58%, falling to 23% over 40 kyr as the beds strip. The key's Ice stream.
- **Snow and ice** are drawn from the snow-and-ice share and the ice's thickness smoothed twice over neighbours (edges are curves; one-cell patches melt away), eased over ~0.8 s when the climate updates (`src/gpu/season.js`, each frame on the GPU); lakes under snow are frozen over.
- **Collision** (a convergent boundary where no ocean sinks) has open sawteeth, as a thrust belt, and no arc.
- **Symbols scale with the globe**: trench teeth, arcs, transform arrows, hotspots, rivers' weight, fronts and dry lines, by the globe's radius on screen against 380 px, within ×0.75–3.5. Hotspots are red ringed points and an erupting province a red stippled disc.
- **Patterns** (the zones' screens, rain stipple) are fixed to the globe and scale with the zoom: drawn in three planar projections of the sphere blended sharply by the normal (triplanar), at a frequency from the globe's radius in pixels, with the next octave's marks fading in between the last's (`octave`, `hatch`, `stipple`), and marks of constant width on screen.
- **The key** (`src/render/key.js`) at the globe's lower left draws each view's symbols as the scene does; `K` or the Key button toggles it (open on wide screens).
- **Weather view.** Three charts (`wxMode`): pressure (isobars, rain), temperature (a prussian-to-sienna wash, isotherms every 5 °C, 0 °C heavier) and humidity (ochre-to-verdigris wash for the column's relative humidity, a line every 20%, rain); the picker under the views appears in Weather. Pale land, the paper for sea; cold fronts are prussian with triangles toward the warm air, warm fronts sienna with semicircles toward the cold, stationary fronts alternate, dry lines are ochre with open scallops toward the moist air, and H and L mark the highs and lows.
- **Climate view.** Land is a mosaic of tesserae fixed to the globe (a jittered 3D lattice, snapped to octaves of zoom). Each takes one class with probability ∝ p², so ecotones interleave and the logistic tails don't speckle. Each class is its group's ink in its second letter's screen-space pattern (`zoneColor` in the shader and `zonePattern` in `figures.js` must match). A, C and D also step in value, so the groups part in grayscale. The sea is a plain wash with short viridian strokes on the stipple's staggered lattice (`strokes`, fixed to the globe and octave-blended like the other patterns, 0.9 px wide), their density the export production (15 px apart at ~5 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹, 5 px at ~80, none in the gyres), over an ochre stipple whose density is the dust carried over it, which settles on the sea ice and the ice sheets too (see "How dust works"); the tectonic symbols are hidden.
- **The season.** Below ~4 yr/s the globe shows snow and ice at the planet's clock (`years`; the plates still step in banked half-Myr); above ~40 yr/s, the share of the year under them; between, a blend. The caption names the month below 300 yr/s.

## Drawing the boundaries

`traceBoundaries` (`src/render/lines.js`) walks the Voronoi edges between plates into polylines that run between triple junctions; each polyline separates one pair of plates. It smooths each line on the sphere (three Laplacian passes, then two rounds of Chaikin corner-cutting, junctions fixed). Every point is classified from the two plates' rotations there: the relative velocity across the line against the velocity along it, averaged along the line, so a boundary reads in long runs. Each point also keeps which side stays on top and whether the sinking side is ocean. The overlay places symbols along each line (pinned to it, `along.js`; spaced evenly on screen at the usual zoom), following geologic-map convention:
- trenches: a rule with sawteeth on the overriding plate, and red arc marks on that side where ocean sinks;
- ridges: a red rule, with the globe tinting crust younger than 1 Myr;
- transforms: a lighter rule with paired half-arrows for the sense of slip;
- inactive boundaries: a dotted rule.

Lines are retraced after each plate step (~6 ms) and reprojected each frame.

## Open work

- **Ice that carves.** The ice barely answers the orbit (0.1–0.8% of its volume at 220–260 ppm, weak 41 kyr, nothing near 100 kyr), so the Regolith experiment has no rhythm to show (`tools/regolith-survey.mjs`). - **Drawing.** The globe draws a level-7 mesh (163,842 vertices); a level-6 engine's fields are refined onto it by one step of Loop subdivision on the GPU (`src/gpu/refine.js`: a midpoint takes 3/8 of each end and 1/8 of the two vertices across; an old vertex keeps 10/16 of itself at valence six), so coasts, isobaths, lakes, ice edges and zones' borders are curves at close zoom. At level 7 the mesh is the engine's grid and nothing is refined.
- **Level 7's tuning.** The forces are discretized on the cells, and the same planet on a finer grid pushed and pulled harder: refined from level 6, level 7's plates settled ~30% faster (rms 4.4 against 3.4 cm/yr, activity 1.5×, the sea +127 m and land 23% by 30 Myr), and level 5's ~40% slower. Each is a stable equilibrium, not a runaway: kicked ×0.7 or ×1.3, a level returns to its own speed within ~6 Myr. Two sources, measured on one state read at both levels: ridge push sums a one-sided gradient at the plate's edge, which ends at the edge cell's own depth, nearer the axis on a finer grid (level 7's ~12% stronger); slab pull counts edge cells beside slab memory one cell wide, whose band and staircase change with the cell, and it is the lever (scaling ridge push alone barely moved level 7). PARAMS are tuned at level 6, so `balance` scales the two with the cell, fitted: K_RIDGE × res^0.2 and K_SLAB × √res. Over 60 Myr level 7 then holds rms 2.8–3.2 cm/yr, activity 0.90–1.13×, the sea +9 to +50 m and land 27–28%, against level 6's 2.9–3.5, 0.84–1.01×, +30 to +53 m and 28–29%; coupled over 20 Myr, CO₂ settles at ~375 ppm and 13.6–14.5 °C (level 6: ~390, 14.0–14.6). Making the ridge's edge exact instead (the depth mirrored to the axis at age zero) made levels agree on one state (3% apart) but shifted push onto the ridges, and level 6 itself then drifted (1.2×, +112 m in 30 Myr), so it's out.
