# Isochron

An Earth-like planet on one globe, from plate tectonics down to weather, with every layer coupled to the others. `docs/PLAN.md` is the plan of record: the thesis (one planet, one state, one dial), the nine layers, their couplings, the scene, the figures, the experiments, the phases, and the roadmap ahead (phases 6–10: the year, ice that carves, minimal life, dust, the engine on the GPU). This file describes the code as it is.

It is a submodule of the a9l.im repository at `projects/isochron` (github.com/a9lim/isochron). It is registered on the site (`content/projects/isochron.md`, with its social card) and staged by the parent's build; the parent's dev server serves it. The root `AGENTS.md` covers the shared layer, the design system ("Inside the scene") and the build.

## Status

The dial runs from 1 yr/s to 10 Myr/s; below a few years a second the globe shows the season.

- **Optimization pass (2026-11-15):** the planet runs on a worker, the weather and the season's drawing on the GPU, the chart on its own worker (see "Threads and the GPU"). The engine runs at level 7 (55 km), or level 6 (110 km, a quarter of the work) on phones and tablets (`?level=` overrides); the globe and the weather run at level 7, a coarser engine's fields refined onto the mesh on the GPU (`src/gpu/refine.js`, Loop subdivision); the ice and the climate's moisture run on the engine's own GPU device, the energy balance and the ocean on a 220 km grid at every level. ## Running and checking

Serve from the parent root with `node docs/sim-overhaul/serve.mjs 8790` (the parent `.claude/launch.json` calls it "sims") and open `/isochron/`. `?seed=<string>` picks the planet. Open `/isochron/tests/gpu.html` for the GPU's tests (multigrid convergence, PV conservation, Phillips' criterion, the step's cost; `?l6` times level 6 too); results land in `window.__results`.

```bash
(cd tests && npm install)               # once: WebGPU in node (Dawn), for the GPU parity tests
node --test tests/                       # grid, clock, plates, landscape, climate, ice, carbon, ocean, and GPU ice, moisture, tide, plates and surface against the CPU (the weather's are in tests/gpu.html)
node tools/engine-cost.mjs 7 20 0.5 earthlike gpu   # the engine's plate step by part at level 7 (drop gpu for the CPU's)
node tools/planet-run.mjs earthlike 600 1000 10 '{"CARBON":{"FIXED":220}}'   # the coupled planet: 600 kyr of ice ages
node tools/ice-check.mjs earthlike 80     # ice grown under the climate and orbit
node tools/survey.mjs '{}' 600 b         # 300 Myr of plates and landscape: continents, land, sea level, erosion, speeds
node tools/climate-check.mjs earthlike   # the climate on a seeded planet: means, zonal profile, zone shares, land seasons
node tools/plates-survey.mjs '{}' 600 b  # plates alone
for f in main.js colors.js src/*.js src/*/*.js; do node --check "$f"; done
```

`window.__isochron` exposes the page's mirrors of the engine (`P, tec, land, climate, ice, carbon, plumes`), the `engine` worker, `wx`, `globe`, `chart`, `camera`, `scheduler`, `setPlaying`, `setView`, `setExperiment`, `tick(now)` (one frame, for driving a hidden tab) and `prof` (each section's share of a frame, ms); `P.years` is the planet's clock. `?exp=snowball` (or `iceages`, `highstand`, `thermostat`) opens an experiment.

WGSL compiles only in the browser, so check the console (and `tests/gpu.html`) after shader edits. Derivatives (`fwidth`) must stay in uniform control flow, which is why the fragment shader computes both sea and land and then selects.

## How the plates work

- **Plate frames.** Each plate keeps its crust in its own frame, on a full copy of the grid (mask, thickness, felsic fraction, formation time, suture time). World cell i reads plate k at Qₖ⁻¹xᵢ. Motion is exact (a full turn restores every cell), and nothing is resampled while a plate stays whole. Merges are the one resampling: one plate's cells are re-expressed in the other's frame.
- **The step.** Each world cell tests the plates that held it or a neighbour last step:
  - none covers it: new oceanic crust for the plate that left;
  - one covers it: that plate;
  - several cover it: the most buoyant stays on top. The others are consumed only if the plates are closing at that cell (`CLOSING`). An overlap where plates slide past each other is grid rounding, and the loser stays hidden. Without that rule, transforms churn crust and leave false slabs that pull along the boundary.
- **Consumption.** Ocean that goes down leaves slab memory at the cell, inherited along a migrating trench and decaying over `SLAB_TAU`, and builds arc crust on the overriding plate. Continent against continent stacks crust, marks a suture, adds collision resistance, and tallies the pair toward a merge.
- **Forces.**
  - Slab pull acts along each plate's edge only: each edge cell pulls with the longest slab of its plate within one cell. Counting the whole memory band made pull grow with speed and run away.
  - Ridge push is the gradient of subsidence within a plate's own ocean.
  - Drag is −c ∫ (1 + KEEL·φ)(I − r rᵀ) dA · ω, regularized by plate area.
  - ω relaxes toward the solved value over `V_RELAX` and is capped at `V_MAX`.
- **Starting subduction.** Old seafloor founders: at a plate's edge, any cell past `FOUNDER_AGE`; at its own continent's margin, the attached ocean breaks away as a new plate (`maybeInitiate`, at most every `INITIATE_GAP` and below `MAX_LIVE_PLATES`).
- **Plate count.** Too few plates, or one too large, splits the largest along the candidate plane that crosses the most sutures. A plate below `MICROPLATE` joins its largest neighbour.
- **Boundary relaxation.** A cell whose neighbours mostly belong to another plate passes its crust to that plate (`relaxBoundaries`, every step). Nearest-cell rounding otherwise roughens boundaries a cell at a time until they are fractal and dotted with one-cell islands. This is a numerical regularization (a curvature flow at one cell's scale): it moves crust and never makes or destroys any.
- **Genesis.** The largest continents each seed a plate at their centre, and ocean seeds fill the rest. Plates grow at their own pace, faster over continental crust, so boundaries meet at sea and continents start as passengers with passive margins.
- **Order.** The step visits the cells in index order, and three of its effects follow that order: a slab inherits a neighbour's slab already grown this step (so along an index-ordered run of trench cells it grows several cells in one step), a consumed frame cell is gone for the cells after it, and a founder keeps its later neighbours from foundering. The first matters: at level 7, inheriting from the step's start instead ran the plates at 2.3 against 2.9 cm/yr over 40 Myr, and level 7's slab scaling (√res) was fitted with it. The GPU's step reproduces all three (`src/gpu/plates.js`). An order-independent reference would be cleaner, but level 7 would need refitting first.

## How the landscape works

`Landscape` (`src/engine/landscape.js`) steps after every plate step, on the same crust.
- **Routing.** Cells below sea level are outlets. A priority flood fills depressions (lakes) with a small rise so flats still drain, and each cell drains to its steepest neighbour on the filled surface. The flood's pop order is a topological order, so accumulation and the solver are single passes.
- **Erosion.** Stream power with n = 1, dz/dt = −K Qᵐ S, solved implicitly from the outlets upstream (Braun and Willett's scheme), so any step is stable. Q is drainage area weighted by the climate's runoff. Rock removed lowers the surface by only (1 − ρ_crust/ρ_mantle) of its thickness, because the crust rebounds, so erosion takes thickness (`f` in the code is that factor).
- **Sediment** goes downstream with the water. Lakes keep what fits; at the coast it builds a shelf to `SHELF` below sea level and then moves to the deepest neighbouring sea. Deposits mix felsic material into the crust, so a thick enough delta turns oceanic crust continental.
- **Diffusion.** Continental thickness diffuses between continental cells, faster under thick crust (hillslopes and small rivers below the grid's scale, and the collapse of orogens). It replaces phase 1's non-conservative relaxation of thick crust: erosion, sediment and diffusion move crust and never make or destroy it (tested).
- **Water.** Sediment fills basins and fast spreading makes young, shallow seafloor, and both raise the sea. Over `WATER_TAU` (150 Myr) the ocean's volume drifts toward the land fraction set at genesis, a stand-in for the water ridges degas and trenches subduct. Earth's freeboard has held for billions of years by a mechanism still debated. High stands from fast spreading still happen on shorter timescales (seed `c` drowns to 18% land and recovers).

## How the climate works

`Climate` (`src/engine/climate.js`) is a diagnostic of the geography, recomputed in cycles: the energy balance, then winds and rain at six points of the year, then the zones. `work(ms)` advances a cycle for a time budget (a generator yields between stages), so the engine's worker spends the time between ticks on it and the climate relaxes toward the current geography; a cycle takes ~300 ms warm, ~700 ms cold. `solve(n)` runs whole cycles headless.
- **Energy balance** (standard), on level 5 (220 km cells) whatever the engine's level (at 110 km the cold cycle's three solves cost ~2 s of a level-7 opening, and the temperature is smooth at that scale), with `transfer.js` restricting forcing down and prolonging temperature up (through the levels between): C ∂T/∂t = S(1 − α) − (A + BT) + 5.35 ln(CO₂/CO₂₀) + ∇·D∇T, solved as the annual mean and two harmonics, each by complex-symmetric conjugate gradients (COCG), warm-started. T is the sea-level temperature; the surface is colder by 6.5 K/km. Albedo depends on the temperature through the year (snow below +1 °C on land, sea ice below −1.5 °C), so absorbed sunlight is re-sampled at 24 points of the year from the last solution: a fixed point, which keeps the ice–albedo feedback and its hysteresis. The seasonal harmonics use a smaller diffusivity (`D_SEASON`) than the mean (`D`): with one diffusivity, continents smaller than ~5,000 km get the ocean's seasons, a known weakness of diffusive EBMs.
- **Circulation** (synthesis): a template. The ITCZ is the warmest latitude in each 10° sector of longitude (penalized away from the equator), the Hadley edge scales as √ΔT/Ω (Held and Hou), and surface pressure is a sum of the ITCZ's trough, the subtropical highs, the subpolar lows and the polar highs, plus −0.75 hPa per kelvin of each place's departure from its latitude's mean (smoothed): thermal lows over hot land, highs over cold land. The wind solves r u + f k×u = −∇p/ρ.
- **Moisture** (synthesis): column water W, steady, ∇·(uW) − ∇·(κ∇W) = E − P, upwinded, each cell's nonlinear balance by Newton: in the page by 160 Jacobi passes on the GPU (`src/gpu/moisture.js`, warm-started), headless by Gauss–Seidel in four sweep orders (`SWEEPS`, 6 warm, which stops ~4% of the rain short of the converged balance the GPU reaches; 60 sweeps match it to 0.1%). The carrying wind takes the geostrophic part and 40% of the frictional drift (the drift is shallower than the moist layer). Evaporation pulls W toward saturation (Clausius–Clapeyron), weaker over land, by wetness. Rain is (W_s/60) e^{15.6(r − 0.603)} mm/day at column humidity r (the shape of Bretherton and others' fit; constants from memory, unchecked), plus lifting up slopes, plus a light background suppressed where the air sinks. Rain shadows come from air that has already rained out.
- **Zones** (synthesis): monthly temperature and rain from the harmonics; Köppen's thresholds (Peel and others' version, from memory) as logistics (1.5 K, and 0.25 in log rain), giving a probability over 14 classes. Runoff (rain less evaporation, Budyko's curve) goes back to the landscape.
- **Feedbacks:** zones set land albedo (deserts brighter, forests darker); runoff sets erosion.
- **Routing roughness:** water routes over the surface plus ±25 m of roughness fixed to each plate's own cells (`LAND.ROUGH`), so flats drain in branching networks rather than straight parallel lines; erosion still uses the true surface, and a cell the roughness routes uphill doesn't cut.

## How ice and carbon work

`Planet` (`src/engine/planet.js`) steps the layers together: plates, plumes and the landscape per half-Myr plate step; carbon and ice every frame; the climate's cycles in the background, each completed one refreshing weathering's map (`carbon.measure`) and, through the climate's version, the ice's mass balance.
- **The orbit** (`OrbitCycles`): eccentricity near 405, 124 and 95 kyr, obliquity near 41 and 54 kyr, precession near 22 kyr, seeded around Earth's values (from memory). The climate sees each term scaled by sinc(W/P) for a window W of what the dial passes in ~2 s, so at plate speed it sees the mean orbit and at kyr/s the cycles whole. The insolation table is rebuilt when the orbit changes.
- **Ice** (`Ice`, stepped on the GPU in the page by `src/gpu/ice.js`: four dispatches a step, the step size from an atomic maximum, never read back mid-run; 48 steps a tick at most; the state read back every ~100 ms): thickness on the surface grid, in the mantle's frame. The flux is the shallow-ice approximation's, −Γ H⁵ |∇s|² ∇s, explicit with the step its stability allows (~25 yr at 110 km, ~6 at 55 km); `advance(years, ms)` steps within a real-time budget and drops the rest, so at plate speed the ice lags the climate rather than the clock (its closure). The mass balance is snow below freezing at the ice's surface (the lapse rate makes a high sheet keep itself cold) less melt by positive degree days. The bed relaxes toward −(ρᵢ/ρₘ) H over 3 kyr (`tec.load`), ice afloat calves, and the ice's water is taken from the sea (`tec.iceWater`); the sea is re-solved when that has moved ~10 cm. The climate counts grounded ice as land, its surface as the height, and its albedo as snow's.
- **Organic carbon:** burial scales with the sediment the rivers deliver to the sea, oxidation of old organic carbon with the rock they erode, each a fifth of the reference outgassing at the opening (`CARBON.ORG`).
- **Carbon** (`Carbon`): an exogenic reservoir R (atmosphere and ocean), pCO₂ ∝ R². Outgassing scales with crust opened at ridges and ocean closed at trenches, measured from the plates' motion across each boundary (`tec.kinematics`: counting gap cells also counted the grid's rounding, a few cells per boundary per step, so the rate grew as steps shrank), against the spin-up's last 10 Myr, plus provinces' pulses. Its weathering reference is taken at the climate's third cycle (the first is cold). Weathering is the climate's map (runoff^0.65 × e^{(T − 15)/Tₑ} × fresh-rock supply, summed over ice-free land), times the response to CO₂ since the climate last solved (the sensitivity through Tₑ, and pCO₂^0.3 directly). Implicit in R, substeps of 20 kyr; `equilibrium(V)` is the analytic balance (tested).
- **Plumes** (`Plumes`): ten hotspots fixed in the mantle build crust on whatever passes (seamount chains) and lift swells (`tec.swell`). Plume heads arrive as a Poisson process (every ~30 Myr): a flood of basalt over a disc ~1,100 km across within 1 Myr, 8,000 Pg of carbon, a new hotspot, and, under a large continent, a rift through the plume (`tec.rift(k, point)`).

## How the ocean and the weather work

- **Gyres** (`Ocean`, on the climate's coarse grid: the stress restricted, the SST anomaly from the energy balance's coarse mean, ψ and the anomaly published back on the surface's grid in `fine`; ~0.1 s a solve at 220 km, 1.1 s at 110 km): the depth-integrated flow under the annual-mean wind stress, steady, Stommel's form on the unit sphere, ε∇²ψ + ∂ψ/∂λ = a² curl τ/(2Ωρ₀), coasts at ψ = 0 (islands too), ε set for a two-cell western boundary layer. BiCGSTAB with a Jacobi preconditioner, warm-started, 120 iterations a cycle and the gyres only every third climate cycle (the basins change slowly). Surface currents u = k × ∇ψ/H (H = 400 m). The SST anomaly T′ solves upwinded u·∇(T + T′) + λT′ = κ∇²T′ + upwelling, where upwelling cools coasts the Ekman drift leaves. Moisture uses the SST (T + T′) for the sea's saturation, and sea ice forms on it (in the energy balance's albedo, its share of the year under ice, and the season drawn), so currents shape the ice edge: with the gyres at ~12 Sv the anomaly runs −6 to +1.7 K and the edge's spread in latitude went ±1.4° → ±2.0°; it stays near a latitude circle, as diffusive energy balances' ice edges do. Tested against Sverdrup's interior and the western boundary current in a box basin.
- **Weather** (`GpuWeather`, `src/gpu/weather.js`): two-layer QG on the GPU, on the drawn mesh (level 7, 55 km), its forcing refined from the engine's grid, L_d = 800 km. PV is carried by fluxes that are differences of ψ at Voronoi corners (exactly non-divergent), 20% upwinded, with ∇⁴ hyperviscosity scaled with the cell's 3.2 power (with the fourth power, the finer grid's noise outgrew it). Adams–Bashforth 2 at 200 s (800 s at level 5, halving with the cell: the advective limit with the upper layer's ~115 m/s jets; 300 s blew up on day 1). The upper layer starts with half the climate's shear and the forcing builds the rest: starting with all of it, storms grew explosively and the transient's jets (~110 m/s on day 3) sat at the step's limit and sometimes passed it; now winds climb to ~50–65 m/s with no spike. The lower layer's state is q₂ less its orography, whose PV is carried alongside, so the ground can change under the weather. Inversion each step: the barotropic Poisson and baroclinic Helmholtz solves at once (a vec2) by two V-cycles of multigrid on levels 7 down to 2 (`src/gpu/hierarchy.js`: weighted Jacobi, two sweeps each way; restriction gathers a coarse cell's six midpoints; prolongation averages a midpoint's two parents; level 2 solved exactly by a precomputed inverse), warm-started. Each cycle cuts the error tenfold. The barotropic solve screens at 1/k₀ ≈ 32,000 km, which fixes ψ's constant (a ~2% touch on the largest waves). Forcing: the baroclinic ψ relaxes over 15 days toward SHEAR · (f/f₀) · (T − T̄) from the climate's temperature at the season; Ekman friction on the lower layer; damping in the tropics in flux form, so PV's integral is conserved (to 1e-8 over 10 days). Tracers on the lower layer: θ relaxed to the climate's over 5 days, and moisture from the sea, raining past 90% of saturation. 66 dispatches and ~0.55 ms of GPU a step on an M5 Max (timestamps), ~240 ms a model day; the page gives it 12 ms of each frame, so it runs live to ~3 days a second. A kernel writes the globe's weather fields (eddy pressure over the template, its zonal mean interpolated between 3° bands, rain, temperature, humidity, blended with the climate's mean) into the mesh's vertex buffer each frame, from the last two steps blended by how far the clock is into the next (ψ before the last step is kept for it), so at a slow dial the steps don't land a frame at a time. Tested on the device (`tests/gpu.html`): storms grow above Phillips' criterion and die below; PV is conserved; multigrid converges.
- **The chart** (`chart.js`, traced in `chart-worker.js` from the weather read back every ~120 ms and restricted to the engine's grid: tracing on the 55 km mesh cost ~650 ms a chart against ~100): sea-level pressure is the climate's template at the season plus the storms (ρfψ₂ less its zonal mean), smoothed, drawn as isobars at whole hPa (every 4, heavier every 20); rain as stipple. Counts on the grid's lines (smoothing passes, chain lengths, the rings of a high or low) scale with the level, so the chart reads alike at every resolution. Highs and lows are extremes of the pressure eased to 220 km over ~440 km, at least 1 hPa from their surroundings. Ends that nearly meet, meet (`joinEnds`): each end of a front, dry line or shear line moves onto the nearest end of another within ~320 km, or onto the nearest point of one within ~190 km, the last points tapering, so fronts' pieces, occlusions and their remnants, and dry and shear lines running into fronts join with a kink. On the page each chart is linked to the last (`linkCharts`: lines of a kind by nearest middle, centres within ~400 km) and drawn morphing from it over the time between charts (`chartFrame`), new lines fading in and old ones out. Fronts are the zero of ∇TFP·n̂ on θ where |∇θ| and TFP are in their upper ranges and the low-level flow is cyclonic (fronts lie in troughs), traced on triangles, chained, smoothed, typed by v·n̂ against 0.45 of the chart's median |v·n̂| (low-level flow runs nearly along the isotherms here, so an absolute threshold called every front stationary), then held in runs (a majority over ±8 points, runs under 10 points absorbed) with their symbols on one side per run. The locator's zero must be a maximum of TFP along n̂ (a minimum drew a second front beside each). Fronts are then split into stretches of one type; a stretch lying mostly within ~290 km of a longer one of its type is the same front found twice and goes; a cold stretch within ~380 km of a warm one has caught it, and the matched parts merge along their midline into an occluded front (mulberry, triangles and semicircles alternating on one side). Troughs (umber, dashed), ridges (umber zigzag) and subtropical shear lines (prussian, dash-dot) are ridge lines of −p, p and cyclonic vorticity, found by the Hessian's eigenvector (aligned per triangle), long and not round; troughs a front already marks aren't drawn. The fields (θ, moisture, wind, vorticity) and thresholds are averaged over successive charts, and pieces whose ends meet and line up are bridged, so fronts move rather than flicker. Dry lines are the same on the relative humidity the Humidity chart draws (the column's water over wsat(θ)), over land, from the top quarter of its gradients, where θ's gradient is under 1.25× the fronts' threshold, pieces bridged and thresholds averaged as fronts' are (on the column's water, the top 15% and no bridging, the chart found 0–1). The weather runs only in its view, waits for a warm climate, spins up ~12 days, and crossfades to the climate's mean between ~3 and ~30 days a second.

## How the tides and the Moon work

- **The tide** (`Tides`, `src/engine/tides.js`; `GpuTides` in the page): linear shallow water on the rotating sphere on a C grid over the Voronoi cells (height on cells, the velocity normal to each edge between two sea cells, coasts closed), forced by the semidiurnal equilibrium tide of a Moon over the equator (cos²φ e^{2iλ}, ¾(m/M)(R/a)³R = 0.27 m today), with the body tide (α = 1 + k₂ − h₂ ≈ 0.69) and self-attraction and loading as β ≈ 0.9 on the sea's height (standard values, from memory). Coriolis acts through the cells' velocities rebuilt from their edges (Perot's reconstruction), with Heun's step for the rotation, forward–backward for the waves, drag implicit. Drag: bottom friction C_D·U₀/H (0.0025 m/s) and internal-tide drag ½κh²N/H (3e-8 against the depth's step² between cells, capped at a quarter of the depth: a continental slope's whole drop as roughness walled the shelves off and spun up ~150 spurious amphidromes). Run to periodic (32 cycles cold, 5 warm: a basin mode rings for days) and the harmonic read over the last cycle; dissipation is the drag's work, 2ρrH⟨u²⟩ per edge's diamond. Tested: on a still global ocean the forced degree-2 response is analytic (within 1%); mass is kept to 1e-16; the GPU matches the CPU to 0.01% (82 ms against 1.8 s at level 5; 0.47 s cold and 70 ms warm at level 6).
- **At level 6 always** (the engine's level-6 cells, which nest first in a level-7 grid): shelves resolve at 110 km (at level 5 the lunar semidiurnal tide dissipated 1.5 TW, at level 6 4.2), and the Moon then recedes alike at every engine level. On the opening planet: median amplitude 0.27 m, a resonant gulf to ~7 m, ~20 deep-water amphidromes, 4.2 TW in the lunar semidiurnal tide (Earth's ~2.5; 60% on shelves, as Earth's), ~6 TW in all, so this planet's Moon recedes ~6 cm/yr and its day lengthens ~3.7 ms a century (Earth's 3.8 cm/yr and ~2.1 ms, from memory). Its ocean is deeper than Earth's (median 5.6 km), which the solver takes as given; the constants are Earth's, not fitted to Earth's total.
- **The Moon** (`Moon`): each plate step, the lunar tides' torque P/(Ω − n) slows the spin (C dΩ/dt) and recedes the Moon (L = μ√(GMa)); the solar tide slows the spin too. The lunar tides' dissipation is the semidiurnal's × 1.25 (the diurnal tides; from memory), the solar's (0.46)² of it. Dissipation scales with (R/a)⁶ between solves; the worker re-solves every ~2 Myr (the coasts move) or when 2(Ω − n) has moved 0.3% (the basins' resonances move with it). With Earth's dissipation (3.7 TW) the Moon recedes 3.8 cm/yr and the day lengthens 2.1 ms a century (tested). `CLIMATE.OMEGA` follows the spin: the Hadley edge, the template's winds, the gyres' β and the weather (which takes Ω when built, with L_d ∝ 1/Ω, and is rebuilt when the Weather view opens if the spin has moved 0.3%). Headless (no GPU), the Moon stays put.
- **The Tides view** (`globe.js`, mode 5): the sea's tint by the semidiurnal amplitude (co-range), co-tidal lines every lunar hour drawn as the zeros of sin 6G from the harmonic's complex sixth power (no seam where the phase wraps), fading into each amphidrome, and a sienna stipple, its density the dissipation per area. Below ~6 h/s the tint is the sea's height now (the lunar and solar semidiurnal tides, so springs and neaps beat over 14.8 days) and a heavier line marks high water. The tide's clock is the sky's: its phase is 2(θ − the Moon's right ascension), so the bulge sits under the Moon. The record figure carries the day's length; Fig. 7 a Tides node (the basins in, the spin out to the climate, the ocean and the weather).
- **The sky** (`sky.js`, on the overlay outside the globe's disc): a seeded star field fixed in space, drawn through a perspective whose scale zoom doesn't change (stars at infinity), turning about the spin axis by the sidereal angle θ where a day takes a few seconds or more (below ~6 h/s), held still above; the Sun on the ecliptic at its longitude for the date (the orbit's calendar, at the orbit's obliquity), shown below ~0.25 yr/s; the Moon at its longitude (advancing at its mean motion) shown below ~5 days/s, lit toward the Sun (an almanac's ○ full and ● new), both at their true sizes ×5 so a nearer Moon looks larger. Ink on the paper; the dark theme's paper is the night. Sun and Moon don't light the globe (its hillshade keeps the cartographic light).

## Threads and the GPU

- **The engine's worker** (`src/engine/worker.js`) owns the `Planet` (level 7; level 6 on phones and tablets), and its own GPU device for the ice, the moisture, the tide, the plates' step and the surface's fields. The page keeps the clock: each frame it plays, it posts a tick (the years, the plate steps its scheduler planned, the orbit's window); the worker runs them all. Plate steps are planned no shorter than the engine can keep up with (it reports its cost per step, ~70–100 ms at level 7 with the landscape's, drawing's and the climate's shares), and the clock holds while two steps are outstanding, so the top of the dial runs at what the engine manages (at level 7 ~5.6 Myr/s in node, against ~4.0 with the plates on the CPU; at level 6 the whole dial). Every experiment runs at 1 Myr/s or slower. The climate runs alongside as an async loop, a 10 ms slice of its cycle at a time, awaiting the GPU where the cycle yields a promise (`solveAsync` is the headless form). It posts `surface` after steps or when the sea moves (the drawn fields from `surface.js`, the boundaries and rivers traced, the plates' rotations, the crust's statistics), `climate` after each cycle (harmonics, zones, ocean), `tides` after each solve of the tide (the harmonic and the dissipation per unit equilibrium tide, prolonged to the engine's grid), and `fast` ~10 times a second (with the Moon's distance, the spin and the dissipation) (ice, carbon, plumes, the record's samples, the model figure's sums). The page's mirrors (`mirror.js`, and plain objects in `main.js`) have the engine's shapes, so drawing and figures read them unchanged. Experiments' parameters go over as a `params` message; `CARBON.FIXED: 'now'` holds CO₂ where it stands.
- **The shown moment.** Surfaces queue on the page as they arrive (irregularly: a landscape step makes some slower), and the globe shows a moment τ that follows them a step and a half behind at the engine's pace (measured over the last 3 s of arrivals), nudged ±50% by how far it is from that aim and eased over 0.3 s; it eases into the newest when nothing waits. Between the two surfaces either side of τ the globe blends (`setFields` modes: step, reset, refresh), boundaries turn with their plates to τ and morph, and rivers morph over the landscape's step.
- **The plates on the GPU** (`src/gpu/plates.js`, from `Tectonics.stepAsync` through `Planet.plateStepAsync`, which runs the plumes first so their crust and swells go up with the step). The CPU's plate frames stay authoritative: the landscape and the plumes write them through (owner, ref), and merges, rifts and initiation rewrite whole plates on the CPU. The GPU keeps a mirror of every frame (crust as h, φ, formation and suture time per frame cell; masks as a bit per plate per frame cell) and the world's memories (slabs, arcs, collision fronts). Each step sends up the top cells' h and φ as the CPU's frames hold them, the rotations, the ice's load and the swells. 36 dispatches follow at level 7: two decide passes, the slabs' chain, writes, relaxation, sampling, decay, elevation, the kinematics, the sea level (one workgroup's Newton), foundering and the per-plate sums. One read back brings down owner and ref, the crust on top, elevation, the sea level and land, the sums (ω is solved on the CPU in f64), counters, collision tallies, and every frame cell the step changed, as edits applied to the CPU's frames. The memories come down only before a merge or an initiation; afterwards the touched plates, owners and memories go back up. A frame cell two world cells reach is written once, by the lowest index among neighbours, so the step is deterministic. One step from one state agrees with the CPU's at 99.99% of cells; over 40–60 Myr the GPU's statistics differ from the CPU's no more than a CPU nudged by 3e-3 in ω (tests/gpu-plates.test.mjs). The pump awaits the step, one pump at a time; while pumps run back to back the climate takes a 10 ms slice every 100 ms (it may fill a pump's wait), and between pumps it runs freely. The surface's fields are prepared on the device too (`src/gpu/surface.js`, from the engine's arrays uploaded each time, so it doesn't matter which side last stepped them).
- **The GPU** (one device, `src/gpu/device.js`): the globe draws; the weather steps and writes the globe's weather buffer; the season writes its climate buffer. Kernels declare only the bindings their entry uses (`kernel()` binds by name under the 'auto' layout), at most eight storage buffers each.
- **The chart's worker** (`src/render/chart-worker.js`) gets ψ₂, θ and moisture read back from the GPU with the template's pressure and derives wind, vorticity and the eddy pressure before tracing; one read and one trace are in flight at a time.

## The experiments

Each starts from the planet's opening (choosing one reloads with `?exp=`), sets the dial and the view, and has one knob; the caption carries its measured number.
- **Earth-like**: the opening.
- **Snowball**: sunlight (82–106%), CO₂ held at its value when chosen; the ice line. At 88% the planet freezes to the equator, and turning the sun back up doesn't thaw it at once (the ice–albedo hysteresis).
- **Ice ages**: CO₂ held (150–450 ppm), 10 kyr/s; the ice's volume and its dominant period (periodogram of the record over 400 kyr).
- **High stand**: mantle heat (scales slab pull, ridge push and the speed cap); sea level and land.
- **Thermostat**: outgassing (0.25–4×); CO₂ against its analytic balance.

## Drawing the climate

- **Relief view.** Land takes the vegetation of its climate: the zones' mixture blended as natural colours (`biome` in the shader: forest, savanna and steppe, desert, boreal forest, tundra), with rock toward the summits; umber height alone until the climate has run. Snow and sea ice are a light fill with a hard edge (the contour of the snow-and-ice share at ½, antialiased by its derivative) and a hairline, ice sheets the same with a stronger outline and contours of the ice's surface every 500 m. Lakes are water where their inflow beats ~1 m/yr of evaporation, drawn from a smoothed, signed depth (dry land below zero) with a hard shore and its hairline, from two cells up (one cell would draw as a hexagon). Rivers above 1,500 m³/s and three cells long are drawn in the sea's ink, their weight the discharge: main stems run continuously (at each confluence the larger river runs on; rivers big in their wettest month alone are traced too), relaxed toward their neighbours (four passes at level 6, eight at level 7; corner-cutting alone kept the grid's straight runs between corners) and corner-cut three times, with each tributary's end moved onto its stem's smoothed course; a river ends where the drawn relief crosses sea level along its course, so on the coast the globe draws (halfway to the sea's cell, 2% of river points lay over drawn sea; now 0.4%); they break at lakes. Where the globe shows the season (below ~12 yr/s) each river's weight is its discharge that month: the climate's monthly runoff (`monthlyRunoff`: snow held below 0 °C and melted at 3 mm/°C/day, evaporation through the year by temperature, baseflow a third of the runoff where much rain runs off and little in deserts; the months sum to the year's Budyko runoff) routed down the drainage (`Landscape.monthly`, no travel time). On the opening planet 188 of 193 rivers swing over 3× in a year (56 over 10×), high-latitude rivers peak with the melt (May–July in the north), the largest swings 42,000–204,000 m³/s; a river under a tenth of its mean (and 450 m³/s) that month is a dotted bed, an intermittent stream as maps draw it. The deep sea's relief is eased twice more than the coast's, so isobaths are curves. The young-crust tint at ridge axes is gone: the smoothed ridge rule draws the axis, and the tint drew the grid's cells.
- **Motion between steps.** Plate steps are a tenth of a second's worth of the dial (50 kyr to 0.5 Myr; the landscape steps every 0.25 Myr). The globe's fields are kept for the last two steps (`setFields(…, fromStep)`), and the vertex shader blends them (`blend`) over the time from one step's arrival from the engine to the next's (the dial's time for the step, or the gap between arrivals if the engine is slower), so the surface moves continuously (each step moves the crust less than half a cell). Boundaries turn with the mean of their two plates' rotations, and rivers with their plate's, to the same displayed moment (`turned` in `overlay.js`), and each new line morphs from its predecessor's course (`matchLines` pairs them by plate pair or plate and nearest middle, rivers strictly: both ends near and lengths within 40%; each new point corresponds to its nearest point on the old line, so points move across a line, not along it; `morphed` blends by the step's φ, rivers by the landscape's). Symbols are pinned to the line (`along.js`): each point carries u, a coordinate along the line in radians, which a retrace inherits from its predecessor at the corresponding point (smoothed over ~±250 km, a tenth of each retrace's stretch let go, points past the old ends carried on at arc length), and symbols sit at fixed values of u, so as a line moves they move across it with it and its ends advance or retreat over symbols that stay put (counted from the line's start, 16% of trench teeth and 42% of the chart's symbols jumped more than a fifth of a gap along their line per retrace; pinned, under 1%). The morph runs u from the inherited value to the relaxed one, so nothing jumps when a retrace arrives. The marks are a lattice in u: at the usual zoom every 16th node shows, at the symbol's gap on screen; where a line is foreshortened half of them shrink away an octave at a time, and zoomed in past the symbols' scale the next octave grows in. Dashes, dots and the ridge's zigzag follow u too. Rivers with no predecessor fade in and those with no successor fade out. Boundaries take six Laplacian passes, so a re-traced boundary barely shifts. Which side stays on top reads in runs (a running mean over ±3 corners, then runs shorter than ~4 cells take their neighbours' side), and each retrace keeps half its predecessor's value at the corresponding point, so a trench's teeth don't switch sides. On the weather chart every line drifts whole with its mean wind (turned about r × v̄, so it keeps its shape) and every centre with its own, for up to three model hours between charts (every 150 ms), and each new chart corrects it. Chart lines are relaxed (Laplacian, ends held) before corner-cutting.
- **Snow and ice** are drawn from the snow-and-ice share and the ice's thickness smoothed twice over neighbours (edges are curves; one-cell patches melt away), eased over ~0.8 s when the climate updates (`src/gpu/season.js`, each frame on the GPU); lakes under snow are frozen over.
- **Collision** (a convergent boundary where no ocean sinks) has open sawteeth, as a thrust belt, and no arc.
- **Symbols scale with the globe**: trench teeth, arcs, transform arrows, hotspots, rivers' weight, fronts and dry lines, by the globe's radius on screen against 380 px, within ×0.75–3.5. Hotspots are red ringed points and an erupting province a red stippled disc.
- **Patterns** (the zones' screens, rain stipple) are fixed to the globe and scale with the zoom: drawn in three planar projections of the sphere blended sharply by the normal (triplanar), at a frequency from the globe's radius in pixels, with the next octave's marks fading in between the last's (`octave`, `hatch`, `stipple`), and marks of constant width on screen.
- **The key** (`src/render/key.js`) at the globe's lower left draws each view's symbols as the scene does; `K` or the Key button toggles it (open on wide screens).
- **Weather view.** Three charts (`wxMode`): pressure (isobars, rain), temperature (a prussian-to-sienna wash, isotherms every 5 °C, 0 °C heavier) and humidity (ochre-to-verdigris wash for the column's relative humidity, a line every 20%, rain); the picker under the views appears in Weather. Pale land, the paper for sea; cold fronts are prussian with triangles toward the warm air, warm fronts sienna with semicircles toward the cold, stationary fronts alternate, dry lines are ochre with open scallops toward the moist air, and H and L mark the highs and lows.
- **Climate view.** Land is a mosaic of tesserae fixed to the globe (a jittered 3D lattice, snapped to octaves of zoom). Each takes one class with probability ∝ p², so ecotones interleave and the logistic tails don't speckle. Each class is its group's ink in its second letter's screen-space pattern (`zoneColor` in the shader and `zonePattern` in `figures.js` must match). A, C and D also step in value, so the groups part in grayscale. The sea is a plain wash, and the tectonic symbols are hidden.
- **The season.** Below ~4 yr/s the globe shows snow and ice at the planet's clock (`years`; the plates still step in banked half-Myr); above ~40 yr/s, the share of the year under them; between, a blend. The caption names the month below 300 yr/s.

## Drawing the boundaries

`traceBoundaries` (`src/render/lines.js`) walks the Voronoi edges between plates into polylines that run between triple junctions; each polyline separates one pair of plates. It smooths each line on the sphere (three Laplacian passes, then two rounds of Chaikin corner-cutting, junctions fixed). Every point is classified from the two plates' rotations there: the relative velocity across the line against the velocity along it, averaged along the line, so a boundary reads in long runs. Each point also keeps which side stays on top and whether the sinking side is ocean. The overlay places symbols along each line (pinned to it, `along.js`; spaced evenly on screen at the usual zoom), following geologic-map convention:
- trenches: a rule with sawteeth on the overriding plate, and red arc marks on that side where ocean sinks;
- ridges: a red rule, with the globe tinting crust younger than 1 Myr;
- transforms: a lighter rule with paired half-arrows for the sense of slip;
- inactive boundaries: a dotted rule.

Lines are retraced after each plate step (~6 ms) and reprojected each frame.

## Open work

- **Tuning.** With the landscape and the water buffer, land holds 24–29% on the default seed and seed `b` over 300 Myr; seed `c` has a high stand to 18%. `PARAMS` still need a survey against Earth-like targets: seed `c` keeps one plate at `V_MAX` for long stretches, and mean ocean depth is 5.3 km (Earth's is 3.7) because isostasy here ignores the water's load and the lithosphere's roots. The climate is tuned on the default seed (`tools/climate-check.mjs`): mean 13–14 °C, ~1,000 mm/yr of rain, zone shares near Earth's but short of D (continental) climates.
- **Speed.** The page's thread spends ~2 ms a frame (overlay and line morphs; line matching searches near the last match). At level 7 (node, M5 Max) the engine spends ~5.5 ms a plate step on the GPU (~1.2 up, ~3.4 waiting, of which the sea level's one-workgroup Newton is ~1.2, ~0.5 down; 18 on the CPU), ~38 on the landscape each 0.25 Myr, ~1.4 preparing the surface on the GPU (11 on the CPU) and ~10 tracing lines; boot ~1.7 s (node: the spin-up at level 6 ~1.05 s, the level-7 grid 0.14, refinement 0.21, the climate's setup 0.14); a climate cycle ~1.0 s cold and ~480 ms warm (energy balance 460/135, the phases' template and moisture 340/180, zones 110). A level-6 plate step costs ~2.1 ms on the GPU (5.6 on the CPU), the surface 0.7 (2.6), a landscape step ~8 (sea level by guarded Newton from the last level; crustal diffusion over a prebuilt edge list); boot ~1.4 s, a cold cycle ~550 ms. At the dial's top level 7 now spends most of a step on the landscape and the lines. Next: the landscape (10b), the lines' and rivers' tracing, the sea level across workgroups, then the energy balance (multigrid, complex), the template and zones (10c).
- **Drawing.** The globe draws a level-7 mesh (163,842 vertices); a level-6 engine's fields are refined onto it by one step of Loop subdivision on the GPU (`src/gpu/refine.js`: a midpoint takes 3/8 of each end and 1/8 of the two vertices across; an old vertex keeps 10/16 of itself at valence six), so coasts, isobaths, lakes, ice edges and zones' borders are curves at close zoom. At level 7 the mesh is the engine's grid and nothing is refined.
- **Level 7's tuning.** The forces are discretized on the cells, and the same planet on a finer grid pushed and pulled harder: refined from level 6, level 7's plates settled ~30% faster (rms 4.4 against 3.4 cm/yr, activity 1.5×, the sea +127 m and land 23% by 30 Myr), and level 5's ~40% slower. Each is a stable equilibrium, not a runaway: kicked ×0.7 or ×1.3, a level returns to its own speed within ~6 Myr. Two sources, measured on one state read at both levels: ridge push sums a one-sided gradient at the plate's edge, which ends at the edge cell's own depth, nearer the axis on a finer grid (level 7's ~12% stronger); slab pull counts edge cells beside slab memory one cell wide, whose band and staircase change with the cell, and it is the lever (scaling ridge push alone barely moved level 7). PARAMS are tuned at level 6, so `balance` scales the two with the cell, fitted: K_RIDGE × res^0.2 and K_SLAB × √res. Over 60 Myr level 7 then holds rms 2.8–3.2 cm/yr, activity 0.90–1.13×, the sea +9 to +50 m and land 27–28%, against level 6's 2.9–3.5, 0.84–1.01×, +30 to +53 m and 28–29%; coupled over 20 Myr, CO₂ settles at ~375 ppm and 13.6–14.5 °C (level 6: ~390, 14.0–14.6). Making the ridge's edge exact instead (the depth mirrored to the axis at age zero) made levels agree on one state (3% apart) but shifted push onto the ridges, and level 6 itself then drifted (1.2×, +112 m in 30 Myr), so it's out. Level 5 (`?level=5`, spun up at level 5) settles at rms 2.3–3.2 but 0.5–0.7× its own spin-up's rate: unsurveyed. Seed `earthlike` only. In the dark theme the land takes more ink (`palette().tint`) so it stays lighter than the sea.
